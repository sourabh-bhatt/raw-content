# Public and Private Controls on Property Usage

## Public Controls

Public controls, primarily through **zoning laws**, dictate what types of buildings and activities are allowed in certain areas. These zones might be designated for **residential**, **commercial**, **industrial**, or **agricultural** use, among others. Zoning laws ensure that areas are used in ways that align with community planning and safety standards.

## Private Controls

On the other hand, private controls, such as **deed restrictions**, are set by previous owners or community associations. These restrictions might limit house paint colors, fence types, or even dog breeds.

## Common Property Uses

 The commonly available property uses include: 

- **Residential:** Properties designed for people to live in, such as houses, apartments, and condominiums.
- **Commercial:** Spaces used for business activities, like shops, offices, and restaurants.
- **Industrial:** Areas meant for manufacturing, warehousing, and distribution of goods.
- **Agricultural:** Land used for farming or ranching.
- **Recreational:** Properties intended for leisure and entertainment purposes, such as parks, sports complexes, and vacation homes.
- **Specific Purpose:** This category includes buildings and lands earmarked for specialized uses like churches, hospitals, colleges, and cemeteries.
- **Public:** These are lands owned by government entities - state, federal, or municipal - and might include schools, libraries, and public parks.

These concepts provide the foundation to assess the potential and limitations of real estate, enabling informed decisions and sound advice to clients.
