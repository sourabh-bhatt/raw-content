# Understanding Fixtures in Property Disputes

Courts use a **three-part test** to determine whether an item is considered a fixture. Let's break down these key factors:

## 1. Method of Attachment

- This refers to how permanently an object is fastened to the property.
- Items that are built-in or permanently attached are usually considered fixtures.
- **Rule of thumb:** The more permanent the attachment, the less likely the item can be removed.

## 2. Adaptation

- If an item is specially designed or modified for a specific location in the building (e.g., a custom bookcase), it is likely a fixture.
- This factor focuses on how well the item fits into its surroundings.

## 3. Intention of the Parties

- Courts look at what the person who attached the item intended when they installed it.
- Did they intend for the item to become a permanent part of the property?
- The person's intention at the time of attachment is what matters most.
- **Remember:** The annexor's original intent determines whether an item becomes a fixture.
