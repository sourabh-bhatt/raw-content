# Special Types of Personal Property

## Definition of Personal Property

**Personal property** refers to movable items that are not permanently attached to land or buildings. Examples include:

- Aren't permanently attached to land or buildings
- Can be moved from one place to another

## Exceptions to the Rule

However, there are exceptions to this general rule. Some property, even when **attached to land**, can still be considered personal property. These items are called **fixtures** when they become permanently attached to real property.

### Example

Imagine you decide to attach a piece of equipment to your land for a temporary business project. Although it's physically connected to the land, its intended use and temporary nature might allow it to remain classified as **personal property**. This is often called a **trade fixture** in real estate.

### Trade Fixture Examples (Special Types of Personal Property)

Trade fixtures are a **special type of personal property** because they are items a tenant adds for business purposes, even if they are attached to the building.

- **Example 1:** A tenant rents space for a car repair shop and bolts a hydraulic car lift to the floor.

  - The lift is considered a trade fixture.
  - It remains the tenant’s personal property as long as it is removed before the lease ends, since it is essential to running the business.

- **Example 2:** A tenant replaces something that is already part of the property—such as installing a new air conditioner in the repair shop or putting in a new bar in a tavern.
  - These are **not** treated as trade fixtures.
  - They usually become the landlord’s property, unless both sides agree otherwise.

## Importance of Understanding These Distinctions

Recognizing these distinctions is crucial because they can impact how property is treated legally and financially. Understanding these nuances helps to:

- Navigate property rights more effectively
- Make informed decisions regarding assets
- Understand what stays with the property when it's sold
