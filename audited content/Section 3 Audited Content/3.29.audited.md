# Tenancy by the Entirety: Joint Ownership for Married Couples

**Tenancy by the Entirety** represents a special form of joint ownership reserved exclusively for **married couples**. This concept stems from an old common law idea that views a married couple as a single legal entity. Not all states recognize this type of ownership; in fact, it's recognized in less than one-third of states. **Virginia does recognize tenancy by the entirety**.

## What Makes Tenancy by the Entirety Special?

- It is **resilient** and lasts as long as both spouses are alive and remain married.
- Both spouses own **100% of the property together**, not just half each.
- Neither spouse can sell or transfer their "share" of the property independently, unlike in a joint tenancy.

## How Can Tenancy by the Entirety End?

A Tenancy by the Entirety can be dissolved through:

1. Mutual agreement between spouses to end it.
2. Divorce.
3. Both spouses deciding to sell the property together.
4. Death of one spouse.

## Benefits of Tenancy by the Entirety

- **Right of Survivorship:** If one spouse passes away, the surviving spouse automatically becomes the sole owner, bypassing the probate process.
- **Protection from Creditors:** Creditors of one spouse cannot force the sale of the property to collect debts.

## Comparison with Joint Tenancy

Both Tenancy by the Entirety and Joint Tenancy share these features:

- **Right of Survivorship**
- Requirement of the **four unities**: interest, title, time, and possession

However, Tenancy by the Entirety is unique in its durability through marriage—it remains intact as long as the spouses are married.
