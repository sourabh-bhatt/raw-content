# Property Ownership for Married Couples in Virginia

<!-- Rewritten it -->

<!-- When you're learning about **property ownership** for **married couples** in Virginia, you need to understand that Virginia follows **common law property** rules, not community property laws. -->

In Virginia, **married couples** are subject to **common law property** rules rather than community property laws.

## Virginia Property Ownership Types

Virginia recognizes several ways married couples can own property:

### Tenancy by the Entirety

This is the most common form of ownership for married couples in Virginia. Both spouses own the entire property together. When one spouse dies, the other automatically owns the whole property.

### Joint Tenancy with Right of Survivorship

Both spouses own equal shares of the property. When one spouse dies, the surviving spouse automatically gets the deceased spouse's share.

### Tenancy in Common

Each spouse owns a separate share of the property. These shares can be equal or unequal. When one spouse dies, their share goes to their heirs, not automatically to the surviving spouse.

## States That Use Community Property

Virginia is NOT a community property state. Only these states use community property laws:

- Arizona
- California
- Idaho
- Louisiana
- Nevada
- New Mexico
- Texas
- Washington
- Wisconsin

## Why This Matters in Virginia Real Estate

Understanding Virginia's property ownership laws helps in:

- Advise clients on how to take title
- Understand what happens when property owners die
- Know the legal rights of each spouse in property transactions
