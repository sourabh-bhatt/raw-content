# Easement by Implication in Property Rights

An easement by implication arises without a written contract. It is created through the actions or conduct of the parties involved.

## Understanding Easement by Implication

Picture this scenario:

- A large plot of land is divided into smaller parcels.
- Prior to the division, a part of the land was used to access or provide utility to another part—for example, a path used to reach the main road.
- This existing use can lead to the recognition of an **easement by implication** after the division.

It is essential that this easement is **reasonably necessary** for the property to be used as intended. However, it does *not* need to meet the stricter criterion of being **absolutely necessary**, which applies to easements created out of necessity.

## Example of Easement by Implication

Owning mineral rights on land allows entry to extract the minerals, even if the deed does not mention access rights. This is an example of an implied easement, which permits use of the property as allowed by law, even without specific wording in the deed.

This example highlights a fundamental expectation in property transactions: when a property is divided, it is assumed that the seller intended to grant the buyer all necessary easement rights to fully use and enjoy the property.
