# Property Rights: Understanding Dower and Curtesy

<!-- When delving into **property rights**, it's essential to comprehend concepts like **dower** and **curtesy**, even if they're not used today. These were legal rights that spouses had in each other's property under old common law. -->

Concepts like **dower** and **curtesy** provide historical context for property rights. These were legal rights that spouses had in each other's property under old common law.

## Dower

- **Dower** was a right where a wife could inherit part of her husband's property when he died.
- This meant she gained a **life interest** in part of his real estate. This gave her a place to live or income during her widowhood.

## Curtesy

- **Curtesy** was a similar concept but for husbands.
- When his wife died, a husband could receive a limited inheritance in his wife's property.
- This was often a **life interest** in part of her real estate, with similar purposes to dower.

## Modern Changes in Property Rights

- Most states have eliminated these old concepts to create equality.
- Virginia abolished dower and curtesy rights. The state now provides the same **inheritance rights** to both men and women when their spouse dies.
- This change eliminates gender discrimination in property rights. It aligns with modern values of fairness and equality.
