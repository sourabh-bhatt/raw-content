# Understanding Statutory Estates in Property Rights

**Statutory Estate** refers to property interests created by state law rather than private agreements. These rights exist because laws define how property is owned, used, or transferred.

## Key Examples of Statutory Estates

Let's break down some key examples of statutory estates for a clearer picture:

- **Dower and Curtesy**: These are somewhat old-fashioned terms that may still appear. They refer to the legal rights spouses have in each other's property. _Dower_ applies to a surviving wife's right to one-third of her deceased husband's real estate for life, while _curtesy_ refers to a surviving husband's right to his deceased wife's real estate for life. These concepts have evolved over time, but they're foundational in understanding **spousal rights to property**.

- **Community Property**: Virginia is NOT a community property state. Virginia follows common law property rules where property belongs to the spouse whose name is on the title. This has important implications for buying and selling property, as understanding whose name is on the title determines ownership rights.

- **Homestead Exemption**: This is a protective law that can shield a portion of a home's value from certain creditors, providing families with a degree of financial security. In Virginia, the homestead exemption protects up to $25,000 of home equity from creditors ($50,000 if over age 65 or disabled). The specifics can vary widely from state to state, so it is important to understand the local rules wherever practicing.
