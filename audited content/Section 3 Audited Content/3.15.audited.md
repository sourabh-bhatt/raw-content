# Private Controls in Property Rights

**Private controls** influence the use and appearance of residential properties. They help maintain harmony and consistency within communities.

## Restrictive Covenants

**Restrictive covenants** are like promises written into a property's deed or lease agreement. These are agreements made, usually by developers, that set rules on how a property can be used or what it can look like. Think of it as a set of rules that stays with the land, no matter who owns it over time. These covenants "run with the land," meaning they bind all future owners. They're there to ensure that the property maintains certain standards, whether it's about the color of houses or the types of fences allowed.

## Easements vs. Restrictive Covenants

When comparing **restrictive covenants** to **easements**, remember that both can apply to future owners of the land. However:

- **Easements** do not restrict the owner's use of the property. They grant another party a right of use, such as a right of way.

- **Restrictive covenants** limit how a property may be used or altered to maintain agreed-upon standards within a community.

## Zoning Laws

**Zoning** also restricts how properties can be used, but unlike restrictive covenants, zoning is based on laws set by local governments. In cases where zoning laws and deed covenants clash:

- **The more restrictive rule applies**. If zoning allows something but deed covenants prohibit it, the deed covenants control. If deed covenants allow something but zoning prohibits it, zoning controls.

## Enforcement of Restrictive Covenants

If someone violates these restrictive covenants, **Homeowners' Associations (HOAs)** or affected owners have the right to intervene. They can ask a court to enforce these rules. This preserves the intended character of the neighborhood.

### Important Note on Restrictions

Restrictive covenants cannot unreasonably prevent someone from selling their property. They must not impose unfair limitations on the owner's ability to sell or mortgage their land. The right to transfer property is a fundamental aspect of ownership.

## Homeowners' Associations (HOAs)

**HOAs** are organizations within a community that set and enforce rules for properties in their jurisdiction. Key points about HOAs include:

- Created by real estate developers and governed by property owners through elected Boards.
- Aim to uphold community standards. This covers aspects such as noise control and the aesthetic appeal of homes.
- Non-compliance can result in fines or loss of access to community amenities.

HOAs have their ground rules laid out in **bylaws** and **declarations**. These may cover:

- Management of common areas
- Setting fees for community upkeep
- Guidelines for maintaining the neighborhood's visual appeal

Failure to comply with these rules can lead to penalties. This includes fines or a lien on property for unpaid dues.
