# Reasonable Accommodations in Housing Legislation

<!-- As you delve into the realm of housing legislation, it's crucial to grasp the concept of **reasonable accommodations**. This is especially important when it comes to understanding the rights of **tenants with disabilities** and the responsibilities of **landlords**. -->

Housing legislation includes the concept of **reasonable accommodations**. This affects the rights of **tenants with disabilities** and the responsibilities of **landlords**. Landlords must follow specific rules based on when their rental units were built.

## Tenant's Right to Modify Dwelling Units

<!-- One significant aspect you need to know is a tenant with a disability's right to make changes to their dwelling unit. This means that as a tenant with a disability, you're allowed to modify your living space to better suit your needs. However, these modifications must be **reasonable**, and the **expense** for them falls on you, the tenant. -->

Tenants with disabilities have the right to make changes to their dwelling units. Tenants with disabilities may modify their living space to better suit their needs. However, these modifications must be **reasonable**, and the **expense** falls on the tenant.

## Landlord's Role in Reasonable Accommodations

<!-- Beyond modifications to the physical space, landlords have another vital role in accommodating tenants with disabilities. -->

Beyond modifications to the physical space, landlords have another role in accommodating tenants with disabilities. They must make reasonable accommodations in their **rules, policies, and services** to ensure that persons with disabilities have the same opportunity to use and enjoy the premises as anyone else. For example:

- A landlord might need to bend the rules about where **service animals** are allowed if a tenant requires one for assistance.
