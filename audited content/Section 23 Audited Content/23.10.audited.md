# Taking Legal Action Against Housing Discrimination

<!-- ## Your Right to Initiate Civil Action -->

## Right to Initiate Civil Action

<!-- When you encounter **discrimination** in your housing transactions, it's important to know how you can take action. You have the right to initiate a **civil action** in a relevant **U.S. district court** or **state court**. -->

Individuals who encounter **discrimination** in housing transactions have the right to initiate a **civil action** in a relevant **U.S. district court** or **state court**. This action can be started up to **two years** after either:

- The event of the discriminatory practice, or
- The end of a breached **conciliation agreement**

<!-- whichever happened last. This allows you to seek proper remedies for any discriminatory housing practices you've experienced. -->

whichever happened last. This allows individuals to seek proper remedies for any discriminatory housing practices experienced.

## Timeframes for Filing Civil Actions

If your case has gone through an **administrative process**, there is a specific timeframe to keep in mind:

<!-- - You can start a civil action no later than **180 days** after the administrative process concludes, or
- Up to **two years** following the discrimination or its cessation, -->

- Individuals can start a civil action no later than **180 days** after the administrative process concludes, or
- Up to **two years** following the discrimination or its cessation,

<!-- whichever comes later. Importantly, you can pursue this legal route regardless of whether you've filed a formal complaint or the status of that complaint. -->

whichever comes later. Individuals can pursue this legal route regardless of whether they have filed a formal complaint or the status of that complaint.

## Possible Remedies and Court Powers

In these civil actions, if it's determined that discriminatory housing practices have occurred or are imminent, the court or jury can:

- Award **compensatory damages**
- Award **punitive damages**
<!-- - Order payment of your **attorney's fees** and other costs -->
- Order payment of **attorney's fees** and other costs
- Issue various **orders** or **injunctions** to provide relief

## Limitations on Relief

<!-- It's crucial to understand that any relief granted will **not undo prior contracts, sales, encumbrances, or leases**. -->

Any relief granted will **not undo prior contracts, sales, encumbrances, or leases**. This applies to those completed before the relief was granted and involving parties who had no real-time knowledge of the complaint or civil action.

## Role of the Attorney General

<!-- In cases deemed significantly important to the public, the **Attorney General** has the right to join your civil action. With this intervention, the Attorney General can seek the same kinds of relief that you, as a private party, would be eligible for. -->

In cases deemed important to the public, the **Attorney General** has the right to join civil actions. With this intervention, the Attorney General can seek the same kinds of relief that private parties would be eligible for.
