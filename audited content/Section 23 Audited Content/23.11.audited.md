# Reasonable Modifications and Accessibility in Housing for Individuals with Disabilities

<!-- When studying housing laws, it's crucial to understand the rights and accommodations related to individuals with disabilities. Housing laws include rights and accommodations related to individuals with disabilities. **Reasonable modifications** represent a key concept for ensuring that housing units are accessible and livable for **persons with disabilities**.

## Rights to Reasonable Modifications

- If a person with disabilities is living in, or wishes to live in, an individual housing unit, they have the right to make modifications to their living space.
- These modifications must be **reasonably necessary** to make the unit more accessible and comfortable.
- This right applies to housing units constructed both before and after **March 13, 1991**.
- The term **reasonably necessary** means adjustments that do not cause **undue hardship** or a **fundamental alteration** to the premises.

## Landlord Obligations for Post-March 13, 1991 Properties

For properties built after March 13, 1991, landlords have specific obligations to ensure accessibility from the outset:

- Construction of **public and common areas** must be usable by individuals with disabilities.
- Spaces should be designed to be easy to use and accessible to everyone, regardless of physical capabilities.
- **Doors** must be installed wide enough to accommodate **wheelchairs**, facilitating ease of movement into, out of, and within the unit, as well as in common areas such as hallways and entrances.
