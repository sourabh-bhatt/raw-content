# 4.95 Assignable vs. Non-Assignable Contracts in Real Estate

Contracts differ between those that are **assignable** and those that are **not assignable**. Real estate contracts offer diverse stipulations regarding assignability.

## Non-Assignable Contracts: Personal Service Contracts

-   A common example is a **listing agreement** where a specific real estate broker is engaged to sell a property.
-   This type of contract is **not assignable**.
-   The party **cannot transfer obligations or rights** under this contract to someone else.
-   The **personal service nature** of the agreement - where the expertise, trust, or reputation of the individual or entity is paramount - underpins its non-assignability.

## Assignable Contracts: Most Other Real Estate Agreements

Most other real estate contracts stand on a different footing. These include:

-   **Purchase agreements**
-   **Deeds of trust**
-   **Contracts for sale**
-   **Lease contracts**
-   **Options**

The default position for these documents is that they are **assignable**. Unless explicitly stated otherwise within the contract, parties may **transfer their rights and obligations to another party**. However, assignment typically requires consent from all parties to the contract.

## Contract Assignability Considerations

This difference affects real estate transactions and influences negotiation and deal handling. Determining whether a contract is assignable aids in:

-   Planning
-   Strategy
-   Managing expectations and obligations efficiently

This knowledge enables effective contract use in real estate practice.
