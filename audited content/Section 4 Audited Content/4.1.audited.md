# Understanding Void and Voidable Contracts

<!-- When diving into the world of **contracts**, it's crucial to understand the concepts of **void** and **voidable contracts**.  -->
These terms stem from certain **defenses** that can either prevent a contract from taking shape initially or allow one party to withdraw from an otherwise valid contract.

## Key Differences Between Void and Voidable Contracts

<!-- Imagine you're entering into a contract, but something isn't quite right. This "something" could be various factors such as **incompetency**, **misrepresentation**, **mistake**, **illegality**, or **duress**. Depending on the nature of this issue, the contract you're dealing with might be considered either **void** or **voidable**. -->
Consider entering into a contract with certain issues. These issues could be various factors such as **incompetency**, **misrepresentation**, **mistake**, **illegality**, or **duress**. Depending on the nature of this issue, the contract might be considered either **void** or **voidable**. 

### Void Contract

- A void contract is like a building that was never built.
- From the beginning, there's something fundamentally missing that prevents it from being considered a valid contract.
<!-- - It is as if the contract never existed because it lacks the necessary elements to be recognized legally. -->
- The contract never existed because it lacks the necessary elements to be recognized legally.   

### Voidable Contract

- A voidable contract is like a building with a shaky foundation.
- It stands, looks fine on the outside, and functions like any other building.
- However, if the flaw is discovered, the whole structure's integrity comes into question. 
<!-- - However, if you discover the flaw, the whole structure's integrity comes into question. -->
- In contract terms, this means that while the contract is valid, **fairness** or **legal principles** give one party the power to exit the agreement without facing liability.
<!-- - This is possible provided they can prove the existence of one of the defenses mentioned above. -->
- This is possible provided they can prove the existence of one of the defenses mentioned above.
