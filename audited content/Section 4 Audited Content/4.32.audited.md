# Understanding the Legal Boundaries of Drafting Purchase Agreements

<!-- When navigating the world of real estate transactions, it's critical to understand the boundaries of what you **can and cannot do**, especially concerning **drafting purchase agreements**. -->
Understanding the boundaries of what brokers **can and cannot do** affects **drafting purchase agreements** in real estate transactions.

## Unauthorized Practice of Law

Virtually everywhere across the nation, there are stringent rules against **practicing law without a license**. This includes the act of drafting contracts for someone else, which extends to real estate purchase agreements. Specifically:

- Drafting real estate contracts from scratch without being a licensed attorney can lead to accusations of **unauthorized practice of law**.

## Role of Non-Attorney Brokers

<!-- As a **non-attorney broker**, you are generally allowed to: -->
A **non-attorney broker** is generally allowed to:

- Help and guide clients through the process of filling out **standard purchase agreements**.
- Use **template forms** that require essential information to be filled in.

<!-- However, it is important to note: -->
However, non-attorney brokers:

- Must not draft contracts themselves.
- Cannot charge an additional fee for assisting clients with these standard forms.

## State Variations: The Example of Virginia

Rules can vary widely based on location. For example:

- In **Virginia**, real estate brokers are permitted to **prepare sales contracts directly** for their clients without the risk of being charged with unauthorized practice of law.

This exception highlights the need to understand specific state regulations.

## Best Practices for Brokers

- Support and advise clients using **standard, pre-formatted agreements**.
- Avoid drafting contracts from scratch.
- Brokers cannot charge extra fees for helping clients complete template forms.
