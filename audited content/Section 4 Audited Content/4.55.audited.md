# Legal vs. Non-Legal Property Descriptions in Real Estate

<!-- When you're diving into the world of real estate, one concept you'll encounter is the difference between **legal** and **non-legal descriptions** of property. -->

Real estate involves the difference between **legal** and **non-legal descriptions** of property.

## Non-Legal Descriptions

<!-- Non-legal descriptions are basically a simpler, more casual way to talk about or identify real estate. They are *not* meant for official documents like deeds or mortgages, where you need a precise, legal description. Think of non-legal descriptions as conversational shorthand used in settings like advertising or when drafting listing agreements. -->

Non-legal descriptions are a simpler way to identify real estate. They are *not* meant for official documents like deeds or mortgages, where precise, legal descriptions are required. Non-legal descriptions serve as conversational shorthand used in settings like advertising or when drafting listing agreements.

### Common Examples of Non-Legal Descriptions

- **Street Address**: The most typical example. It helps people find where a property is located but does *not* provide the detailed information needed to determine the exact boundaries of the property. However, when putting together a sales contract, just the street address may be sufficient to identify the property, though legal descriptions are preferred for clarity.

- **Front Footage**: Refers to the part of the property that touches a street or body of water. This is another non-legal way to describe a piece of land, focusing on its access points rather than its precise dimensions or boundaries.

## Use in Real Estate Transactions

<!-- Understanding the difference between these descriptions and when to use them is key in real estate transactions. Non-legal descriptions are useful for communication and marketing, but remember, they do *not* replace the detailed, precise requirements of a **legal property description** in formal documents. Non-legal descriptions should be supplemented with legal descriptions in important documents. -->

The difference between these descriptions affects their use in real estate transactions. Non-legal descriptions are useful for communication and marketing, but they do *not* replace the detailed, precise requirements of a **legal property description** in formal documents. Non-legal descriptions should be supplemented with legal descriptions in formal documents.
