# Assignment in Real Estate Contracts

<!-- **Assignment** is when you transfer the *rights*, *title*, or *interest* you have in a contract to another person. **Assignment** is when a party transfers the *rights*, *title*, or *interest* they have in a contract to another person. 

Example: Think of it like passing a baton in a relay race: the original party is handing over their position (and obligations) to someone else, who is called the **assignee**, while the original party is the **assignor**.

## Key Points About Assignment

- <!-- Not all contracts can be assigned. Unless the contract specifically prohibits it, most contracts allow you to assign your rights to someone else. -->
Not all contracts can be assigned. Unless the contract specifically prohibits it, most contracts allow parties to assign their rights to someone else.
- In property transactions, sellers often dislike contracts being assigned without their consent.
- To address this, sellers include an **assignment clause** that:
  - Either prohibits assignment outright, or
  - Allows assignment only with the seller's *written approval*.
- Even if assignment is permitted, the original buyer (assignor) usually remains liable if the new buyer (assignee) fails to fulfill the contract terms.

<!-- Understanding assignment is crucial because it affects your flexibility and responsibilities within a contract. -->

If parties need or want to transfer their interest in a contract, they must:

- Carefully review the contract to see if assignment is allowed.
- Understand any conditions or restrictions on assignment.
