# 4.91 Power of Attorney in Real Estate Transactions

A **Power of Attorney** is a written document that appoints a person as another's *attorney-in-fact*. This role empowers the appointee to make decisions and take actions on behalf of the grantor. The power granted is deeply personal and based on trust between the parties.

## Termination of Power of Attorney

-   The Power of Attorney automatically ends when either the attorney-in-fact or the person who granted the power passes away.
-   This termination occurs because the authorization is intimately linked to the personal trust and agreement between the two parties.

## Conveying Property Using Power of Attorney

When conveying property on behalf of the grantor, the following requirements apply:

-   The attorney-in-fact must provide proof that the grantor is still alive at the time any documents are signed.
-   This step ensures all parties that the transaction is legitimate.
-   It confirms that the attorney-in-fact remains lawfully empowered to act on the grantor's behalf.

Example: When signing a deed to transfer property for the grantor, the attorney-in-fact must verify the grantor's living status at the time of signing to ensure the conveyance is valid and enforceable.
