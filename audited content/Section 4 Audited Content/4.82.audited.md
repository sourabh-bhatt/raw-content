# 4.82 Intoxication and Contract Capacity

In Virginia real estate contracts, **intoxication** affects a person's **capacity to enter into a contract**. When someone was intoxicated at the time they entered the contract, Virginia law considers them as having **limited capacity to contract**. This means the contract may not be binding.

## Voidable Contracts Due to Intoxication

Under Virginia Code § 11-9.1, when a party was intoxicated at the time of signing a real estate contract, a specific window exists to declare the contract **voidable**. Key points include:

-   The affected party must act **quickly** to cancel the contract.
-   Delaying action can be interpreted as **acceptance** of the contract's terms.
-   Once accepted, the contract becomes **ratified and binding**.

## Legal Implications

Intoxication affects contract law when **capacity to contract** is questioned. Prompt action regarding this issue can impact the **validity of a contract** and protect legal rights.
