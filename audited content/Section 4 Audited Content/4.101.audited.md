# 4.101 Understanding Breach of Contract in Real Estate

A **breach of contract** occurs when someone doesn't hold up their end of the agreement. When the other party hasn't kept their promise according to the contract, and there's no valid legal reason for their failure, the **non-breaching party** could be eligible for compensation.

## Forms of Compensation for Breach of Contract

This compensation can take different forms:

-   **Monetary damages**: Often awarded to compensate for losses.
-   **Liquidated damages**: These are predetermined amounts agreed upon beforehand in the contract, serving as a forecasted estimation of losses in case of a breach.
-   **Specific performance**: A court order requiring the breaching party to fulfill their original obligations under the contract.

> **Note:** The non-breaching party cannot receive both monetary damages (including liquidated damages) and specific performance simultaneously; it is one or the other.

## Limitations on Damages in Contract Breaches

The law typically does *not* allow the following in cases of contract breaches:

-   **Punitive damages**: These are intended to punish the breaching party and are generally not awarded in contract disputes.
-   **Compensation for pain and suffering**: Damages for intangible harm or emotional distress are usually not recoverable in contract law.

These boundaries govern the management and response to contract breaches.
