# Minors and Contracting in Real Estate

<!-- Understanding the concept of **minors** in contracting is crucial for your knowledge base. Let's break it down: -->
The concept of **minors** in contracting affects legal capacity and enforceability:

## Who Is Considered a Minor?

- In Virginia, anyone under **18 years of age** is considered a minor.
- Minors have **limited capacity** to enter into contracts.

## Voidable Contracts and Minors

- Contracts entered into by minors are generally **voidable**.
- This means a minor has the ability to **disaffirm** or back out of a contract they have entered into.
- This protection recognizes the limits in judgment and experience of individuals under 18.

## Disaffirming a Contract

- A minor cannot ignore a contract indefinitely.
- Upon reaching age 18, the individual must **reject or disaffirm the contract within a reasonable time**.
- Failure to act within this timeframe makes the contract **binding and enforceable**.
- This transition marks the shift from protections afforded to minors to the responsibilities expected of adults.

## Contracts for Necessities

<!-- - There is an important exception regarding contracts for **necessities**. -->
- An exception exists regarding contracts for **necessities**.
- Necessities include essentials such as **food, clothing, and shelter**.
- Contracts made by minors for necessities are **not automatically voidable**.
- Courts evaluate each case to determine if the contract genuinely served a necessary purpose.
- If so, these contracts may be **enforceable** against the minor.
