# Understanding Monetary Damages in Breach of Contract

<!-- When you're dealing with a **breach of contract**, understanding how **monetary damages** work is crucial. These are essentially the financial compensation due to one party from another when the terms of their agreement aren't met. Let's break it down. -->
**Monetary damages** are financial compensation due to one party from another when the terms of their agreement aren't met in a **breach of contract**.

## Types of Monetary Damages

<!-- There are two primary types of monetary damages you'll encounter: -->
Two primary types of monetary damages exist:

- **Liquidated Damages**
  - This is a specific amount that both parties agree upon in the contract itself, to be paid out if the contract is breached.
  - <!-- Think of it as a predefined penalty for not sticking to the agreement. -->
  This serves as a predefined penalty for not sticking to the agreement.

- **Benefit of the Bargain Damages**
  - <!-- This type is more intricate and depends on whether you are the seller or the buyer. -->
  This type is more intricate and depends on whether the party is the seller or the buyer.

### For Sellers

- Damages equal the difference between the **contract price** and the **fair market value** of the property.
- <!-- This represents the profit you would have made had the deal gone through as planned. -->
  This represents the profit the seller would have made had the deal gone through as planned.

### For Buyers

- Damages equal the difference between the **fair market value** and the **contract price**.
- <!-- This represents the savings or financial advantage you missed out on because the contract wasn't fulfilled. -->
  This represents the savings or financial advantage the buyer missed out on because the contract wasn't fulfilled.

## Understanding Monetary Damages

- <!-- Knowing how these damages are calculated and applied ensures you have a clear footing on what financial remedies you can pursue if a breach occurs. -->
  Knowing how these damages are calculated and applied ensures clear understanding of financial remedies available if a breach occurs.
- This helps in drafting contracts that are fair and enforceable, protecting all parties involved.
