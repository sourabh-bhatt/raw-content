# Understanding the "Time Is of the Essence" Clause in Real Estate Contracts

<!-- When you encounter the phrase **"time is of the essence"** in a contract, it signals that the parties involved have agreed on a specific deadline for a task to be completed. -->
The phrase **"time is of the essence"** in a contract signals that the parties involved have agreed on a specific deadline for a task to be completed. This clause carries weight because if the agreed-upon deadline is not met—even by a few minutes—it could be considered a **breach of the contract**.

## Example of the Clause in Action

Example: A contract specifies a property closing date and time as **"November 3, 2009, at 12:00 pm,"** and explicitly states that **"time is of the essence."** This means:

- Arriving at 12:05 pm, even just five minutes late, would constitute a breach of the sales contract.
- The strictness of this clause ensures all parties respect and adhere to the timelines set in the contract.

## When "Time Is of the Essence" Is Not Included

If a contract does **not** specify that **"time is of the essence,"** the situation is more flexible:

- The law allows a **reasonable amount of time** to complete the task.
- What is considered **reasonable** depends on the nature of the act that needs to be performed.
