# Understanding Survival and Merger in Real Estate Contracts

<!-- When diving into the world of **contracts**, it's crucial to understand the concepts of **survival** and **merger**, especially in Virginia real estate transactions. -->

## The Concept of Merger

<!-- In contract negotiations, the promises and obligations outlined in your contract typically **"merge"** into the **deed** once the real estate transaction closes. -->
In contract negotiations, the promises and obligations outlined in the contract typically **"merge"** into the **deed** once the real estate transaction closes. 

<!-- - This means that once the transaction closes, if the other party hasn't completely held up their end of the bargain, but you've still accepted the property, you generally cannot insist on full performance thereafter. -->
- This means that once the transaction closes, if the other party hasn't completely held up their end of the bargain, but the buyer has still accepted the property, the buyer generally cannot insist on full performance thereafter.

### Example of Merger

Example: Consider dealing with a contract that includes a **merger clause**, and within this contract, there's a promise from the seller to deliver a house with shutters freshly painted. However, at settlement, the shutters remain unpainted. 

- If you, as the buyer, accept the house at settlement without raising objections or negotiating a price reduction or credit for the unpainted shutters, your ability to later seek remedies for painting those shutters is typically lost.

## Protecting Yourself: The Survival Clause

<!-- To avoid such situations, the key is to include a contract clause that allows certain promises and obligations to **"survive"** closing. -->
To avoid such situations, include a contract clause that allows certain promises and obligations to **"survive"** closing. 

- This means that even after the property changes hands, specific agreed-upon responsibilities remain enforceable.
<!-- - Such clauses safeguard your interests or those of your client by ensuring that all agreed-upon terms are met, even after the keys have been handed over. -->
- Such clauses safeguard interests by ensuring that all agreed-upon terms are met, even after the keys have been handed over.
