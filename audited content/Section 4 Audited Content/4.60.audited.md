# Power of Attorney and Contract Enforcement in Real Estate

## Power of Attorney

A **Power of Attorney** is a written document. It lets one person give another person legal authority to act for them. The person giving the authority is called the **principal**. The person receiving the authority is called the **attorney-in-fact**.

As a real estate agent, you will encounter these documents in your practice.

- The **scope of authority** can vary:
  - It might be broad. This allows the attorney-in-fact to handle many tasks such as selling property.
  - It can be narrow. This restricts the attorney-in-fact to specific actions only.

- The relationship is **personal**. It **ends** if either the principal or the attorney-in-fact dies.

- If an attorney-in-fact needs to conduct a real estate transaction, they may need to provide proof that the principal is still alive.

## Contract Enforcement

**Not all contracts are enforceable**. As a real estate agent, you need to understand what makes a contract unenforceable.

- Knowing these factors helps you:
  - Avoid potential problems.
  - Guide your clients better.

- Many factors can affect whether a contract can be enforced. These factors can help protect agents and clients from legal problems.
