# Liquidated Damages in Real Estate Contracts

<!-- When entering into a contract, it's crucial to understand the concept of **liquidated damages**. **Liquidated damages** are damages that contracting parties agree upon in advance, should there be a **breach of contract**. If someone doesn't uphold their end of the deal, liquidated damages serve as a pre-decided amount that compensates the non-breaching party.

## Points About Liquidated Damages

- When liquidated damages are specified in a contract, they usually represent the **sole form of compensation** available if the other party breaches the agreement.
- This usually means the non-breaching party cannot pursue other types of monetary damages or **specific performance** actions, which compel the breaching party to fulfill their original obligations under the contract.
- The liquidated damages amount must be a reasonable estimate of actual damages that would occur from a breach.

## Liquidated Damages in Purchase Agreements

In the realm of purchase agreements, liquidated damages often take the form of **earnest money** or any payments made according to the contract thus far. 

- **Earnest money** is typically a deposit made to show good faith when submitting a purchase offer.
- This earnest money can be retained as liquidated damages by the non-breaching party if the contract is breached.
