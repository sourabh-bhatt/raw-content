# Consideration in Real Estate Transactions

<!-- Consideration is a **fundamental concept** you need to grasp, especially when it comes to understanding how real estate transactions work. -->
Consideration is a **fundamental concept** in real estate transactions.

## What is Consideration?

Consideration is something of value that each party gives or promises to give in exchange for the other party's promise. 

<!-- For example, imagine you are making an offer to purchase a property. The moment the seller accepts your offer, you can see **consideration** at work: -->

Example: When making an offer to purchase a property, the moment the seller accepts the offer, **consideration** is at work:

- As the **buyer**, you promise to pay a certain amount of money.
- As the **seller**, the seller promises to transfer ownership of the property at an agreed-upon time.

## Consideration in Contracts

<!-- Consideration is crucial because it ensures there is an **exchange of value** between the parties. -->
Consideration ensures there is an **exchange of value** between the parties. Without it, the agreement may not be legally enforceable. For instance:

- If a seller promises to give their property away as a **gift** without requiring anything in return, there is no valid contract.
- This is because there is no mutual exchange—no consideration—because only one party is giving something of value.

## Legal Significance

For a contract to be **enforceable** in real estate:

- Both parties must agree to give something up.
- The seller gives up the property.
- The buyer gives up a certain sum of money.

Without this **mutual exchange**, the agreement lacks one of the essential elements that make contracts valid and binding.
