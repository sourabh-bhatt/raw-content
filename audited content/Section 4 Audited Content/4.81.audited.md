# 4.81 Terminating Purchase Agreements in Real Estate

**Terminating purchase agreements** involves consequences when either party fails to uphold the agreement.

## Buyer Breach of Purchase Agreement

When the **buyer** fails to follow through with purchasing the real estate as committed, this constitutes a **breach of the purchase agreement**. These agreements typically specify the actions that can be taken in the event of a breach:

-   Most often, buyer breach results in loss of the **earnest money deposit**. This deposit is usually treated as **liquidated damages**, a pre-determined amount agreed upon to compensate for the breach.
-   If the agreement does not specify a remedy or limit it to liquidated damages, the seller may:
    -   Sue for the **"benefit of the bargain"**, meaning the profit they would have made from the completed transaction.
    -   Pursue **specific performance**, a legal action compelling the buyer to complete the purchase as originally agreed.

## Seller Breach of Purchase Agreement

When the **seller breaches** the agreement, the buyer has the right to:

-   Terminate the contract and demand the return of earnest money.
-   Seek compensation for any **actual losses** incurred if there is no liquidated damages clause.
-   Pursue **specific performance**, which would force the seller to proceed with selling the property as agreed.

> *Note:* Specific performance is more common in real estate due to the unique nature of property, where monetary compensation might not be sufficient.

## Effect of Death on Purchase Agreements

The death of either party (buyer or seller) does **not** automatically terminate the purchase agreement. Instead:

-   The obligations of the deceased party transfer to their **estate**.
-   The agreement remains in effect and must be fulfilled according to its original terms.

These concepts demonstrate the binding nature of real estate transactions. Knowledge of breach implications and party rights and obligations governs transaction processes.
