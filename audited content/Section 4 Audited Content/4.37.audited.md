# Understanding Defenses in Contracts

<!-- When you're navigating the world of **contracts**, it's crucial to understand the concept of **defenses**. Think of a defense as a significant issue that, if shown to be true, can: -->

Contract **defenses** are legal issues that, if proven true, can:

- Stop a contract from ever coming into existence (making it **void**), or
- Turn a contract that seemed valid into one that cannot be enforced by law (making it **voidable**).

<!-- This means that even if you see what looks like a typical agreement process—an **offer**, **acceptance**, and the exchange of something of value (**consideration**)—you might not actually have a legally binding contract on your hands. -->

Even when an agreement appears to have the typical elements - an **offer**, **acceptance**, and **consideration** - a legally binding contract may not exist if defenses apply.

<!-- Recognizing and identifying these defenses is key to ensuring that you're dealing with contracts that are not only clear in terms but also **enforceable under the law**. -->

Identifying these defenses helps determine whether contracts are enforceable under law.
