# 4.67 Understanding Consideration in Contract Law

**_Consideration_** serves as a fundamental concept in contract law, particularly in monetary transactions.

## What is Consideration?

Consideration represents a **trade-off** between contracting parties. Two parties exchange promises where each agrees to perform or refrain from specific actions. This exchange can involve delivering goods, making payments, or other commitments. Both sides must promise action or forbearance - this constitutes **consideration**.

## Charitable Promises and Enforceability

One-sided promises made without expecting return consideration, known as **_charitable promises_**, generally lack legal enforceability. Several factors explain this:

-   There is no bargaining or exchange of promises.
-   If someone pledges to give away their property as a gift but then decides against it, that promise can easily become unenforceable.

Courts are cautious about enforcing these charitable promises. They prefer not to obligate someone to carry out an act of charity, even if they've promised to do so.

## Legal Significance

Both parties must have commitments at stake - actions they have agreed to perform or avoid. This mutual exchange provides legal enforceability to contracts, making **consideration** a foundational element in contract law.
