# Legal Capacity and Contractual Agreements

## Understanding Legal Capacity

<!-- When you're dealing with **contractual agreements**, it's crucial to understand who has the **legal capacity** to enter into a contract. One critical aspect is recognizing when someone may not have the capacity due to **legal insanity**. -->
The **legal capacity** to enter into a contract affects contractual agreements. One aspect is recognizing when someone may not have the capacity due to **legal insanity**. This means the person has been officially declared incompetent or insane by a court.

People in such a state cannot be expected to comprehend the terms and conditions of a contract, as **understanding** is a fundamental requirement for **consent**. Without the ability to agree, there's no **"meeting of the minds,"** which is essential for a contract to be valid. Hence, any contracts made by someone deemed **legally incompetent** are considered **voidable** because of the absence of this mutual understanding.

## Exception: Lucid Intervals

<!-- There is an important exception to this rule: -->
An exception exists to this rule:

- During moments when an individual, previously declared legally insane, is found to be of **sound mind** (a period known as a **lucid interval**), any contract they enter into is **enforceable**.
- These **lucid intervals** are specific times when the person was capable of making informed decisions at the time of contract formation.
- If a contract is signed during one of these lucid intervals, it remains **valid and enforceable**, even if the person's mental state later deteriorates.
