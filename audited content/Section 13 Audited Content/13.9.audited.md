**Taxation** impacts property transactions substantially. Taxation represents the government's method of collecting revenue from citizens. This collected revenue funds various public activities and services, ranging from infrastructure projects to public education.

## Powers of Taxation

<!-- Let's break down the powers that come with taxation: -->
Taxation powers include:

1. **Setting Tax Rates**
   - The government has the authority to establish tax rates.
   - These taxes can be applied to property, known as **ad valorem taxes**, which are based on the value of the property.
   - Taxes can also be applied to specific projects through **special assessments**.
   - Ad valorem taxes are a common source of revenue for local governments and are critical to understanding property valuation and cost implications for buyers and sellers.

2. **Placing Liens on Property**
   - When taxes on a property remain unpaid, the government can impose a **lien** on it.
   - Types of liens include **special assessment liens**, **property tax liens**, and **federal tax liens**.
   - A lien marks the property indicating that a debt or obligation must be satisfied before the property can be sold or refinanced.
   <!-- - Understanding liens is pivotal as they can affect the transferability of property and complicate transactions. -->
   - Liens can affect property transferability and complicate transactions.

3. **Enforcing Tax Sales**
   - If liens remain unsettled, the government can enforce the sale of the property through a **tax sale**.
   - A tax sale is conducted to recover the owed tax amount.
   - This process can dramatically alter property ownership.
