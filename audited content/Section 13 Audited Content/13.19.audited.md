# IRS Form 1099-S Reporting in Residential Real Estate Transactions

**Residential real estate transactions** require familiarity with **Internal Revenue Service (IRS) Form 1099-S**. This form reports these transactions to the Federal Government.

## Scope of Transactions Requiring Form 1099-S

<!-- The scope of transactions requiring reporting on **Form 1099-S** is broad. You must report if the transaction involves any ownership interest in real estate, including: -->
Transactions requiring reporting on **Form 1099-S** have broad scope. Reporting is required if the transaction involves any ownership interest in real estate, including:

- Sales or exchanges of real estate
- Land, whether or not there are structures on it
- Permanent structures such as houses or commercial buildings
- Condominium units, including their fixtures and communal areas
- Shares in cooperative housing corporations

## Responsibility for Filing Form 1099-S

### Primary Responsible Party

- The settlement agent is usually responsible for filing Form 1099-S.
- If a settlement statement is used, the settlement agent named on this document is responsible.

### Alternate Responsible Parties

- If another type of closing statement is used or no specific settlement agent is listed, responsibility falls to the person who prepared the statement. This person shows the transaction details and how funds are distributed.

### Order of Responsibility When No Settlement Agent Is Designated

If no settlement agent is designated, the responsibility follows this order:

1. **Mortgage lender**
2. **Seller's broker**
3. **Buyer's broker**
4. **Buyer**
