Buying or selling real estate involves **transfer and recording taxes**. These **excise taxes**, sometimes referred to as **revenue stamps**, are fees charged by local jurisdictions for conducting real estate transactions within their territory. They represent the cost of doing business in real estate in that specific area.

## Who Pays Transfer and Recording Taxes?

Typically, the responsibility falls on the shoulders of the **seller**, also known as the **grantor**, or the **lessor**. These taxes need to be taken care of at a crucial timeâ€”**when the deed is recorded**.

## How Are These Taxes Paid?

Generally, payment involves purchasing a stamp from the official responsible for recording deeds. This stamp isn't just for show; it's affixed to the deed as solid proof that the tax has been fully paid.

## Calculating Transfer Taxes

<!-- The amount you'll pay for transfer taxes is usually tied directly to the **sales price** of the property. For example: -->
Transfer tax amounts are usually tied directly to the **sales price** of the property. Example:

- In Virginia, the tax rate is **$0.25 for every $100** of the property's sales price.

This is a straightforward calculation, but keep in mind, the rate can vary greatly depending on the jurisdiction.

## Common Exemptions from Transfer Taxes

Not every transfer will trigger these taxes. Some common exemptions include:

- Mortgages
- Correction deeds
- Transfers between a married couple
- Transfers between a parent and child
