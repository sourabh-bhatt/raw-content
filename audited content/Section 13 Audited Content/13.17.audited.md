# Understanding Property Taxes in Real Estate

<!-- When you dive into the world of real estate, understanding **property taxes** becomes crucial. Property taxes are fees that local governments impose on real estate, primarily to generate revenue for various governmental operations. -->
**Property taxes** are fees that local governments impose on real estate, primarily to generate revenue for various governmental operations.

## Authority and Enforcement

- **Local governments** have the power to tax properties.
- If property taxes go unpaid, local governments can seize and force the sale of real estate to settle those debts.

## Methods of Calculating Property Taxes

There are primarily two approaches to calculating property taxes:

### 1. Ad Valorem Process

- The term *ad valorem* means "according to value" in Latin.
<!-- - Taxes are assessed based on the unique value of your property. -->
- Taxes are assessed based on the unique value of the property.
- Essentially, the higher the property value, the higher the tax bill.

### 2. Special Assessments

- These are taxes levied for specific local improvements such as street paving, sewer additions, or recreational facilities.
- Only properties benefiting from these improvements are taxed.
- Unlike general property taxes, which fund broader government functions, special assessments are targeted.

## Tax Incentives and Benefits

Investing in real estate offers several tax advantages:

<!-- - **Real property taxes** are deductible on your Federal tax returns if you itemize deductions, which can lower your taxable income.
- **Mortgage interest payments** are also deductible. -->
- **Real property taxes** are deductible on Federal tax returns when itemizing deductions, which can lower taxable income.
- **Mortgage interest payments** are also deductible.
- For income-generating real estate, property taxes are considered **operating expenses**, which can reduce taxable gains or increase deductions for business losses.

## Priority of Liens

- Both **special assessments** and standard **property tax liens** have a high priority over other types of liens, regardless of when they were recorded.
