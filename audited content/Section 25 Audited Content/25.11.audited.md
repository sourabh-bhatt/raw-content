# National "Do Not Call" Registry and Real Estate Sales Calls

The National **"Do Not Call" Registry**, introduced in 2003, is a list where individuals add their phone numbers to signal that they don't want to receive sales or solicitation calls. This affects real estate professionals making calls to potential clients.

## Key Rules for Calling Potential Clients

- **Never make sales calls** to anyone registered on the National Registry. Their numbers are off-limits.
- A person's registration remains active until they either remove their number or disconnect their service. This means the list must be continually respected.
- **Before calling**, check the Registry through the dedicated website.
- Update your call lists by removing any Registry-listed numbers **at least every 30 days** to maintain compliance.
- These rules **do not apply** to:
  - Business-to-business calls
  - Calls from political organizations
  - Calls from charitable organizations

## Exceptions to the Rules

- **Existing relationships** allow calls without violating the rules.
- However, if the individual has made an **"Entity-Specific request"** to not receive calls from a company, that request must be honored.
- The **"safe harbor" clause** protects against accidental calls to Registry numbers, provided the caller can demonstrate:
  - A system is in place to prevent such calls
  - Regular checks of the Registry are performed
  - Records of these checks are maintained

## Penalties for Violations

- Violating the Registry rules can result in fines up to **$46,517 per call**.
- Violators may also face **injunctive remedies**, legally requiring them to stop certain activities.
- Each violating call is considered a separate offense, so penalties can accumulate quickly.
