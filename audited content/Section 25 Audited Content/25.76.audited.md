# Continuation of Real Estate Business After Broker's Death or Incapacitation

When a **licensed real estate broker**, who operates as a **sole proprietorship** or is the only licensed broker in a business entity, passes away or becomes incapacitated, their business can legally continue operations temporarily. The **Real Estate Board** allows the business of the deceased or incapacitated broker to continue for a limited period of **180 days**. This period is granted solely to wrap up the business affairs.

## Order of Preference for Business Continuation

The process for determining who may manage the business during this period follows a specific hierarchy:

1. **First Choice: Personal Representative**
   - A personal representative officially recognized by the court to manage the deceased broker's estate takes priority. This person helps conclude the business activities.

2. **Second Preference: Agent with Power of Attorney**
   - If no court-qualified personal representative exists, an agent designated through a **power of attorney** by the broker (before death or incapacitation) is next. The power of attorney must explicitly authorize the agent to handle these duties.

3. **Third Option: Executor Named in the Will**
   - If no agent was designated, the executor named in the broker's will is considered next to take charge.

4. **Fourth Option: Adult Family Member**
   - If there is no executor named, an adult family member of the deceased or incapacitated broker may be authorized to manage the business affairs.

5. **Fifth in Line: Employee or Independent Contractor**
   - If none of the above are available, an employee or independent contractor who worked with the broker may take responsibility to conclude the business.

If none of these options are suitable or available, the **Real Estate Board** has the authority to appoint another appropriate person to wrap up the business within the 180-day period.
