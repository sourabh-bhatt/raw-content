# When a Real Estate License Is Not Required

<!-- When working in real estate, you need to understand **when a license is not required**. This knowledge helps you follow the law and avoid problems. -->
In real estate, understanding **when a license is not required** helps ensure legal compliance.

## License Exemptions in Real Estate

- **Selling Personally Owned Property**: A license is not required to sell property that one owns. This allows people to handle their own real estate sales without getting licensed.

- **Acting Under Power of Attorney**: A license is not required if legally authorized to act for someone else under a power of attorney. However, **no financial compensation** can be received for real estate activities. This allows property management within legal limits without a license.

- **Attorneys Licensed to Practice Law**: Attorneys do not need a real estate license when handling real estate matters as part of their legal practice. This exemption recognizes their professional license and legal expertise.

- **Actions Taken Under Court Order**: People managing real estate in bankruptcy cases, wills, trusts, or estates under a court order do not need a real estate license. This helps with legal management of properties in these situations.

- **Auctioneers**: Auctioneers who auction property do not need a real estate license. This exemption covers the unique nature of property auctions.

- **Rental Agents**: Rental agents who only show rental properties to potential tenants do not need a real estate license. This exemption recognizes their limited activities. They only show properties rather than handle broader real estate transactions.

- **Sole Proprietor-Broker Owned Firms**: In Virginia, all real estate firms must be licensed regardless of structure. A sole proprietor-broker must have both an individual broker license and a firm license.
