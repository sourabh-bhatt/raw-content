# Virginia Real Estate Transaction Recovery Fund Act (TRF)

The Virginia Real Estate Transaction Recovery Fund Act (TRF) provides financial compensation to individuals who have incurred monetary losses due to the misconduct of a real estate agent. The TRF's nature, funding mechanisms, and consumer claim processes are part of real estate professional responsibilities.

## Understanding the Nature of the TRF

The **Transaction Recovery Fund (TRF)** acts as a financial safety net. If a client suffers financial loss because of dishonesty or wrongful actions by a licensed real estate agent, the TRF can compensate them. This system requires maintaining high ethical standards in practice.

## Funding the TRF

Funding for the TRF comes from several sources, with notable contributions from new licensees:

- When first becoming licensed, licensees are required to pay a **$20 fee** into the TRF.
- This is not just a one-time fee. Should the fund's balance drop below **$400,000**, the Board has the authority to assess an additional fee of up to **$20** from all agents, whether active or not, over a two-year period.
- Licensees will be notified by mail and given **45 days** to respond.
- If failing to pay after a reminder and an additional **30-day** period, licenses will be automatically suspended until the payment is made.

The interest earned by the TRF is used for administrative expenses. Surplus amounts are funneled into education, research, or to cover fund shortages, ensuring the TRF's sustainability and continued support to those it serves.

## Payments to Consumers

For a consumer to claim compensation from the TRF, the following process must be followed:

1. The consumer must secure a **final judgment** from a Virginia court, confirming that they have suffered a loss due to the improper or unethical conduct of a licensee.
2. The consumer must attempt to collect the full compensation from this judgment first, including:
   - Seizing the licensee's personal assets.
   - Filing a claim in any bankruptcy proceedings against the licensee.
3. If these efforts do not result in full compensation, the consumer can file a claim with the Board.
4. The claim must be submitted within **12 months** of the final judgment.
5. The claim must include a detailed **affidavit** outlining the licensee's actions and the steps taken by the consumer to seek reimbursement.
