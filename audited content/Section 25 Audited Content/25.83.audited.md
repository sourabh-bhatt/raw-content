# Understanding Individual Real Estate Licenses: Active and Inactive Status

<!-- When applying for your individual licenses, either as a **salesperson** or as a **broker**, it's crucial to understand the process and the options available to you. Let's break these down together, focusing on **initial licenses** and the concept of **active** and **inactive status**, to ensure you have a solid foundation. -->
When applying for individual licenses, either as a **salesperson** or as a **broker**, understanding the process and available options is required. The following covers **initial licenses** and the concept of **active** and **inactive status**.

## Initial Licenses
<!-- If you're stepping into the world of real estate without holding a license in any other state, you'll be looking at obtaining an **initial license**. This is your starting point. -->
Individuals entering real estate without holding a license in any other state must obtain an **initial license**. This is the starting point.

## Active vs. Inactive Status
<!-- Upon applying, you're faced with a decision: **active** or **inactive status**. This choice governs what you can and cannot do with your license, so let's dive into each. -->
Upon applying, applicants must choose between **active** or **inactive status**. This choice governs what can and cannot be done with the license.

### Active Status
<!-- Choosing an **active status** means you're ready to dive into real estate headfirst, affiliated with a broker. Here's what that entails: -->
Choosing an **active status** means being ready to practice real estate, affiliated with a broker. This entails:

- **Issuance and Display**: An active license is issued not directly to the licensee but to the **employing broker**, who is then responsible for making this license available for public viewing at the office. The physical license is not for the licensee to keep on their desk.

- **Ownership**: Despite whose name is on it, the license is the property of the **Board**. If leaving a firm, switching to inactive status, or facing revocation of the license, it must be returned to the Board. This reinforces the concept that licenses are part of a broader regulatory ecosystem.

### Inactive Status
<!-- On the other hand, if you're not ready or able to affiliate with a broker immediately, you might opt for an **inactive status**. Here are the key considerations: -->
If not ready or able to affiliate with a broker immediately, applicants might opt for an **inactive status**. Here are the key considerations:

- **Restrictions**: Holding an inactive license means being on the bench. Licensees **cannot perform any activities** that would normally require an active real estate license. It's a pause on real estate practice.

- **Time-Sensitive**: This status isn't indefinite. Licensees have a **three-year window** to switch to an active status before needing to re-qualify, which includes retaking **pre-license education** and the **exam**. However, exceptions are made, notably for those with related experience or **military members and their spouses**, who may receive waivers at the Board's discretion.

<!-- Understanding these paths and their implications is vital as you embark on your journey in real estate. Whether you're raring to go with an active license or need time to align your circumstances with an inactive one, making an informed choice will set the course of your career. -->
These paths and their implications apply to real estate licensing. Whether choosing an active license or needing time with an inactive one, making an informed choice determines licensing status.
