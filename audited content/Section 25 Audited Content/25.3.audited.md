# Enforcement Actions in Virginia Real Estate

<!-- When you're navigating the complexities of the real estate profession, understanding how **enforcement actions** are handled is crucial. If there's an alleged **violation**, there are specific steps for reporting and addressing these issues, which are essential for maintaining integrity and accountability in the real estate industry. -->
When an alleged **violation** occurs, specific steps exist for reporting and addressing these issues to maintain integrity and accountability in the real estate industry.

## Filing a Complaint

- If someone believes a violation has occurred, a limited time exists to file a complaint:
  - With the **Real Estate Board** within **one year** of the incident.
  - Alternatively, both the individual affected and the **Virginia attorney general** can take the complaint to a **Virginia State court** within **two years**.

## Consequences of a Validated Complaint

If a complaint is validated, the consequences for the violator are serious and varied:

- **Fines:**
  - First-time violation: up to **$2,500**.
  - Subsequent violations: up to **$2,500 per violation**.
  - These monetary penalties serve as a deterrent.

- **Injunctive Relief:**
  - The court may order the violator to do or stop doing something to directly address and remedy the conduct that led to the violation.

- **Attorney Fees:**
  - The violator may be required to cover attorney fees, adding another layer of financial responsibility.

- **Punitive Damages:**
  - Additional funds awarded as further punishment.

## Disciplinary Actions by the Real Estate Board

<!-- The **Real Estate Board** plays a key role in disciplining licensees based on the severity of the violation: -->
The **Real Estate Board** disciplines licensees based on the severity of the violation:

- Imposing fines.
- Suspending the violator's license.
- Revoking the license entirely.
