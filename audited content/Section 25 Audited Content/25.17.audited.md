# Maintaining and Managing Financial Records in Real Estate

<!-- Let's dive into the essential topic of maintaining and managing financial records in the realm of real estate, as dictated by certain regulations. Understanding this is crucial for anyone engaging in real estate transactions, ensuring accountability and transparency. -->
Maintaining and managing financial records in real estate follows specific regulations. These practices ensure accountability and transparency in real estate transactions.

## Location and Content of Financial Records

- A complete record of all financial transactions executed under the authority of the **principal broker's license** must be kept either at the main office or a designated branch office.
- If the principal office is outside the state and there is a branch within the state, identical records must also be available in the in-state office.
- These records should detail:
  - The **source of funds**
  - The **date funds were received**
  - Where and when the funds were **deposited**
  - Upon completion of the transaction, what ultimately happened to those funds

## Responsibilities of the Principal Broker

- The **principal broker** must maintain a record-keeping system that clearly and precisely demonstrates adherence to these guidelines.
- The **accounting records** should be detailed enough to easily assess compliance with these rules.

## Improper Financial Record-Keeping Practices

The following practices are considered improper and violate the responsibilities of a principal or supervising broker:

1. **Failure to retain brokerage agreements and disclosures:**
   - Not keeping, for **three years post-execution**, every brokerage agreement, disclosures and consents concerning **dual agency or representation**, and disclosures of a brokerage relationship to an unrepresented party.
   - These documents ensure all parties are fully informed throughout the transaction.

2. **Failure to store critical transaction documents:**
   - Not storing, for **three years from the closing date** or from the date a transaction is ratified if it does not close, all critical documents including:
     - Executed contracts of sale
     - Releases from contracts
     - Executed lease agreements
     - Property management agreements
     - Settlement statements related to the real estate transaction
   - All documents must be complete, legible, and within the broker's control or possession unless legally restricted.

3. **Neglecting accurate accounting of funds:**
   - Failing to keep a comprehensive and accurate account of **receipts and disbursements** when holding money on behalf of others.
   - This must be maintained for **three years following** the closure or termination of the sales transaction, lease termination, or the conclusion of the licensee's role in the lease.
   - This ensures all financial dealings are transparent and traceable.

4. **Failure to maintain required records:**
   - Not maintaining any records as required by these guidelines for the full **three-year period** is considered improper practice.
