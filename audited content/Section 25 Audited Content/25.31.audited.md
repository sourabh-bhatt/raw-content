# Advertising Regulations for Real Estate Agents

Real estate agents engaging in **advertising** must understand and adhere to specific regulations that guide property promotion and representation. These rules ensure promotional efforts are consistent, transparent, and properly supervised.

## Definition of Advertising

**Advertising** in this context encompasses all forms of communication used to promote, represent, or solicit for any activity related to licensed real estate. This includes, but is not limited to:

- Digital media
- Printed materials
- Direct communication methods

## Contact Information

**Contact information** refers to telephone numbers or web addresses, which are contact points between agents and potential clients.

## Disclosure Requirements

A critical component of advertising in the digital age is **disclosure**. For electronic media, such as websites or social media, disclosure must include key information about the firm or the licensee that is easily accessibleâ€”either on the main page or just one click away. This information must highlight:

- The licensed name and location of the firm, or
- If it's personal advertising, the agent's name, the firm's name, and business location.

For non-electronic advertising, the emphasis is on including the relevant licensed names.

## Supervision and Firm Identification

All advertising efforts must be directly supervised by the **principal or supervising broker** and should prominently feature the firm's licensed name, ensuring that professional affiliations are clear and visible.

## Electronic Media Advertising Rules

Specific rules apply to electronic media advertising:

1. All electronic advertisements must fulfill chapter provisions, ensuring consistency and reliability in listing presentation.
2. Disclosure information must be easily accessible in these advertisements, ensuring transparency and integrity.
3. Agents are responsible for keeping electronic listings updated and accurate, reflecting any material changes to the property status or description as soon as possible. This applies whether the agent directly controls the listing's online presentation or if modifications need to be communicated to a third-party service.

## Non-Electronic Advertising Requirements

Other forms of advertising, such as physical for-sale signs and business cards, have specific requirements:

- Must include, at a minimum, the firm's name and contact information.
- Ensure prospective clients have a clear path to inquire further about the property.

## Prohibited Advertising Practices

Several actions are explicitly prohibited in advertising practices, including:

- Implying ownership or licensure inaccurately.
- Failing to disclose real estate licensee ownership in advertised properties.
- Omitting the firm's licensed name.
- Advertising without explicit consent.
- Vague advertising that fails to mention the services being provided.
