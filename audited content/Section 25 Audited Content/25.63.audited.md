# Disclosure Obligations When Involved in Real Estate Transactions

<!-- When you buy, sell, or lease real property and you, a family member, your firm, or an entity you own has a stake in the property, you have a specific obligation to disclose this involvement clearly and in writing to the other parties involved in the transaction. -->
When buying, selling, or leasing real property where licensees, family members, their firms, or entities they own have a stake in the property, there is a specific obligation to disclose this involvement clearly and in writing to the other parties involved in the transaction.

## When Disclosure Is Required

- If licensees, family members, their firms, or entities they own are buying, selling, or leasing real estate, this involvement must be disclosed.
- Disclosure must occur **right from the start**, specifically when first discussing a specific property.

## What Must Be Disclosed

- Licensees must clearly state in writing that they are **real estate agents**.
- Licensees must disclose any **ownership interest** that they, family members, their firms, or other entities they own have in the property.

## Purpose of Disclosure

- Virginia law requires this disclosure.
- It is designed to prevent **conflicts of interest** and ensure all parties are fully informed.
- Transparency encourages **fairness and integrity** in real estate transactions.

## Compliance Requirements

- Proper disclosure protects professional reputation.
- It upholds the **trust clients place in the real estate profession**.

Example:

If you are a real estate agent and your firm owns a property you are helping to sell, you must disclose this fact in writing to the buyer and seller before proceeding with negotiations. This ensures everyone is aware of your interest and can make informed decisions.
