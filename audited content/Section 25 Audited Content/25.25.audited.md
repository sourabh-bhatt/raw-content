# Standards of Conduct in Virginia Real Estate

<!-- As you delve into the realm of real estate, it's crucial to grasp the **Standards of Conduct** set forth by the **Virginia Real Estate Board**. These standards ensure your actions align with the industry's expectations. Let's unpack these guidelines together, focusing on what they mean for you and your practice. -->
The **Standards of Conduct** set forth by the **Virginia Real Estate Board** ensure licensee actions align with industry expectations. These guidelines apply to all real estate practice.

## Regulatory Authority and Discipline

Licensee conduct, both as an individual and within a firm, is subject to scrutiny. The Virginia Real Estate Board can discipline licensees through:

- Reprimand
- Monetary penalty (fine)
- Probation
- Suspension
- Revocation

This disciplinary power applies to everyone, regardless of whether their license status is active or inactive.

## Firm Conduct

<!-- A foundational aspect of these standards is how your firm operates in relation to licensing. -->
These standards address how firms operate in relation to licensing. Points include:

- All real estate firms must obtain and maintain both **business licenses** and **individual licenses**.
- This applies to both **salespersons** and **brokers**.
- Maintaining proper licensing ensures that licensees and their firms are fully authorized to engage in real estate transactions.
- Proper licensing helps maintain professional standing in the industry.

## Disclosures

<!-- Transparency is key in real estate transactions. -->
Transparency is required in real estate transactions. Licensees, whether salespersons or brokers, are mandated to make necessary disclosures in specific situations.

### Disclosure of Interest

One area of disclosure involves any personal or familial interest licensees might have in a real estate transaction. The rules are:

- If a licensee, family member, or their firm have any ownership or interest in the property involved in a transaction, this must be fully disclosed to all parties.
- This disclosure ensures fairness and transparency.
- It helps prevent conflicts of interest or perceptions of bias in dealings.
