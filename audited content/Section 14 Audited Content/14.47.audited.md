# Understanding Principal Lending Agreements and Interest Calculations

<!-- When you're diving into the world of real estate financing, it's crucial to understand how **interest calculations** and **payments** work within **principal lending agreements**. Let's break down the principal options that you'll encounter. -->
**Interest calculations** and **payments** work within **principal lending agreements** in real estate financing. The following are the principal options available.

## Types of Loan Agreements

<!-- There are different kinds of agreements that dictate how interest on a loan is calculated and how payments towards the loan principal are made. Navigating these agreements is fundamental to both advising clients accurately and understanding the financial implications of various loan options. -->
Different agreements dictate how interest on a loan is calculated and how payments towards the loan principal are made. These agreements affect advising clients and understanding the financial implications of various loan options.

### 1. Fixed-Rate Mortgages (FRMs)
- The **interest rate remains constant** throughout the term of the loan.
- Monthly payments for interest and principal remain unchanged.
- This stability makes budgeting easier for borrowers.

### 2. Adjustable-Rate Mortgages (ARMs)
- Interest rates can **fluctuate over the loan's term**.
- Rate changes are usually tied to a specific **financial index**, plus a set margin.
- Initial rates may be lower than FRMs, but there is a risk of increase over time.
- <!-- Understanding how rate adjustments work is important as they can significantly impact monthly payments and overall loan costs. -->
- Rate adjustments can impact monthly payments and overall loan costs.

### 3. Interest-Only Loans
- Borrowers pay only the **interest** for a specified period.
- **Principal payments are postponed**, resulting in lower initial payments.
- After the interest-only period ends, monthly payments increase substantially as the borrower begins to pay down the principal.

### 4. Negative Amortization Loans
- Borrowers may make payments **less than the interest owed**, causing the loan balance to increase over time.
- This is a risky option because borrowers can end up owing more than the original loan amount.

### 5. Balloon Mortgages
- Borrowers make regular, smaller payments for a set period.
- Followed by a single, large payment (the **"balloon" payment**) to pay off the remaining loan balance.
- This can be risky if borrowers are not prepared for the large sum when it is due.
