# Understanding Down Payments in Real Estate Financing

<!-- When you're exploring real estate financing, it's crucial to understand the concept of **down payments**. Real estate financing includes the concept of **down payments**. A down payment represents the portion of the property's **purchase price** that must be paid upfront in cash. This element influences loan conditions and requirements when securing a **mortgage**.

## Conventional Loans and Down Payments

<!-- If you opt for a **conventional loan**, which is not backed by any government program, be prepared to pay a higher down payment compared to loans that are government-supported. Typically, conventional lenders expect you to make a down payment of at least **20%** of the home's purchase price. 

- For example, for a property valued at $100,000, you would need to have $20,000 ready as a down payment. -->
A **conventional loan**, which is not backed by any government program, requires a higher down payment compared to government-supported loans. Typically, conventional lenders expect a down payment of at least **20%** of the home's purchase price.

Example: For a property valued at $100,000, the required down payment would be $20,000.

## The 20% Down Payment Threshold

- Staying at or below an **80% LTV** is important because it exempts you from needing **private mortgage insurance (PMI)**.
- **PMI** is an extra cost levied on borrowers who put down less than 20%, designed to protect the lender in case you default on the loan. -->
The 20% figure relates to the **loan-to-value ratio (LTV)**. Putting down 20% creates an LTV of 80%, which represents a threshold for lenders.

- Staying at or below an **80% LTV** exempts borrowers from needing **private mortgage insurance (PMI)**.
- **PMI** is an extra cost levied on borrowers who put down less than 20%, designed to protect the lender in case of default.

By understanding and planning for your down payment, you can save on additional costs and secure more favorable loan terms.
