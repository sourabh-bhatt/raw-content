# Understanding Fannie Mae and Its Role in Real Estate Financing

<!-- When exploring the realm of real estate financing, it's essential to understand the role of **Fannie Mae**, also known as the **Federal National Mortgage Association (FNMA)**. -->
**Fannie Mae**, also known as the **Federal National Mortgage Association (FNMA)**, plays a major role in real estate financing.

## Background of Fannie Mae

- Initially established as a federal agency, Fannie Mae was created to purchase **FHA-insured loans**.
- Although it once traded publicly on the New York Stock Exchange, its shares are no longer traded there.

## Fannie Mae's Role in the Mortgage Market

- Fannie Mae does *not* extend loans directly to borrowers.
- Its primary function is in the **secondary mortgage market**, where it:
  - Buys and sells mortgages insured by the **Federal Housing Administration (FHA)**.
  - Handles mortgages guaranteed by the **Department of Veterans Affairs (VA)**.
  - Deals with **conventional mortgages**.

- Beyond buying and selling mortgages, Fannie Mae supports the mortgage market by issuing and selling:
  - **Mortgage-backed securities**
  - **Mortgage-backed bonds**
  - **Discount notes**

## Fannie Mae in Market Liquidity

Fannie Mae's operations maintain liquidity in the mortgage industry:

- When mortgage funds are scarce, Fannie Mae purchases mortgages, injecting capital into the system.
- When there is an abundance of lending capital, it sells mortgages.

This dual role helps stabilize the mortgage market, ensuring that funds remain available for borrowers.
