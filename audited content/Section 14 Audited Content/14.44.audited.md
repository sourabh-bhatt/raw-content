# Understanding the Loan Estimate in Real Estate Financing

<!-- When you're diving into the world of real estate financing, understanding the **Loan Estimate** is crucial. -->
The **Loan Estimate** is a document in real estate financing. This document stems from requirements set by **RESPA** (*Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act*) and **TILA** (*Truth in Lending Act*). <!-- These laws mandate that lenders or mortgage brokers must give you an early heads-up on the **settlement service charges** you're likely to encounter. -->
These laws mandate that lenders or mortgage brokers must provide borrowers with information about **settlement service charges** they are likely to encounter.

## Timing and Delivery of the Loan Estimate

- <!-- If you apply for a loan and don't receive a Loan Estimate immediately, the lender or mortgage broker has a **three-business-day window** to either mail it to you or deliver it directly. -->
If borrowers apply for a loan and do not receive a Loan Estimate immediately, the lender or mortgage broker has a **three-business-day window** to mail it or deliver it directly.
- The numbers on this document are **estimates**, not final amounts. Actual costs may vary due to changing market conditions.

## Fees Covered by the Loan Estimate

<!-- The Loan Estimate details various fees you should expect, including: -->
The Loan Estimate details various fees borrowers should expect, including:

- **Loan fees**: Costs directly associated with securing the loan.
- **Fees paid in advance**: Such as interest.
- **Reserves**: Funds set aside for property taxes and homeowner's insurance to ensure these bills are covered.
- **Government charges**: Including transfer and recording taxes.
- **Additional fees**: Specific to the loan or property situation.
