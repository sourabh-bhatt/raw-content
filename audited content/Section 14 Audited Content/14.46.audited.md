# VA Guaranteed Home Loan: Financing Options for Veterans

<!-- Understanding different financing options is important when entering real estate. -->
The **VA Guaranteed Home Loan**, or simply, the **VA loan**, is a financing option in real estate. This loan is guaranteed by the **Department of Veterans Affairs (VA)**. It helps eligible **veterans** buy, build, or refinance homes without a large down payment or mortgage insurance.

## Background and Purpose

- Introduced through the **Servicemen's Readjustment Act of 1944**, the VA loan system typically does not provide the loan directly.
- Instead, it acts as a **guarantor** for loans given to veterans by approved lenders.
- The only exception is in rare situations where qualified veterans cannot find mortgage funding locally; in these cases, the VA may directly lend money.

## Role of Primary Market Lenders

- **Primary market lenders** review and approve veterans and the properties being mortgaged, following VA guidelines.
- They decide on loan terms such as **fixed** or **adjustable rates**.
- If a veteran cannot repay the mortgage, the VA covers a portion of the loan, minimizing the lender's risk.

## Benefits of VA Loans

- Allow for **little to no down payment**.
- Do **not require mortgage insurance**, reducing overall borrowing costs.
- Lower the entry barrier to homeownership for veterans.

## VA Loan Structure and Entitlement

- The VA guarantee is capped by a **basic entitlement** - up to **$36,000**.
- There is an **extended entitlement** for larger loans.
- This entitlement defines how much the VA will cover if the loan defaults.
- The actual loan amount is determined by lenders, who may lend up to **four times the veteran's entitlement** without a down payment, provided the loan does not exceed the property's appraised value.

## Eligibility Requirements

- Veterans must have served a minimum duration in the military, depending on wartime or peacetime service.
- A **Certificate of Eligibility (COE)** is required to demonstrate entitlement to the VA loan benefit, though it does not guarantee loan approval.
- The property must pass an **appraisal** to confirm its value aligns with the loan amount.

## Closing Costs and Funding Fee

- Closing costs are limited and can include various fees, but generally exclude the origination charge or the loan amount itself, except in refinancing cases.
- A **funding fee** is charged on every VA loan to fund the program.
- The funding fee percentage varies based on factors such as whether it is the veteran's first VA loan and the size of any down payment.

## VA Direct Lending in Rare Cases

- In rare circumstances where no local financing is available, the VA can directly fund loans.
- This safety net ensures eligible veterans have access to home financing even in challenging markets.
