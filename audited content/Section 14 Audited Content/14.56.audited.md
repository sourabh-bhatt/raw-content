# FHA Loans: An Overview of Federal Housing Administration Financing

<!-- When you step into the world of real estate financing, one term you'll often encounter is **FHA loans**. -->
**FHA loans** are a term in real estate financing. These loans are a product of the **Federal Housing Administration (FHA)**, which falls under the Department of Housing and Urban Development. FHA provides **mortgage insurance** on loans from **FHA-approved lenders** across the United States and its territories. 

<!-- It's crucial to grasp that while FHA insures these mortgages â€“ covering **single-family homes, multi-family homes, manufactured homes, and even hospitals** â€“ it doesn't directly offer the loans. -->
While FHA insures these mortgages - covering **single-family homes, multi-family homes, manufactured homes, and even hospitals** - it doesn't directly offer the loans. This insurance makes lending to certain borrowers less risky for lenders by reducing their chance of facing a mortgage default.

## Nature of FHA Loans

- **Lenders in the primary market** qualify both borrowers and properties under FHA standards, which are less stringent than those for conventional loans.
- FHA allows for various types of mortgage agreements, including both **fixed rate** and **adjustable rate mortgages**.
- Borrowers are responsible for **FHA mortgage insurance payments**. In the event of default, lenders can claim against this insurance to recoup losses.

## Benefits of FHA Loans

- **Down payment requirement:** Borrowers can put down as little as **3.5%**, enabling financing of about **97%** of a home's purchase value.

## FHA Mortgage Insurance Premiums

All FHA borrowers must purchase FHA mortgage insurance, which includes:

- **Upfront premium:** Typically **1.75%** of the loan amount, due at closing but can be financed into the loan.
- **Monthly premiums:** Added to the mortgage payment, calculated based on the base loan amount, loan term, and **loan-to-value (LTV) ratio**. Rates range from **0.45% to 1.05%**.

### Termination of Premiums

- For loans with an **LTV over 90%**, premiums are collected for the entire loan term.
- For loans with an **LTV of 90% or less**, premiums stop after **11 years** or at the loan term's end, whichever comes first.

## Qualifying for an FHA Loan

- No minimum income requirement, but a **steady income history over the last two years** is expected.
- Various income sources are accepted, including part-time and bonus pay, provided they are consistent.
- FHA debt-to-income ratio limits:
  - **31%** for housing costs
  - **43%** for housing expenses plus other long-term debts
- These limits are lenient than conventional loans, and exceeding them may be possible with compensatory factors.
- **Credit score flexibility:** FHA criteria are forgiving than conventional loans.
- Lenders must use **FHA-approved appraisers** to assess property value and condition.

## Loan Limits and Closing Costs

- **Maximum loan amounts** insured by FHA vary based on local cost of living and average home prices.
- FHA specifies allowable **closing costs** to ensure charges are reasonable and customary.
