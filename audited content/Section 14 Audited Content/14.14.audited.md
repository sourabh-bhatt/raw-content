Real estate financing includes the key term **escrow account**. Understanding how it operates and the legal requirements around it, especially the rules under the **Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA)**, applies to financing knowledge.

## What is an Escrow Account?

<!-- An escrow account is essentially a holding tank used by your lender to accumulate reserves for certain property-related expenses like **taxes** and **insurance**. -->
An escrow account is essentially a holding tank used by lenders to accumulate reserves for certain property-related expenses like **taxes** and **insurance**.

- Lenders cannot require more than **one-sixth of the estimated annual disbursements (equivalent to two months)** as a cushion. This limit prevents excessive reserves and ensures fairness to homeowners.

- RESPA mandates clear communication regarding escrow accounts:
  - Within **60 days after establishment of the escrow account** for property purchase, lenders must provide a **detailed escrow account statement**.
  - After this initial statement, homeowners receive an **annual escrow statement** for account status information.

## Escrow Account Applications

Knowledge of how escrow accounts operate and the associated legal requirements:

- Ensures homeowners are well-informed about fund allocation.
- Protects against overcharging for reserves.
- Enables navigation of real estate financing complexities.

Example: If your annual property tax is $1,200, your lender will collect $100 per month into your escrow account. However, they cannot require you to keep more than $200 (one-sixth of annual amount) as a cushion in the account.
