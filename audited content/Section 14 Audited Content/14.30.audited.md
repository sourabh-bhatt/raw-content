# Applying for a Mortgage Loan: Understanding the Uniform Residential Loan Application (FNMA 1003 Form)

The mortgage application process centers on the **Uniform Residential Loan Application**, or **FNMA 1003 Form**. This form requires detailed information about several key areas of financial life. The following sections outline the necessary preparation:

## Key Sections of the Loan Application

- **Employment and Salary History**: Outline where work occurs, the nature of the job, and earnings amount. This information helps lenders assess income stability and ability to repay the loan.

- **Monthly Income**: Detail all sources of income received in a typical month, including job and any additional income sources.

- **Assets and Liabilities**:
  - **Assets**: List owned items, such as savings accounts, stocks, or real estate.
  - **Liabilities**: List owed amounts, including car loans, student debt, or other obligations.
  This section helps lenders evaluate net worth.

- **Authorization for Verification**: By signing the application, borrowers give the lender permission to verify all the information provided. This includes checking employment status, income, debts, and more to ensure accuracy.

- **Intention of Occupancy**: State whether the borrower plans to live in the property being purchased. This affects interest rates and loan terms, as **primary residences** often qualify for better conditions than **investment properties**.

## Supporting Documents Required

Alongside the application form, you'll need to submit several documents to support your claims, including:

- **W-2 Forms and Paycheck Stubs**: Confirm your employment and income.
- **Purchase Contract**: Outlines the terms of the property sale.
- **Tax Returns**: Provide a comprehensive look at your financial history.
- **Benefits or Retirement Income Statements**: Required if any income comes from benefits or retirement.
- **Bank Statements**: Offer a snapshot of your current financial health.

## Important Considerations

It's vital to be honest in every part of your loan application. Providing false information is not only detrimental to your loan prospects but also a **federal offense**. When guiding your clients through this process, emphasize the importance of accuracy and honesty. This detailed information helps lenders make informed decisions about loan approvals and terms, directly impacting your client's buying capabilities.
