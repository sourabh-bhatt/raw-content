# Lot, Block, and Subdivision Method (Recorded Plat Method)

<!-- When you're learning to describe property legally, you'll come across what's known as the **Lot, Block, and Subdivision method**, or more commonly, the **Recorded Plat method**. This method plays a crucial role in understanding how large tracts of land, called **subdivision plats**, are divided and noted for legal and real estate purposes. -->

The **Lot, Block, and Subdivision method**, commonly called the **Recorded Plat method**, describes how large tracts of land, called **subdivision plats**, are divided and noted for legal and real estate purposes.

## Understanding Subdivision Plats

- A **subdivision plat** starts as a large piece of land. This land is initially located and described by other methods like **metes and bounds** or the **government survey system**.
<!-- - Imagine a subdivision plat as a detailed map that outlines the layout, showing the positioning of individual **lots** and their corresponding numbers. -->
- A subdivision plat functions as a detailed map that outlines the layout, showing the positioning of individual **lots** and their corresponding numbers.

## Blocks and Lots

- Once a subdivision plat is planned, it is broken down into **blocks** and **lots**.
- A **block** is a collection of contiguous lots, generally separated by access roads.
- A **lot** represents the smallest unit of land intended to be sold or conveyed as a whole within this plat.

## Approval and Recording Process

- Before subdivision plats can become official, they require approval from the local planning commission or governing body to ensure compliance with local regulations.
- For a plat to be recognized legally, it must be **recorded**. This involves:
  - Obtaining a **book and page reference number**.
  - Ensuring the plat books are accessible for **public inspection**.
- These recorded details form the **legal description** of the property.

## Legal Description of Parcels Within Recorded Plats

- When dealing with a parcel within a recorded plat, the legal description includes:
  - The **lot and block number**
  - The **tract name**
  - Location details such as **map book reference**, **county**, and **state**

## Recording Requirements

<!-- - For subdivision plats to have any legal standing, they must be recorded with a public authority, typically the Circuit Court Clerk's office of the area where the property is located. -->

- Subdivision plats must be recorded with a public authority, typically the Circuit Court Clerk's office of the area where the property is located, to have legal standing.

## Changes and Further Subdivision

- Subdivisions can evolve over time. They might be further subdivided or altered after the initial plat is recorded.
- Such changes require the creation and recording of new or partial plats.
- The legal description may need to include references to all relevant plats to reflect the parcel's division history.
