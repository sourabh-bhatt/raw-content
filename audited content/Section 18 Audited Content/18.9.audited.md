# Understanding Title Records and Recording Acts

## Recording Acts

*Recording Acts* are state laws that specify the procedures for recording title records. These laws define:

- What documents must be recorded
- How documents should be filed
- Where the filing takes place
- The priority (pecking order) for payment among competing interests

## Title Records

<!-- *Title records* are documents that significantly affect an owner's title, interest, or rights in real estate. These include: -->

*Title records* are documents that affect an owner's title, interest, or rights in real estate. These include:

- **Deeds**
- **Mortgages**
- **Deeds of trust**
- **Liens**
- **Easements**

These documents form the legal framework of property ownership and rights.

## Location of Records

Title records are typically maintained at the local level in Virginia at the circuit court clerk's office in each city or county where the property is located. This local maintenance ensures that records are accessible to:

- Property owners
- Potential buyers
- The general public

<!-- This accessibility facilitates the discovery of important information about a property. -->

This accessibility facilitates the discovery of property information.

## Constructive Notice

<!-- One of the most important concepts in real estate is *constructive notice*. By properly recording property encumbrances according to the Recording Act, you provide legal notice to all interested parties, whether or not they actually check the public records. This means: -->

*Constructive notice* functions in real estate through proper recording of property encumbrances according to the Recording Act. This provides legal notice to all interested parties, whether or not they actually check the public records. This means:

- Everyone is legally informed about any claims or encumbrances on a property
- It acts as a defense mechanism in property transactions

<!-- For example, if a lien is recorded on a property, any future buyer is considered to have notice of that lien, even if they do not personally review the records. -->

Example: If a lien is recorded on a property, any future buyer is considered to have notice of that lien, even if they do not personally review the records.
