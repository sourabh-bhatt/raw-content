# Recording Deeds: Function and Implications

<!-- When you obtain a **deed** to a property, it is valid from the moment you receive it, even if you do not record it. However, choosing *not* to record your deed leaves you vulnerable to competing claims on the property from third parties. -->

A **deed** to property is valid from the moment it is received, even if not recorded. However, failing to record a deed leaves owners vulnerable to competing claims on the property from third parties.

## The Purpose of Recording a Deed

<!-- Recording your deed is not merely a procedural step; it creates a **public record** that includes all transactions and any claims against the property, such as **loans** or **liens**. This public record serves to verify that the **property title** is clear and **marketable**. -->

Recording a deed creates a **public record** that includes all transactions and claims against the property, such as **loans** or **liens**. This public record serves to verify that the **property title** is clear and **marketable**.

## Functions of Recording Deeds

Recording the deed in the public registry serves two main purposes:

1. **Protection through Constructive Notice**
   <!-- - Recording provides *constructive notice* of your ownership. This means that everyone is legally considered aware that you are the rightful owner, even if they have not personally checked the records. -->
   - Recording provides *constructive notice* of ownership. This means that everyone is legally considered aware of rightful ownership, even if they have not personally checked the records.
   <!-- - This status is crucial in defending your claim against anyone else who might later assert ownership. -->
   - This status supports defending ownership claims against others who might later assert ownership.

2. **Protection of Financial Interests and Priority**
   - Recording protects others with a financial interest in the property, such as creditors.
   - It establishes a clear order of priority, ensuring that in the event of a dispute, there is a transparent system to determine whose claim comes first.

## Risks of Not Recording

- Interests in a property that are not recorded may become secondary to someone else's recorded interest.
- This can lead to complications or even loss of claim.

<!-- Therefore, recording your interest in a property is essential not only to assert your ownership but also to maintain the integrity of the property's title for all parties involved. -->

Recording property interests supports ownership assertion and maintains the integrity of property titles for all parties involved.
