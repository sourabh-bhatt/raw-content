# Proprietary Lease in Cooperative Ownership

<!-- Imagine you're entering into a unique type of rental agreement known as a **proprietary lease**. This is not your typical landlord-tenant situation. Example: A unique type of rental agreement is the **proprietary lease**. This is not a typical landlord-tenant situation. Instead, the tenant is also a **stockholder** in the **corporation** that owns the building. This is a common setup in **cooperative ownership** scenarios.

## How a Proprietary Lease Works

<!-- - Instead of paying a fixed rent each month, you are responsible for covering a proportionate share of the **carrying charges** for the corporation. -->
- Instead of paying a fixed rent each month, tenants are responsible for covering a proportionate share of the **carrying charges** for the corporation.
- These charges may include:
  - **Maintenance fees**
  - **Property taxes**
  - **Building insurance**

<!-- Think of it as contributing to the overall expenses based on your stake in the corporation. -->
This involves contributing to the overall expenses based on the tenant's stake in the corporation.

## Selling Your Cooperative Unit

<!-- When you decide to sell your cooperative unit:

- The **proprietary lease**, along with your **shares** in the corporation, gets passed on to the buyer. -->
When selling a cooperative unit:

- The **proprietary lease**, along with **shares** in the corporation, gets passed on to the buyer.
- This seamless transition is one of the distinctive features of this leasing model.
