# Eviction and Legal Remedies for Lease Breaches

<!-- When you're dealing with a scenario where a **tenant has materially breached the lease contract**, for instance by failing to pay rent, you, as the **landlord**, have the right to seek a **court order** to remove the tenant. This process is known as **eviction**. -->
When a **tenant has materially breached the lease contract**, such as by failing to pay rent, the **landlord** has the right to seek a **court order** to remove the tenant. This process is known as **eviction**.

## Virginia Eviction Process

In Virginia, landlords must follow specific procedures under the **Virginia Residential Landlord and Tenant Act (VRLTA)**:

<!-- - For **non-payment of rent**, you must give the tenant a **5-day pay or quit notice**

- For **non-payment of rent**, landlords must give the tenant a **5-day pay or quit notice**
- For other lease violations, landlords must give a **30-day notice to quit** (or shorter period if specified in lease)
- After the notice period expires, landlords can file an **unlawful detainer action** in court
- Only a **sheriff** can physically remove a tenant after a court order

## Legal Actions Available to Landlords

<!-- - You can **sue the tenant** for the **unpaid rent**.

- Landlords can **sue the tenant** for the **unpaid rent**.
- Landlords may also seek compensation for any other **damages** that have occurred due to the breach.
- Landlords can pursue both eviction and monetary damages in the same court proceeding.
