# Net Leases

## What is a Net Lease?

<!-- In a **net lease**, you're responsible not only for a set **base rent** but also for a portion of the building's **expenses**. These expenses typically include: -->
In a **net lease**, tenants pay a set **base rent** plus a portion of the building's **expenses**. These expenses typically include:

- **Taxes**
- **Insurance**
<!-- - Other **operational costs** specified in your lease agreement -->
- Other **operational costs** specified in the lease agreement

<!-- Your share of these expenses is generally determined by the amount of space you are leasing; essentially, the larger your leased space, the greater your share of these costs. -->
The tenant's share of these expenses is generally determined by the amount of space leased; the larger the leased space, the greater the share of these costs.

## Effective Rent

<!-- The term **"effective rent"** refers to the total amount you pay, which includes your base rent plus your share of the building's expenses. It is important to clearly understand which expenses you are expected to cover in addition to your rent, as this will impact your total monthly payment. -->
The term **"effective rent"** refers to the total amount paid, which includes base rent plus the tenant's share of the building's expenses. The specific expenses covered in addition to rent impact the total monthly payment.

## Common Usage

<!-- Net leases are particularly common in the **commercial** and **industrial real estate** sectors. Understanding how net leases work is crucial because they directly affect the total cost of leasing the space. -->
Net leases are common in the **commercial** and **industrial real estate** sectors. Net leases directly affect the total cost of leasing the space.
