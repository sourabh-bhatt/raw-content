# Non-Freehold Estates (Leasehold Estates)

<!-- When diving into the world of real estate, it's crucial to understand the different types of estates, specifically the difference between **freehold** and **non-freehold estates**. Let's focus on **non-freehold estates**, also known as **leasehold estates**, to get you started. -->
**Non-freehold estates**, also known as **leasehold estates**, differ from **freehold estates** in property ownership and duration.

## What is a Non-Freehold Estate?

<!-- Imagine you're entering a partnership where you're granted the right to use a piece of property, but you don't actually own it. This is the essence of a **non-freehold estate**. It allows you the **right of possession** without the **right of ownership**. This type of estate has a **definite duration**, setting it apart from a **freehold estate**, which lasts for an indeterminable length of time. -->
A **non-freehold estate** grants the **right of possession** without the **right of ownership**. This type of estate has a **definite duration**, setting it apart from a **freehold estate**, which lasts for an indeterminable length of time.

## The Landlord-Tenant Relationship

<!-- A common scenario you'll come across in this realm is the **landlord-tenant relationship**. Here, the tenant gets what's called a **leasehold**. This means: -->
In the **landlord-tenant relationship**, the tenant receives a **leasehold**. This means:

- The tenant has the **exclusive right to use or possess the property** for a set period.
- Despite having possession, the tenant **does not own the property**.
- Ownership remains with the landlord, who holds a **reversion estate**, giving them the future right to take back possession.

## Leasehold Estates vs. Freehold Estates

<!-- Another key difference to grasp is how **leasehold estates** diverge from **freehold estates** regarding their classification: -->
**Leasehold estates** differ from **freehold estates** in their classification:

- While all property aspects may initially seem to fall under **real property**, **leaseholds** are actually considered **personal property**.
<!-- - This distinction is important because **leaseholds can still exist even after the property itself is transferred to someone else**. -->
- **Leaseholds can still exist even after the property itself is transferred to someone else**.
