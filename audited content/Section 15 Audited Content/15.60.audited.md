In Virginia real estate, understanding how **agency relationships** can come to an end is necessary. Knowing these details ensures agents carry out their responsibilities professionally and legally. The following breakdown shows how these relationships typically terminate:

## Ways Agency Relationships Terminate

1. **Completion of the Agreement**
   - Once the goals of the agency relationship are fulfilled, the relationship naturally concludes. This is the most straightforward scenario.

2. **Expiration Date**
   - Agency agreements must have a specific expiration date agreed upon by both parties.

3. **Mutual Agreement to Terminate**
   <!-- - Either you or your client can decide at any point to end the relationship.
   - This termination relies on **mutual consent**, meaning both parties agree it's time to part ways. -->
   - Either the agent or client can decide at any point to end the relationship.
   - This termination relies on **mutual consent**, meaning both parties agree it's time to part ways.

4. **Default by a Party**
   - If either party fails to meet the obligations set out in the agreement, this **default** can lead to termination.
   <!-- - It is vital to comply with all terms to avoid such a situation. -->
   - Complying with all terms helps avoid such a situation.

5. **Refusal of Dual Representation**
   <!-- - If dual representation (representing both buyer and seller) is proposed and disclosed, but the client refuses to consent, you may terminate the relationship. -->
   - If dual representation (representing both buyer and seller) is proposed and disclosed, but the client refuses to consent, the agent may terminate the relationship.
   - **Full consent and transparency** are necessary for dual representation.
