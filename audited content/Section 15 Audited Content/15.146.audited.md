# Designated Agency in Real Estate Transactions

<!-- When exploring the realm of real estate transactions, you will encounter the concept of **designated agency**. In real estate transactions, **designated agency** represents an arrangement where a single **broker** is responsible for representing both the buyer and the seller within the same transaction. However, this arrangement involves complexity.

## How Designated Agency Works

- Instead of the broker directly representing both sides, the broker assigns one **salesperson** to act on behalf of Client A (for example, the buyer).
- Another salesperson is assigned to represent Client B (the seller).
- Each of these salespeople, called **designated agents**, serves as the sole representative for their respective client.
- Each designated agent has a duty to look out for their client's best interests above all else.

## Role of the Broker

- Despite having two designated agents, both work under the same broker.
- The broker supervises both designated agents but does not act as a dual agent or represent either party directly.
- The broker maintains a neutral supervisory role while ensuring both designated agents fulfill their duties to their respective clients.

## Differences from Dual Agency and Disclosure Requirements

- Designated agency is different from **dual agency**. In dual agency, the broker represents both parties. In designated agency, the broker represents neither party but supervises the designated agents.

- This complex relationship must be clearly and properly **disclosed** to all parties involved, in accordance with relevant state laws.
- Full disclosure ensures everyone understands who is representing whom, safeguarding the **ethical standards** of the real estate profession.
