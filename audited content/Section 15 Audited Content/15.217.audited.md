# Ensuring Accuracy and Honesty in Property Marketing and Communication

## Accuracy in Marketing Materials
<!-- When it comes to ensuring the accuracy of marketing materials for a property, it's your duty to trust statements taken from sources you believe to be reliable, such as **tax records** or **official government websites**. However, a key part of your role is to **never misrepresent a property** in your advertisements. Remember, all your ads must be **honest and not misleading**, as dictated by **state licensing laws**. -->

When ensuring the accuracy of marketing materials for a property, agents should trust statements taken from sources they believe to be reliable, such as **tax records** or **official government websites**. However, agents must **never misrepresent a property** in their advertisements. All advertisements must be **honest and not misleading**, as dictated by **state licensing laws**.

## Assessing the Property
<!-- While it's not your job to uncover every potential issue with a property, turning a blind eye isn't an option either. You are expected to use your **professional judgment** to spot any obvious problems. For example: -->

While agents are not required to uncover every potential issue with a property, turning a blind eye is not an option. Agents are expected to use their **professional judgment** to spot any obvious problems. For example:

<!-- - You are not required to dig up hidden defects.
- You should be alert to any glaring signs that suggest a problem, such as **water damage** or **cracks in the foundation**. -->

- Agents are not required to dig up hidden defects.
- Agents should be alert to any glaring signs that suggest a problem, such as **water damage** or **cracks in the foundation**.

These **"red flag" issues** can indicate significant problems that require further investigation with the seller regarding potential repair or **disclosure**.

## Responding to Buyer Inquiries
Honesty is paramount when responding to inquiries from potential buyers. Keep the following in mind:

<!-- - Ensure your responses are grounded in **facts** you are confident about.
- Avoid giving personal interpretations or assessments of the property's condition.
- Distinguish clearly between **facts** and **opinions**. For example, phrases like _"this is a minor crack"_ or _"the uneven floor is just natural settling"_ can be misconstrued as factual statements and potentially mislead the buyer. -->

- Ensure responses are grounded in **facts** agents are confident about.
- Avoid giving personal interpretations or assessments of the property's condition.
- Distinguish clearly between **facts** and **opinions**. **Example:** Phrases like _"this is a minor crack"_ or _"the uneven floor is just natural settling"_ can be misconstrued as factual statements and potentially mislead the buyer.

For questions outside your expertise, such as **zoning laws** or **property boundaries**:

<!-- - Guide the inquirer to a credible source like a **surveyor**, **engineer**, or an **official government website**.
- If sharing information the seller has mentioned, always clarify that you have **not personally verified or investigated** the claim. -->

- Guide the inquirer to a credible source like a **surveyor**, **engineer**, or an **official government website**.
- If sharing information the seller has mentioned, always clarify that the agent has **not personally verified or investigated** the claim.

## Maintaining Professional Integrity
<!-- Executing these responsibilities with diligence ensures that you maintain **integrity** and **professionalism** in your role, ultimately benefiting both the buyer and seller in the real estate process. -->

Executing these responsibilities with diligence ensures agents maintain **integrity** and **professionalism** in their role, ultimately benefiting both the buyer and seller in the real estate process.
