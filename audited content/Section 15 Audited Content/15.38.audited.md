# Seller's Disclosure and Liability in Real Estate Transactions

<!-- ## Importance of Accurate Seller's Disclosure -->
## Accurate Seller's Disclosure

<!-- When dealing with a situation where a **Seller's Disclosure** inaccurately presents the condition of a property, it's crucial to understand the implications. When a **Seller's Disclosure** inaccurately presents the condition of a property, understanding the implications is necessary. If the disclosure form contains intentionally false or incomplete statements, the seller may face **liability** for any damages or loss the buyer suffers due to this misguidance.

Not making certain disclosures could lead to accusations of **fraud** and/or **misrepresentation**.

## Material Information to Disclose

Material information isn't limited to obvious defects; it encompasses:

- Structural issues such as a leaky roof or a flooding basement
- Health risks including:
  - Toxic mold
  - Lead-based paint
  - Radon gas
  - Asbestos
  - Contaminated soil
  - Other environmental dangers

Neglecting to inform buyers about these material factors could result in accusations of fraud or misrepresentation. This can lead to **legal actions** against the agent for damages.

## Impact on Sales Contract

Fraud or misrepresentation can also allow the buyer to nullify the sales contract. These issues can make a contract **void** or **voidable** depending on the situation.

## Responsibilities Beyond the Seller

The seller has the main responsibility for complete disclosure. However, agents cannot rely only on the disclosure forms. If agents are aware of a defect that the seller refuses or neglects to disclose, they might face **disciplinary actions** by the **state licensing authorities** for not disclosing material facts.

This emphasizes the agent's role in the disclosure process. Agents must:

- Ensure that all known defects are communicated
- Navigate these situations with integrity and professionalism

Doing so helps avoid potential legal or disciplinary repercussions.
