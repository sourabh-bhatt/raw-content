# Listing Agreements

<!-- When you enter into **listing agreements**, you're essentially stepping into a special **agency relationship** between the person selling the property (the **principal**) and the **broker** or **agent** (that's potentially you, not just any salesperson). **Listing agreements** create a special **agency relationship** between the person selling the property (the **principal**) and the **broker** or **agent**. These agreements function as **agency employment contracts**. They often give the broker or agent the authority to utilize **subagents** or **salespersons** to help achieve the goal of selling the property.

## Common Types of Listing Agreements

<!-- Let's dive into the common types of listing agreements you'll come across: -->

Common types of listing agreements include:

1. ### Open Listing Agreements
   - This type of agreement allows the seller to employ multiple brokers or agents simultaneously.
   - Only the broker who successfully sells the property earns a commission.
   - If the seller finds a buyer independently, they don't have to pay any commission at all.

2. ### Exclusive Listing Agreements
   - This agreement offers exclusivity to the broker or agent.
   - The broker has the sole right to sell the property during the contract period.
   - Regardless of who finds the buyer, the broker earns the commission.
   - This creates a strong incentive for dedicated effort.

3. ### Net Listing Agreements
   - These are legal in Virginia but discouraged due to ethical concerns.
   - In a net listing agreement, the seller specifies a **net price** they want from the sale of the property.
   - Anything above that amount serves as the commission.
   - This type of agreement poses potential conflicts of interest and risks unfair outcomes for the seller.
   - In Virginia, net listings require full disclosure to the seller about the potential commission amount.
