# Agency and Fiduciary Duties in Real Estate

<!-- When you're navigating the world of real estate as an agent, understanding your responsibilities is paramountâ€”not only to ensure compliance with state laws but also to honor the common law duties that come with your role. Real estate agents must understand their responsibilities to ensure compliance with state laws and honor common law duties. **Agency** or **fiduciary duty** represents one of the most significant responsibilities. This includes the duty to disclose material facts about a property's physical condition.

## Understanding Material Facts and Disclosure

<!-- Imagine you come across a property with a leaky roof or a basement prone to flooding. There may also be health hazards like toxic mold, lead-based paint, or radon gas. Your obligation is to **disclose these material facts** to potential buyers. These are significant defects or hazards that could influence the buyer's decision or affect their health and safety. -->

Example: A property with a leaky roof or a basement prone to flooding may have health hazards like toxic mold, lead-based paint, or radon gas. Agents must **disclose these material facts** to potential buyers. These are significant defects or hazards that could influence the buyer's decision or affect their health and safety.

### What Are Material Facts?

Material facts are not limited to obvious issues. They also include **latent defects** - those not immediately visible or apparent, such as:

- Hidden insect damage
- Structural issues like a cracked foundation

<!-- As an agent, you need to know about these issues or reasonably investigate them. You can do this by suggesting a **property inspection**. -->

Agents must know about these issues or reasonably investigate them through **property inspections**.

## Disclosure Requirements

Failing to disclose material facts can have severe consequences, including:

<!-- - Accusations of **fraud** or **misrepresentation** against you and your seller -->
<!-- - Buyers seeking damages or attempting to void the sales contract upon discovering undisclosed defects -->

- Accusations of **fraud** or **misrepresentation** against the agent and seller
- Buyers seeking damages or attempting to void the sales contract upon discovering undisclosed defects

This can damage reputation and lead to legal problems.

## Practical Considerations

<!-- This doesn't mean you need to report every minor imperfection. For example: -->

Agents need not report every minor imperfection. For example:

- A visible crack in the driveway is *not* a latent defect, as any buyer can see it for themselves.

<!-- Your focus should be on issues that are **hidden** and **significant enough** to impact the buyer's decision or the property's safety and integrity. -->

The focus should be on issues that are **hidden** and **significant enough** to impact the buyer's decision or the property's safety and integrity.
