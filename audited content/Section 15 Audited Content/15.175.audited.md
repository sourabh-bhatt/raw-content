# Duties When Engaged by a Landlord to Lease Property

<!-- When you're engaged by a landlord to lease property, there are specific duties you'll need to fulfill, all aimed at promoting and protecting the landlord's interests while also ensuring fair treatment of prospective tenants. Let's break down these responsibilities so you can understand how to navigate them effectively: -->

When engaged by a landlord to lease property, there are specific duties agents need to fulfill, all aimed at promoting and protecting the landlord's interests while also ensuring fair treatment of prospective tenants. These responsibilities include:

## 1. Adhere to the Brokerage Agreement

<!-- Your first obligation is to act according to the terms set out in the **brokerage agreement** you have with the landlord. This serves as the foundation for your relationship and the services you are to provide. -->

The first obligation is to act according to the terms set out in the **brokerage agreement** with the landlord. This serves as the foundation for the relationship and the services to be provided.

## 2. Promote the Landlord's Interests

<!-- - Conduct marketing activities to attract tenants who are willing to agree to the rental price and terms outlined in the brokerage agreement, or to terms the landlord finds acceptable. However, you're *not required* to seek additional lease offers once the property is already under a lease or a letter of intent to lease, unless the brokerage agreement specifies otherwise. -->
<!-- - Assist in drafting and negotiating leases and letters of intent to lease. You must present *all* written lease offers or counteroffers between the landlord and tenant promptly, even if there's an existing lease or letter of intent. -->
<!-- - Offer reasonable help to finalize the lease agreement, ensuring all parties are on the same page and the process moves smoothly. -->

- Conduct marketing activities to attract tenants who are willing to agree to the rental price and terms outlined in the brokerage agreement, or to terms the landlord finds acceptable. However, agents are *not required* to seek additional lease offers once the property is already under a lease or a letter of intent to lease, unless the brokerage agreement specifies otherwise.
- Assist in drafting and negotiating leases and letters of intent to lease. Agents must present *all* written lease offers or counteroffers between the landlord and tenant promptly, even if there's an existing lease or letter of intent.
- Offer reasonable help to finalize the lease agreement, ensuring all parties are on the same page and the process moves smoothly.

## 3. Maintain Confidentiality

<!-- Protect all personal and financial information shared by the landlord. This also covers any other information they request to be kept confidential unless a law requires disclosure or the landlord gives you written consent to share it. -->

Protect all personal and financial information shared by the landlord. This also covers any other information they request to be kept confidential unless a law requires disclosure or the landlord gives the agent written consent to share it.

## 4. Exercise Care

<!-- Always act with the standard level of care expected in your profession, ensuring diligent and competent service. -->

Always act with the standard level of care expected in the profession, ensuring diligent and competent service.

## 5. Account for Money and Property

<!-- You must transparently and promptly account for all money and property you receive related to your dealings on behalf of the landlord. -->

Agents must transparently and promptly account for all money and property they receive related to their dealings on behalf of the landlord.

## 6. Disclose Material Facts

<!-- Inform the landlord of any important facts about the property or the transaction that you're aware of, which could influence their decisions. -->

Inform the landlord of any material facts about the property or the transaction that could influence their decisions.

## 7. Comply with Laws and Regulations

Follow all relevant laws and regulations, including **fair housing laws** and those that apply to residential real estate transactions.

## Responsibilities Toward Prospective Tenants

<!-- - Honesty is key: you must *not knowingly provide false information*. -->
<!-- - Disclose all known *material adverse facts* concerning the physical condition of the property. For example, if you know of defective drywall, this must be disclosed in writing. -->
<!-- - Ensure disclosures inform the prospective tenant fully but do not breach confidentiality agreements or misrepresent the property's condition beyond your actual knowledge. -->

- Agents must *not knowingly provide false information*.
- Disclose all known *material adverse facts* concerning the physical condition of the property. For example, if agents know of defective drywall, this must be disclosed in writing.
- Ensure disclosures inform the prospective tenant fully but do not breach confidentiality agreements or misrepresent the property's condition beyond actual knowledge.

## Ministerial Acts and Brokerage Relationships

<!-- - Performing **ministerial acts** for a tenant or potential tenant does *not* breach your obligation to the landlord, nor does it establish a brokerage relationship with the tenant, as long as these acts don't conflict with the brokerage agreement with the landlord. -->
<!-- - Showing alternative properties to prospective tenants or representing other landlords is *not* considered a breach of duty toward the landlord you're currently engaged with. -->
<!-- - Always disclose brokerage relationships as required, maintaining transparency and integrity in your professional conduct. -->

- Performing **ministerial acts** for a tenant or potential tenant does *not* breach agent obligations to the landlord, nor does it establish a brokerage relationship with the tenant, as long as these acts don't conflict with the brokerage agreement with the landlord.
- Showing alternative properties to prospective tenants or representing other landlords is *not* considered a breach of duty toward the landlord agents are currently engaged with.
- Always disclose brokerage relationships as required, maintaining transparency and integrity in professional conduct.

By keeping these responsibilities in mind, agents ensure compliance with the law and foster trust and respect with their clients.
