# Termination of Listing Agreements

**Listing agreements** can come to an end in various ways, aside from successfully selling the property.

## Ways a Listing Agreement Can Terminate

1. **Abandonment by the Broker**
   - If the broker doesn't take reasonable steps towards selling the property, this inaction can lead to the termination of the agreement.

2. **Breach**
   - If either party fails to fulfill the promises made in the agreement, this failure can end the contract.

3. **Lapse of Time**
   - Every listing agreement has a set duration.
   - If this time expires without a sale and isn't extended, the agreement naturally terminates.
   - This duration might be determined by what's specified in the contract or by state law.

4. **Mutual Agreement**
   - Both parties may agree to end the agreement early.

5. **Revocation**
   - Either party can revoke the agreement before it ends naturally.

6. **Death, Insanity, or Bankruptcy**
   - These events affecting either the broker or the seller will automatically end the agreement.

7. **Change in Ownership**
   - If ownership of the property changes for any reason other than the planned sale, the contract terminates.

8. **Destruction of the Property**
   - If the property is destroyed, there is no property left to sell, leading to termination.

These endpoints of a listing agreement help agents navigate responsibilities and prepare for changes in brokerage activities.
