# Understanding Agency Relationships

<!-- When you're navigating the world of real estate, it's crucial to understand how an **agency relationship** can be formed not only through explicit agreements but also through less formal means. -->

Professionals must understand how an **agency relationship** can be formed not only through explicit agreements but also through less formal means.

## Implied Agency

<!-- Imagine you and another person start to interact in a way that suggests you're working together, with one of you acting on the other's behalf. Even if you haven't signed anything formally, your actions and words can lead to what's known as an **implied agency**. -->

When two parties interact in a way that suggests they are working together, with one acting on the other's behalf, their actions and words can lead to what is known as an **implied agency**, even without formal documentation.

Implied agency happens when the conduct between two parties shows they intend to create an agency relationship. The parties' behavior demonstrates this intent even without a written agreement.

## Ostensible Agency

**Ostensible agency** (also called apparent agency) is different from implied agency. This occurs when a principal's actions or words lead a third party to reasonably believe that an agency relationship exists.

Example: If a property owner allows someone to show their property and negotiate with buyers, the buyers might reasonably believe that person is the owner's agent. The owner's conduct created the appearance of agency to the third party.

## Potential Risks and Complexities

<!-- One key thing to remember is that these types of relationships can occur accidentally. They might lead to complex situations, such as a **dual agency**, where conflicting duties emerge.

Understanding this can help you navigate potential pitfalls in your interactions. You want to form only the agency relationships you intend to form. This helps you avoid unintended obligations or conflicts of interest. -->

These types of relationships can occur accidentally and might lead to complex situations, such as a **dual agency**, where conflicting duties emerge.

Understanding this helps professionals navigate potential pitfalls in interactions. Professionals should form only the agency relationships they intend to form. This helps avoid unintended obligations or conflicts of interest.
