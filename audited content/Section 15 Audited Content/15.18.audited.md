# Disclosure Responsibilities When Representing a Seller

## Understanding Physical Condition of the Property

<!-- When you're representing a seller as an agent, it's crucial to understand your responsibilities around disclosing information to prospective buyers, especially about the *physical condition of the property*. -->
When representing a seller as an agent, understanding disclosure responsibilities to prospective buyers regarding the *physical condition of the property* is necessary. This term specifically refers to the state of the land and any buildings on it. It does **not** include:

- Anything beyond the property's boundaries
- Issues related to other nearby properties
- Land use regulations by the government
- Matters concerning public streets and highways

## Material Adverse Facts and Disclosure Requirements

<!-- If you become aware of any *material adverse facts* affecting the physical condition of the property, you **must disclose these** to potential buyers as soon as reasonably practicable. -->
If agents become aware of any *material adverse facts* affecting the physical condition of the property, they **must disclose these** to potential buyers as soon as reasonably practicable.

**What counts as a material adverse fact?**

- Any information that would have a significant impact on the property's value or a buyer's decision to purchase it
- Examples include leaks, structural damages, or issues with the property's systems

## Liability and Exceptions

<!-- There are situations where you might **not be held liable** for providing false information: -->
There are situations where agents might **not be held liable** for providing false information:

- If the false information was given by the seller
- If the information was obtained from a governmental body or a certified professional without knowledge of its inaccuracy
- If information is disclosed because the law requires it
