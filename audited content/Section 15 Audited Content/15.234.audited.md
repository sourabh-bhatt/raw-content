# Compensation and Referral Fees in Virginia Real Estate

<!-- When you step into the world of real estate as a licensee, understanding how you get paid is crucial. Let's break it down to ensure you're on the right track. -->

When entering real estate as a licensee, understanding compensation matters.

## Compensation from the Principal Broker

<!-- - Any compensation you expect for your work comes **directly from the principal broker**, also known as the **broker owner**.

- Any compensation licensees expect for their work comes **directly from the principal broker**, also known as the **broker owner**.
- They are the only ones authorized to pay licensees for the deals they close.
- If licensees leave a brokerage but have deals in progress from their time there, their previous broker can still compensate them for commissions earned on those deals once they close.

## Referral Fees: Rules for Salespersons vs. Brokers

- **Referral fees between brokers:** Only **brokers** can pay or receive referral fees and split commissions.
- **Salespersons:** Cannot pay or receive referral fees for referring clients to other brokers or firms.

### Referral Fees for Services (Inspections, Surveys, etc.)
- Both **salespersons and brokers** can accept referral fees for services related to a sales contract, such as inspections or surveys.
<!-- - **Important:** The person paying for the service must be informed **in writing** about the referral fee **before** the service is requested. -->

- The person paying for the service must be informed **in writing** about the referral fee **before** the service is requested.
- Failure to provide this written disclosure is a violation.

### Referral Fees for Non-Confidential Information

- Active licensees (salespersons or brokers) may accept referral fees for referring **non-confidential information**, such as:
  - Property details
  - Parties involved
  - Financial information
<!-- - **Condition:** Your principal broker must provide **written consent** for accepting these fees. -->

- **Condition:** The principal broker must provide **written consent** for accepting these fees.

## Net Listings Are Prohibited in Virginia

- A **net listing** is when a seller says, "Sell my house and anything you get above this set amount is yours."
- This type of listing is **not allowed in Virginia**.
<!-- - Instead, you and the seller must have a clear agreement that does **not** include keeping extra proceeds over a specified amount. -->

- Instead, agents and the seller must have a clear agreement that does **not** include keeping extra proceeds over a specified amount.
