# Understanding Agency Relationships in Brokerage

<!-- In the world of brokerage, understanding the essence of **agency relationships** is crucial. This is a **legal relationship** where one person acts for another person. An agent is someone who has been trusted to represent another person's interests. -->
In brokerage, **agency relationships** represent a **legal relationship** where one person acts for another person. An agent is someone who has been trusted to represent another person's interests.

## Key Concepts of Agency Relationships

<!-- - **Agent and Principal:** You, as the *agent*, are entrusted by another person, the *principal*, with the power to act on their behalf.
- **Fiduciary Duties:** You must always act in the principal's best interests. These are called *fiduciary duties*. -->
- **Agent and Principal:** The *agent* is entrusted by another person, the *principal*, with the power to act on their behalf.
- **Fiduciary Duties:** Agents must always act in the principal's best interests. These are called *fiduciary duties*.

## Formation of the Agency Relationship

Agency relationships are usually created through an **express agreement**:

<!-- - The principal gives you the authority to act on their behalf, particularly when it comes to interacting with **third parties**.
- You receive a **fee** for your services. -->
- The principal gives the agent authority to act on their behalf, particularly when interacting with **third parties**.
- The agent receives a **fee** for services.

<!-- Understanding this relationship is important for **transactions** and **negotiations**. You must always put the client's interests first. -->
This relationship affects **transactions** and **negotiations**. Agents must always put the client's interests first.
