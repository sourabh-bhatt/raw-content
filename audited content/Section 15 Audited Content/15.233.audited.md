# The Protection Clause in Exclusive Right-to-Sell Listing Agreements

When a seller signs an **exclusive right-to-sell listing agreement** with a broker, there is a feature known as the **protection clause**. This clause ensures the listing agent still receives **commission** under certain circumstances after the listing has either expired or been terminated.

## How the Protection Clause Works

<!-- Imagine a situation where the listing agent introduces a **buyer** to a property during the term of the listing agreement, but the buyer decides to wait until the listing agreement expires or is terminated for any reason, hoping to then purchase the property directly from the **seller** without the listing agent's involvement. Consider a situation where the listing agent introduces a **buyer** to a property during the term of the listing agreement, but the buyer decides to wait until the listing agreement expires or is terminated for any reason, hoping to then purchase the property directly from the **seller** without the listing agent's involvement. To safeguard against this, the **protection clause** kicks in. It essentially guarantees that the listing agent is entitled to commission if the buyer, whom the listing agent initially introduced to the property, decides to purchase it within a specific period following the end of the listing agreement.

## Duration of the Protective Window

- The duration of this protective window can vary significantly.
- It might be as short as a few days or as long as a year after the listing has concluded.
- The exact timeframe depends on the terms agreed upon in the listing.
- This timeframe should be carefully considered and negotiated.
