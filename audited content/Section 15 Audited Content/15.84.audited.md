# Broker Accounting and Fiduciary Responsibilities

<!-- When you step into the role of a **broker**, one of your primary duties is **accounting**. Brokers have a primary duty of **accounting**. This involves more than keeping books in order; it is a **fiduciary responsibility** to safeguard and properly manage all money and property held on behalf of clients. This principle carries professional responsibility similar to being entrusted with valuable items.

## Key Responsibilities and Risks

### Commingling
<!-- One critical mistake to avoid is **commingling**. This happens when you mix or blend your client's funds with your own personal or business funds, such as depositing them into the same account. This can lead to: -->

One mistake to avoid is **commingling**. This happens when brokers mix client funds with personal or business funds, such as depositing them into the same account. This can lead to:

- Confusion
- Errors in bookkeeping
- Putting client funds at risk

<!-- Commingling breaks the trust your clients have placed in you. -->

Commingling breaks the trust clients have placed in brokers.

### Conversion
<!-- Another serious offense is **conversion**. This occurs if you use, borrow, or misappropriate your client's funds for your own use, even if you intend to return them. Conversion is essentially stealing and violates your duty to manage your client's assets responsibly. -->

Another serious offense is **conversion**. This occurs when brokers use, borrow, or misappropriate client funds for their own use, even with intent to return them. Conversion violates the duty to manage client assets responsibly.

## Accountability Beyond Money

<!-- Your accountability extends beyond just money. It also covers any critical documents handed to you by your client, such as: -->

Broker accountability extends beyond money. It also covers documents handed to them by clients, such as:

- **Deeds**
- **Title policies**
- **Abstracts**
- **Mortgages**

These documents are often irreplaceable and hold significant value. Mishandling them can lead to severe consequences.
