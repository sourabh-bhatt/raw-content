# Representing a Landlord Client and Ministerial Acts

<!-- When you're representing a **landlord client**, it's important to remember that you can offer assistance to a **tenant** or potential tenant through what are known as **ministerial acts**, as long as they're not forbidden by law or your **brokerage agreement**. -->

When representing **landlord clients**, agents can offer assistance to **tenants** or potential tenants through **ministerial acts**, as long as they are not forbidden by law or the **brokerage agreement**.

## Understanding Ministerial Acts

<!-- - These are basic tasks or duties that **do not establish a professional relationship** between you as the agent and the tenant. -->
<!-- - By performing these acts, you're simply facilitating certain processes without stepping into a **brokerage relationship** with the tenant. -->

- These are basic tasks or duties that **do not establish a professional relationship** between the agent and the tenant.
- By performing these acts, agents facilitate certain processes without stepping into a **brokerage relationship** with the tenant.

## Ministerial Acts Requirements

<!-- This distinction is crucial because it: -->

This distinction matters because it:

<!-- - Delineates the boundaries of your responsibilities. -->
<!-- - Clarifies the nature of your involvement with both parties (landlord and tenant). -->

- Delineates the boundaries of agent responsibilities.
- Clarifies the nature of agent involvement with both parties (landlord and tenant).

Example: Answering general questions about the property or providing standard application forms to a tenant would be considered ministerial acts, as they do not create a brokerage relationship.

Understanding these limits helps ensure compliance with legal and brokerage requirements while maintaining professional integrity.
