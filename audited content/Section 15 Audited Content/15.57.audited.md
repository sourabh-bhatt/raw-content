# Understanding Material Facts in Real Estate

<!-- When dealing with **material facts**, you're essentially handling information that's significant enough to sway someone's decision-making process. It's crucial that you, as a **broker** or **agent**, understand your obligation to share these material facts with your clients and customers. This responsibility isn't just a best practice; in most cases, it's mandated by **state license laws**. -->
When dealing with **material facts**, agents handle information that's significant enough to sway someone's decision-making process. **Brokers** or **agents** must understand their obligation to share these material facts with their clients and customers. This responsibility isn't just a best practice; in most cases, it's mandated by **state license laws**.

## What Qualifies as a Material Fact?

<!-- A material fact is anything that could be important to the **buyer**, the **seller**, or relates directly to the **property** itself. Material facts typically involve property conditions, defects, or circumstances that affect the property's value or desirability. This can include: -->
A material fact is anything that could affect the **buyer**, the **seller**, or relates directly to the **property** itself. Material facts typically involve property conditions, defects, or circumstances that affect the property's value or desirability. This can include:

<!-- - Details you discover through diligent inspection
- Information disclosed to you by another party about the property -->
- Details discovered through diligent inspection
- Information disclosed by another party about the property

<!-- Your **duty to disclose** does not depend on how you discovered the information. If it's relevant to the property, you need to share it. -->
The **duty to disclose** does not depend on how the information was discovered. If it's relevant to the property, agents need to share it.

## Types of Material Facts to Watch For

<!-- Consider disclosure obligations carefully in all situations. If you are representing the seller and you learn about the buyer's financial situation, you must consider your disclosure obligations carefully. Generally, confidential information about your client should not be disclosed without permission. -->
Consider disclosure obligations carefully in all situations. If representing the seller and learning about the buyer's financial situation, agents must consider their disclosure obligations carefully. Generally, confidential information about the client should not be disclosed without permission.

Remember:

<!-- - Withholding material information about the property, regardless of how you came to know it, is not an option.
- Transparency about property-related facts is essential to ensure all parties have the information they need to make informed decisions.
- Confidential client information requires different handling than property-related material facts. -->
- Withholding material information about the property, regardless of how it was discovered, is not an option.
- Transparency about property-related facts ensures all parties have the information they need to make informed decisions.
- Confidential client information requires different handling than property-related material facts.

<!-- Being upfront about material facts is not only about following the law; it's about fostering **trust** and **integrity** in your professional relationships. -->
Being upfront about material facts involves following the law and fostering **trust** and **integrity** in professional relationships.
