# Dual Agency in Transactions

<!-- When you're in a situation known as **dual agency**, you'll find yourself representing *both sides* in a transaction. This means you represent both the **buyer** and the **seller**. In **dual agency**, agents represent *both sides* in a transaction. This means representing both the **buyer** and the **seller**. This scenario requires specific disclosure and consent requirements in Virginia. Before agents can assume this role, they must gain proper **consent** from all involved parties. This involves making thorough **disclosures** about what dual agency means and the risks it may entail.

## Responsibilities and Challenges of Dual Agency

<!-- As a dual agent, you'll wear two hats. You owe duties to both the buyer and the seller. This might sound straightforward, but it's anything but simple. As dual agents, professionals owe duties to both the buyer and the seller. This creates challenges due to the inherent **conflict of interest**. The buyer and seller are competitors. Each aims for the best outcome from their perspective. Serving both sides fairly and impartially requires careful management.

## Example: Settlement Agents in Transactions

Example: **Settlement agents** facilitate the closing process by handling documents and funds. They work with both parties to ensure all conditions are met before the transaction closes. This duty to multiple parties shows the careful balance required when serving different interests.

## Key Considerations

- Obtain clear and informed **consent** from all parties before acting as a dual agent.
- Provide full **disclosures** explaining the nature and risks of dual agency.
- Maintain **transparency** and clear communication throughout the transaction.
- Carefully balance the competing interests of both buyer and seller.
