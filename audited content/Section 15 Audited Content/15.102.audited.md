# Representing Sellers in Transactions

<!-- When representing sellers in real estate transactions, there are specific responsibilities outlined in the **Virginia Real Estate License Law** that you must adhere to. Understanding these duties ensures you serve your clients effectively and remain in compliance with the law. -->

When representing sellers in transactions, there are specific responsibilities outlined in the **Virginia License Law** that agents must adhere to. Understanding these duties ensures effective client service and legal compliance.

## Duties Under the Brokerage Agreement

- Always perform in line with the **brokerage agreement** terms.
- Actively promote the seller's interests by carrying out marketing activities as specified in the agreement.
- Aim to secure a sale that meets the agreed-upon price and terms, or terms acceptable to the seller.
- Agents do not need to seek additional offers once a sale contract is in place unless the agreement or sale contract requires it.

## Assisting with Offers and Negotiations

- Help draft and negotiate offers and amendments.
- Establish strategies to meet the seller's goals.
- Present *all* written offers to the seller, regardless of existing sale contracts.
- Assist the seller in fulfilling their obligations to facilitate a smooth settlement process.

## Confidentiality and Care

- Maintain the confidentiality of the seller's personal and financial information.
- Disclose only what is legally required or agreed upon by the seller.
- Exercise **ordinary care** in all dealings.
- Account for all money and property belonging to the seller in a timely manner.

## Disclosure Requirements

- Disclose to the seller any **material facts** about the property or transaction that the agent is aware of.
- Follow all relevant laws, including **fair housing laws** and other applicable statutes.
- Be honest with prospective buyers by disclosing known **material adverse facts** about the property's physical condition, such as the presence of **defective drywall**.
- Such disclosures to buyers should always be made in writing.
- Agents do not need to disclose physical conditions outside the property boundaries or government land use regulations.

## Interaction with Buyers and Other Sellers

- If allowed by the agreement and law, assist buyers with **non-advisory tasks** without breaching the agreement with the seller or creating a formal relationship with the buyer.
- It is acceptable to show alternative properties to prospective buyers or represent other sellers without violating obligations to the current seller client.

## Brokerage Relationships and Settlement Services

- Always disclose your **brokerage relationships** in residential real estate dealings as required by law.
- Agents are *not* obligated to inform whether settlement services will be performed by an attorney or a non-attorney settlement agent.
