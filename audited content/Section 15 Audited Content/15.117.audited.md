# Brokerage Relationships and Disclosure Requirements in Virginia Real Estate

## Types of Agency and Brokerage Relationships

<!-- When you're about to start a **brokerage relationship** in Virginia, you need to inform your clients right off the bat what type of agency you'll be providingâ€”be it **standard** or **limited service**â€”or if you're acting as an **independent contractor**. It's crucial to be upfront about how you'll be compensated and if you plan to share that compensation with another broker involved in the same transaction. -->

Before establishing a **brokerage relationship** in Virginia, licensees must inform clients about the type of agency being provided - **standard** or **limited service** - or if acting as an **independent contractor**. Disclosure must include compensation arrangements and any plans to share compensation with another broker involved in the same transaction.

### Limited Service Agency

<!-- For those considering a limited service agency, be very clear in your **brokerage agreement**. You must: -->

Limited service agencies must clearly state the arrangement in their **brokerage agreement**. Requirements include:

- Explicitly state that the agent acts as a **limited service agent**.
- List the specific services being provided.
- Clarify which duties a standard agent would normally perform that will *not* be handled.

This information needs to stand out, either by using **bold lettering**, **ALL CAPS**, *underlining*, or placing it in a separate box. The Virginia Legislature provides specific wording to help ensure proper disclosure.

### Independent Contractor

<!-- If you decide to work as an **independent contractor**, mention this clearly in your brokerage agreement, alongside a detailed list of the services you'll provide to your client. -->

**Independent contractors** must clearly state this arrangement in the brokerage agreement, alongside a detailed list of services to be provided.

## Disclosure to Non-Clients and Customers

<!-- When dealing with **non-clients** or **customers**â€”such as landlords and tenants who aren't represented by another licenseeâ€”make sure to disclose whom you're representing (if anyone) before discussing property details. This disclosure is necessary only during substantial conversations about specific properties. -->

When dealing with **non-clients** or **customers** - such as landlords and tenants who aren't represented by another licensee - agents must disclose whom they represent before discussing property details. This disclosure applies only during substantial conversations about specific properties.

- Initial disclosures to non-clients/customers can be made **orally** under certain conditions.
- **Written disclosures** must be provided before offering any "specific real estate assistance."
- Any changes in the relationship with a client or customer must be documented in writing.

## Dual Agency and Representation

In situations of **dual agency** - where an agent or firm represents both sides in a transaction - this is allowed in Virginia when:

- It is properly disclosed and agreed upon **in writing**.
- The agent explicitly states that personal or financial information obtained from one party will not be shared with the other without permission.
- Any requests for confidentiality are respected.

## Personal Interest and Disclosure Obligations

<!-- As a licensee, if you have a **personal stake** in a transaction, you are obligated to disclose your involvement. This applies whether you are actively advertising the property or not, and extends to properties owned by: -->

Licensees with a **personal stake** in a transaction must disclose their involvement. This applies whether actively advertising the property or not, and extends to properties owned by:

- The licensee personally
- The licensee's family
- The licensee's firm
- The licensee's business

## Disclosure of Material Facts

<!-- Regarding properties, you must disclose all known **material adverse facts** about the condition of the property to both clients and customers. However: -->

Licensees must disclose all known **material adverse facts** about property condition to both clients and customers. However:

- Transaction-specific information, such as customer financial details, should only be shared with the client.

## Record Keeping and MLS Considerations

- The **principal broker** is responsible for keeping records of all agency and representation disclosures for **three years** from the execution date.
- Merely using a **common source information company** (e.g., MLS) does **not** automatically establish a brokerage relationship with a seller, landlord, or another licensee.
- MLS participants may need to disclose their brokerage relationship nature, and such information might be made public.
