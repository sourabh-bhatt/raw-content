# Presenting Yourself as a Qualified Real Estate Agent

<!-- When you're practicing as an **agent**, it's essential that you present yourself to the public as someone uniquely qualified because of your **experience**, **skills**, and **knowledge**. Remember, you have a significant responsibility to serve your **client** with **diligence** and **competence**. -->
When practicing as an **agent**, presenting oneself to the public as someone uniquely qualified because of **experience**, **skills**, and **knowledge** is necessary. Agents have a responsibility to serve their **client** with **diligence** and **competence**.

## Recognizing Your Limits of Expertise

<!-- It's crucial for you to identify when a situation arises that's outside your expertise. In such cases, you should advise your client to seek guidance from other professionals, such as: -->
Agents must identify when a situation arises that is outside their expertise. In such cases, they should advise their client to seek guidance from other professionals, such as:

- **Structural engineers**
- **Surveyors**
- **Accountants**
- **Attorneys**

depending on the need.

## Understanding and Claiming Sub-Specialties

<!-- As you dive deeper into the real estate profession, you'll discover various **sub-specialties** that demand specific knowledge and skills. It's vital to resist the temptation to claim expertise in areas like: -->
As agents progress in the real estate profession, they will discover various **sub-specialties** that demand specific knowledge and skills. Agents must resist the temptation to claim expertise in areas like:

- **Short sales**
- **Commercial real estate**
- **Property management**

<!-- unless you genuinely have the necessary **training** and **experience** to back up such claims. -->
unless they genuinely have the necessary **training** and **experience** to back up such claims.

<!-- Claiming to be an expert in a particular sub-specialty without the proper credentials isn't just misleading; it also subjects you to a higher **standard of care**. Always ensure your claims of expertise are well-founded to maintain trust and uphold your **professional integrity**. -->
Claiming to be an expert in a particular sub-specialty without the proper credentials is misleading and subjects the agent to a higher **standard of care**. Agents must ensure their claims of expertise are well-founded to maintain trust and uphold **professional integrity**.
