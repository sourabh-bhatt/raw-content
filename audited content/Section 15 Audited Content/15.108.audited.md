# Understanding Dual Agency in Transactions

<!-- When you step into the world of real estate transactions, it's crucial to understand the delicate balance of responsibilities, especially when it comes to **dual agency**. -->

Professionals must understand the balance of responsibilities, especially regarding **dual agency**.

## Representing the Seller

<!-- Imagine you're representing a **seller**, tasked with fetching the highest possible price for their property. Now, consider a scenario where an **unrepresented buyer** approaches you, mistakenly believing you're working in their best interest. In such instances, without clear communication, you might create a **dual agency** situation. -->

Example: An agent representing a **seller** is tasked with fetching the highest possible price for their property. Consider a scenario where an **unrepresented buyer** approaches the agent, mistakenly believing the agent is working in their best interest. In such instances, without clear communication, the agent might create a **dual agency** situation.

- To navigate this, agents must **provide written disclosure** using Virginia's **Disclosure of Brokerage Relationship form** to the unrepresented buyer.
- **Disclose in writing that the agent represents the seller**.
- The primary goal is to advocate for the **seller's interests**, not to secure the best deal for the buyer.

## Representing the Buyer

<!-- On the flip side, if your role is to champion the **buyer**, you must: -->

If the agent's role is to champion the **buyer**, the agent must:

- **Provide written disclosure** to both the **seller** and the **seller's agent** using the required Virginia disclosure forms that the agent is the **buyer's advocate**.
- Strive to negotiate the best terms for the buyer.
