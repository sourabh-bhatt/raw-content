# Escrow Accounts in Real Estate Transactions

<!-- When you're handling money or property for others, you might need to set up an **escrow account**, but only if you're actually holding onto these assets. Remember, if you do set one up, it has to be registered in the name of your **licensed firm** and must be with a bank that's **federally insured**. Moreover, you need to clearly mark all accounts and deposits as **"escrow."** -->

When handling money or property for others, agents might need to set up an **escrow account**, but only if they are actually holding onto these assets. If agents set one up, it has to be registered in the name of their **licensed firm** and must be with a bank that's **federally insured**. Moreover, agents need to clearly mark all accounts and deposits as **"escrow."**

## Items to Deposit into an Escrow Account

The following items need to be placed into the escrow account (this list is not exhaustive):

- **Earnest money**
- **Down payments** (which must be deposited within 5 banking days after ratification)
- Rental payments
- Application deposits
- Security deposits
- Advances

<!-- For these items, you must deposit them into the escrow account within **5 banking days after receipt**, or, in the case of application deposits, after the rental application has been approved. -->

For these items, agents must deposit them into the escrow account within **5 banking days after receipt**, or, in the case of application deposits, after the rental application has been approved.

## Interest on Escrow Accounts

<!-- Your escrow account does not have to be interest-bearing. However, if it does earn interest, you must provide a **written disclosure** stating who will keep the interest. -->

Escrow accounts do not have to be interest-bearing. However, if they do earn interest, agents must provide a **written disclosure** stating who will keep the interest.

## Disbursing Funds from the Escrow Account

Funds should only be removed from the escrow account under the following conditions:

- The transaction has been completed.
- There is mutual agreement between the parties involved.
- A court order directs the disbursement.

### Important Restrictions

<!-- - You **cannot withdraw commissions** until they are actually earned and all parties involved agree.
- If an escrow account is running short, you **cannot cover the shortage** with funds from another escrow account. -->

- Agents **cannot withdraw commissions** until they are actually earned and all parties involved agree.
- If an escrow account is running short, agents **cannot cover the shortage** with funds from another escrow account.

## Handling Disputes and Unclosed Transactions

<!-- In cases where there is a disagreement or the transaction does not close, you must hold onto the funds until one of the following occurs: -->

In cases where there is a disagreement or the transaction does not close, agents must hold onto the funds until one of the following occurs:

- All parties reach an agreement on how to distribute the funds.
- A court order is issued.
<!-- - You file an **interpleader**, which means handing the funds over to the court until the disagreement is resolved. -->

- Agents file an **interpleader**, which means handing the funds over to the court until the disagreement is resolved.

Alternatively, the **principal or supervising broker** can decide the outcome based on the terms of the sales contract, but this requires:

- Sending a **written notice** to the party not receiving the funds.
- Giving them **15 days to object**.

<!-- Understanding and abiding by these guidelines ensures that you manage escrow accounts ethically and effectively, safeguarding the financial interests of all parties involved. -->

Understanding and abiding by these guidelines ensures that agents manage escrow accounts ethically and effectively, safeguarding the financial interests of all parties involved.
