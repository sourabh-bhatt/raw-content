# Dual Agency in Residential Real Estate

<!-- In real estate, there's an important concept you need to grasp—**dual agency**. -->
In real estate, **dual agency** is a key concept. 

## Legal and Ethical Requirements

<!-- Imagine you're representing both sides in a residential real estate transaction. To do this legally and ethically, you must obtain **written consent** from all parties involved. Before anyone signs anything, you have to clearly explain the implications of you being a dual agent. -->
When representing both sides in a residential real estate transaction, agents must obtain **written consent** from all parties involved. Before anyone signs anything, agents must clearly explain the implications of dual agency. 

- This disclosure is not just a casual conversation.
- The law requires that this disclosure be in writing.
- It must be delivered to both parties **before** any dual agency begins.

## When Dual Agency Applies

<!-- - If you are already representing someone as their agent, and they want to buy or sell a property involving another client of yours, you can take on a dual role.
- This is only allowed after making the necessary disclosures to both clients. -->
- If an agent is already representing someone as their agent, and they want to buy or sell a property involving another client, the agent can take on a dual role.
- This is only allowed after making the necessary disclosures to both clients.

## Representing a New Client Alongside an Existing One

<!-- - When you want to represent a new client in addition to an existing client under dual agency, you must disclose your role to both the current client and the newcomer **before** proceeding. -->
- When representing a new client in addition to an existing client under dual agency, agents must disclose their role to both the current client and the newcomer **before** proceeding.

## Signed Disclosures

- These disclosures must be **signed by the clients** to be valid.
- Simply including this information in contracts or other transaction documents is insufficient.
- These disclosures do not replace your obligation to establish all brokerage relationships in a written agreement.

## Protecting Yourself and Managing Client Consent

<!-- - Being transparent about your dual agency status protects you legally, provided you follow the rules.
- If a client does not agree to the dual agency arrangement, you have the right to stop representing them in that transaction.
- This refusal does not affect your ability to represent the other client or to continue working with the withdrawn client in other dealings. -->
- Being transparent about dual agency status protects agents legally, provided they follow the rules.
- If a client does not agree to the dual agency arrangement, agents have the right to stop representing them in that transaction.
- This refusal does not affect the agent's ability to represent the other client or to continue working with the withdrawn client in other dealings.

## Contents of the Disclosure Document

The disclosure document must be comprehensive and include:

<!-- - A clear statement that you are acting as a dual agent.
- Identification of which parties you represent and in what capacity.
- Explanation of what dual agency involves, including limitations on the advice you can provide once you take on this role. -->
- A clear statement that the agent is acting as a dual agent.
- Identification of which parties the agent represents and in what capacity.
- Explanation of what dual agency involves, including limitations on the advice the agent can provide once taking on this role.

<!-- By signing this document, all parties give their **informed consent** to your dual role. This step is critical to maintaining transparency and trust in any residential real estate transaction involving dual agency. -->
By signing this document, all parties give their **informed consent** to the agent's dual role. This step maintains transparency and trust in any residential real estate transaction involving dual agency.
