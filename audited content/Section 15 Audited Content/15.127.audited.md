# Liability and Responsibility for Property Condition Statements

## Example
<!-- Imagine you're discussing a property with a potential buyer and you say, _"This house is really built well, and the foundation is particularly solid."_ Fast forward a year, and it turns out the house has severe settling issues, requiring substantial repairs. Your responsibility regarding this statement, and the consequences you might face, depend largely on your role in the transaction. -->

Example: You're discussing a property with a potential buyer and you say, _"This house is really built well, and the foundation is particularly solid."_ A year later, the house has severe settling issues, requiring substantial repairs. Your responsibility regarding this statement, and the consequences you might face, depend largely on your role in the transaction.

## Seller's Role and Liability
<!-- - If you're relaying a statement made by the **seller** and, at the time, there were no visible issues with the foundation, then the seller typically **wouldn't be held liable**. -->
- If agents relay statements made by **sellers** and, at the time, there were no visible issues with the foundation, then sellers typically **wouldn't be held liable**.
- This is because sellers aren't usually expected to possess **specialized knowledge** about construction.

## Real Estate Agent's Responsibility
<!-- - If you, acting as a **real estate agent**, make this statement, the situation becomes more complicated. -->
<!-- - Even if you believed the foundation was solid, you might still be held responsible for **misrepresentation**. -->
- If **real estate agents** make such statements, the situation becomes more complicated.
- Even if agents believed the foundation was solid, they might still be held responsible for **misrepresentation**.
- Possible consequences include:
  - **Damages** to be paid
  <!-- - **Suspension or revocation** of your real estate license -->
  - **Suspension or revocation** of the real estate license
- This is because agents are expected to have:
  - Known about foundational issues, or
  - Taken steps to **verify the state of the foundation** before making such statements.

## Builder's Accountability
- If the statement came directly from the **builder** responsible for constructing the house, they would likely be held accountable for:
  - **Misrepresentation**, or
  - Potentially even **fraud**
- Builders are expected to have **expert knowledge** of their work's quality and the integrity of the foundations they lay.

Understanding these dynamics demonstrates the need for **due diligence** and **integrity** in representations to:

<!-- - Protect yourself legally -->

- Protect agents legally
- Maintain trust with clients
