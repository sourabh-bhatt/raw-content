# Understanding Ministerial Acts in Brokerage and Agency Contracts

When learning about the responsibilities within **brokerage** and **agency contracts**, understanding the concept of **ministerial acts** matters. These are routine tasks that:

<!-- - Do not require you to make decisions -->
<!-- - Do not require exercising independent judgment -->

- Do not require agents to make decisions
- Do not require exercising independent judgment

## Legal Context and Application

<!-- In Virginia, **real estate license laws** allow you, as a **real estate agent**, to perform these ministerial acts for individuals who are *not* your clients. This can occur within real estate transactions without the need to establish an **agency relationship**. -->

In Virginia, **real estate license laws** allow **real estate agents** to perform these ministerial acts for individuals who are *not* their clients. This can occur within real estate transactions without the need to establish an **agency relationship**.

## Ministerial Acts Requirements

<!-- - Ministerial acts are essentially **basic tasks** that help ensure transactions proceed smoothly. -->
<!-- - These acts do *not* require you to act as an advisor or make decisions on behalf of any party. -->
<!-- - Understanding ministerial acts helps delineate the boundaries of your **responsibilities**, especially when you are not in an official agent-client relationship. -->

- Ministerial acts are **basic tasks** that help ensure transactions proceed smoothly.
- These acts do *not* require agents to act as an advisor or make decisions on behalf of any party.
- Understanding ministerial acts helps delineate the boundaries of **responsibilities**, especially when agents are not in an official agent-client relationship.

**Example:**

Providing factual information about a property or scheduling appointments can be considered ministerial acts, whereas giving advice or negotiating terms would not.
