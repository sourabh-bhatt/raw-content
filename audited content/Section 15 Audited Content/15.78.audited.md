# Understanding Open Listings in Brokerage

## What is an Open Listing?

An **open listing** is a type of agreement between a **broker** and a **property seller** that allows multiple brokers to market the seller's property simultaneously. The key feature of this arrangement is that the seller agrees to pay a **commission** only if the broker is the one who actually sells the property or is directly responsible for bringing the buyer.

## Key Characteristics of Open Listings

- **Multiple Brokers Involved:** Several brokers can market the property at the same time.
- **Commission Contingent on Sale:** The commission is payable only if the broker successfully sells the property or finds the buyer.
- **Seller's Right to Sell Independently:** If the seller finds a buyer without any broker's assistance, no commission is owed.
- **Flexibility:** This arrangement encourages competition among brokers and provides the seller with a wide range of potential buyers.

## Formality and Contract Nature

- In Virginia, open listings must be in writing to be enforceable.
- The broker must find a buyer to earn a commission.
- These contracts are typically:
  - **Unilateral:** Only the broker is required to perform (find a buyer).
  - **Executory:** The contract becomes fully effective only when a sale is made.

## Duration and Legal Considerations

- Virginia law requires that open listings have a definite expiration date.
