# Understanding Material Facts and Disclosure Requirements

<!-- When working with properties, you must recognize what constitutes a **_material fact_**. A feature that is critical to one buyer might be irrelevant to another. Certain conditions and characteristics of a property must be disclosed to potential buyers. -->

Agents must recognize what constitutes a **_material fact_**. A feature that affects one buyer might be irrelevant to another. Certain conditions and characteristics of a property must be disclosed to potential buyers.

## Key Material Facts to Disclose

- **Unstable Soils:** Example: If a house is built on unstable soils like marine clay, which may cause foundation issues, this is a material fact.
- **Inaccurate Property Details:** Misrepresentations of lot size or house dimensions can influence a buyer's offer or decision.
- **Encroachments and Easements:** Any restrictions on property use, such as parts of a house built over a utility easement, must be disclosed.
- **Pest Infestations and Hazardous Materials:** Presence of pests, toxic mold, or other hazardous substances inside the home.
- **Structural Problems:** Significant issues with the roof, doors, foundation, or other structural components.
- **Condition of Systems and Fixtures:** Problems with electrical or plumbing systems, appliances, and fixtures.
- **Natural Hazards and Special Zones:** Properties located in flood plains, earthquake zones, landslide-prone areas, or other specially regulated zones.
- **Insurance History:** Properties with a history of insurance claims that may affect insurability or cost.
- **Unpermitted Modifications:** Additions or changes made without proper permits.
- **Zoning Changes:** Upcoming local zoning changes that could impact property use.
- **District Boundaries:** Misunderstandings about school, utility, or taxation district boundaries.
- **Airport Flight Paths:** Properties located on flight paths that may cause noise concerns.
- **Financial Liabilities:** Existing liens, taxes, or special assessments affecting the property.
- **Environmental Hazards:** Presence of lead, radon, asbestos, or contamination of soil or groundwater.
- **Stigmatized Properties:** Properties known for events such as murder, crime, suicide, or those affected by registered sex offenders.

## Example of Disclosure Obligations

<!-- If you represent a seller, you are obligated to inform all potential buyers about any known material defects in the property. Example: When representing a seller, agents are obligated to inform all potential buyers about any known material defects in the property. While this may seem to conflict with the duty to serve the seller's best interests, transparency avoids legal consequences such as fraud or misrepresentation. The agent's role includes facilitating the sale and ensuring honesty and full disclosure throughout the transaction.
