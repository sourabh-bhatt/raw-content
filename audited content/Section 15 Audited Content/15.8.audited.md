# Disclosure of Material Facts When Representing Tenant Clients

When representing **tenant clients** as a **standard agent**, agents must disclose all **material facts** they are aware of related to the property or the transaction. 

## What Are Material Facts?

Material facts are details or information that could influence the tenant's decision about the property. These may include:

- Known **defects** within the property
- **Legal issues** surrounding the property
- **Financial concerns** such as outstanding liens
- **Environmental aspects** like a history of flooding
- **Zoning restrictions** affecting property use

## Disclosing Material Facts

Understanding and communicating these facts protects clients and enables them to make informed decisions. Agents guide and support clients through real estate transactions. This means agents must be diligent in discovering any potential issues or concerns related to the properties.

## Scope of Material Facts

Material facts extend beyond physical attributes or conditions of the property. They also include:

- Legal considerations
- Financial encumbrances
- Environmental conditions

Example: If a property has a history of flooding or zoning restrictions that limit its use, clients deserve to know this information before making commitments.

## Agent's Responsibility

The responsibility to disclose known material facts is part of an agent's duty as a real estate agent. This practice:

- Enables agents to provide high-level service
- Builds trust with clients
- Ensures clients are fully informed throughout the process
- Ethically safeguards both agents and clients against potential disputes or legal issues
