# Designated Agency in Residential Real Estate Transactions

<!-- When you're engaging in a residential real estate transaction, it's important to understand the concept of **designated agency**. -->

When engaging in a residential real estate transaction, understanding the concept of **designated agency** matters. 

## What is Designated Agency?

Designated agency allows the **supervising broker** to assign different agents within the same brokerage to represent different clients in the same transaction. This means:

- Each client gets **dedicated representation** without conflict of interest.
- Avoidance of **dual agency**, where one agent represents both parties.

However, the **supervising broker** overseeing the transaction is considered a **dual agent**.

## Confidentiality Requirements

<!-- As a designated agent, it is crucial to: -->

As a designated agent:

<!-- - Keep your client's **personal and financial information confidential**.
- Protect any details shared during the brokerage relationship or information the client has specifically requested to keep confidential. -->

- Keep client **personal and financial information confidential**.
- Protect any details shared during the brokerage relationship or information the client has specifically requested to keep confidential.

The only exceptions to confidentiality are:

- When the law requires disclosure.
<!-- - When your client agrees to disclosure in writing. -->

- When the client agrees to disclosure in writing.

## Disclosure of Designated Agency

<!-- Transparency about your role is essential. You must: -->

Transparency about the role matters. Designated agents must:

<!-- - Disclose your role as a **designated agent** clearly and conspicuously. -->

- Disclose their role as a **designated agent** clearly and conspicuously.
- This disclosure can be included with other disclosures or provided separately.
- Make the disclosure stand out by using **bold lettering, all caps, underlining**, or placing it in a separate box.

There is a specific form for disclosing designated agents in a transaction. This form:

- Acknowledges that the broker represents more than one party.
- Assures that confidential information shared by one client will not be disclosed to the other party without proper consent or legal requirement.

## Brokerage Relationship Agreements and Legal Considerations

- Choosing a designated agent **does not** exempt agents from having all brokerage relationships detailed in a **written agreement**.
- Disclosing the existence of a brokerage relationship as required by law **does not** expose agents to legal action.
- Such disclosures **do not** terminate any brokerage relationships.

## Handling Client Refusal of Designated Agency

If a client refuses to consent to a disclosed designated agency agreement:

<!-- - You may **withdraw from representing that client without liability**.
- This withdrawal does **not** prevent you from representing the other client in the transaction.
- You may also represent the withdrawing client in future transactions that do not involve designated representation. -->

- Agents may **withdraw from representing that client without liability**.
- This withdrawal does **not** prevent agents from representing the other client in the transaction.
- Agents may also represent the withdrawing client in future transactions that do not involve designated representation.
