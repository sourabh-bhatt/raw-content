# Ratification in Brokerage and Agency Contracts

<!-- When you're learning about **brokerage** and **agency contracts**, there's an important concept you need to graspâ€”**ratification**. -->

When learning about **brokerage** and **agency contracts**, **ratification** is a concept to understand. 

## What is Ratification?

*Ratification* refers to the process by which **approval**, **sanction**, or **validation** is given *after the fact*. 

## How Ratification Works

<!-- - Imagine you're working on behalf of someone **without having their upfront consent**. -->
- Consider working on behalf of someone **without having their upfront consent**. 
- Although this might seem unusual, there is a provision for this scenario called **ratification**.
<!-- - If you perform actions for someone without their initial consent, ratification occurs when that person later decides to officially **approve** or **validate** what you've done. -->
- If agents perform actions for someone without their initial consent, ratification occurs when that person later decides to officially **approve** or **validate** what they have done.

## Ratification

- This process essentially creates an **agency relationship retrospectively**.
<!-- - Understanding ratification helps you navigate situations where you might have acted in someone's interest **without a prior agreement**, and they choose to recognize your efforts officially afterwards. -->
- Understanding ratification helps agents navigate situations where they might have acted in someone's interest **without a prior agreement**, and they choose to recognize agent efforts officially afterwards.

**Example:**

Suppose an agent negotiates a deal on behalf of a client without their prior consent. If the client later approves the agent's actions, ratification has occurred, and an agency relationship is established retroactively.
