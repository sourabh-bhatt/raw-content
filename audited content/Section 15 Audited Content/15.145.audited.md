# Buyer Occupancy Disclosure in Residential Transactions

<!-- When working with buyers in a residential transaction, you must inform the seller if your clients plan to live in the property as their main residence. When working with buyers in residential transactions, agents must inform sellers if clients plan to live in the property as their main residence. This requirement can be fulfilled by ensuring that buyer intentions to occupy the property are clearly stated in the sales contract. This step fulfills the duty to disclose their intentions.

## Disclosure Requirements

<!-- - Clearly stating the buyer's intention to occupy the property in the sales contract meets your responsibility as an agent. -->
<!-- - This disclosure helps protect you legally when the buyer's intentions are properly disclosed. -->

- Clearly stating the buyer's intention to occupy the property in the sales contract meets agent responsibility.
- This disclosure helps protect agents legally when buyer intentions are properly disclosed.

## Legal Protection for Agents

<!-- - By disclosing whether the buyer intends to occupy the property, you help protect yourself from potential legal issues. -->
<!-- - Including the buyer's occupancy intentions in the sales contract safeguards you against potential legal issues related to this disclosure. -->

- By disclosing whether buyers intend to occupy the property, agents protect themselves from potential legal issues.
- Including buyer occupancy intentions in the sales contract safeguards agents against potential legal issues related to this disclosure.

Example: If a buyer plans to use the property as their primary residence, explicitly stating this in the contract informs the seller and protects you as the agent from liability if questions arise later about occupancy status.
