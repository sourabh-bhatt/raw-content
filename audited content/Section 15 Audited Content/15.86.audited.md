# Understanding Estoppel in Agency Relationships

<!-- Estoppel is a crucial concept in real estate because it can establish **agency relationships** in certain situations. -->

Estoppel can establish **agency relationships** in certain situations.

## What is Estoppel?

Estoppel occurs when a party's actions or words lead another party to reasonably believe that an agency relationship exists. Once estoppel is applied, it **locks in the existence of the agency relationship**, making it impossible for someone to deny it.

## How Courts Use Estoppel

- Courts apply estoppel on a **case-by-case basis**, which can make outcomes unpredictable.
- Judges evaluate whether misleading behavior has occurred that would justify estoppel.

## When Does Estoppel Apply?

Estoppel typically comes into play when there has been some form of **misleading behavior**, such as:

- Actions
- Words

These behaviors lead someone to have certain expectations about an agency relationship.

## Application of Estoppel

- Estoppel ensures that a party cannot deny an agency relationship after another party has relied on their statements or actions.
- It protects individuals who have placed their trust in indications of an agency relationship from being harmed by ambiguity or contradictory claims.

**Example:**

A seller acts in a way that leads a buyer to believe the seller's agent has authority to negotiate. If the seller later denies the agent's authority, estoppel may prevent the seller from doing so if the buyer relied on that belief to their detriment.

In essence, estoppel acts as a safeguard against exploitation of ambiguous statements or actions regarding agency relationships.
