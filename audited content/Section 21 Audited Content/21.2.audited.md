# Virginia Property Owners' Association (POA) Act and Subdivision Developments

In real estate, specifically subdivision developments and condominiums in Virginia, understanding the **Virginia Property Owners' Association (POA) Act** is necessary. This Act governs how developments and subdivisions operate within Virginia, laying down the rules for residential and recreational lots, common areas, and the property owners' association itself. As a member of a property owners' association, members are automatically required to contribute to the maintenance of common areas through **assessments**.

## Applicability of the POA Act

- The Act **does not cover developments recorded before January 1, 1999**.
- It does **not apply** to developments created under the **Condominium Act, Cooperative Act, Time-Share Act, or the Membership Camping Act**.
- Additionally, **non-stock, non-profit corporations** primarily offering ski, golf, and other recreational facilities to their members and the public are also exempt.

## Seller Disclosure Requirements

When selling development lots, sellers must include several essential disclosures in the sales contract:

- Whether the property falls under the **POA Act**.
- The obligation to provide an **association disclosure packet**.
- The buyer's rights to **rescission** and to request an updated packet.

### Association Disclosure Packet Contents

The disclosure packet includes information such as:

- The association's name.
- Any associated fees.
- The current budget or a statement of financial position.
- Any architectural restrictions.

Buyers have the right to receive this packet **before settlement**; failure to provide it can result in waiving these rights.

## Exceptions to Disclosure Requirements

Certain transactions are exempt from these disclosure requirements, including:

- Gift transfers.
- Court orders.
- Foreclosures.

## Buyer's Rights

Buyers have the right to **rescind the sales contract** under specific circumstances outlined in the Act. This provides a way to back out of the contract within certain time frames if conditions are met.

## Consequences of Failing to Provide Disclosure Packet

- The association may **lose the right to claim unpaid assessments**.
- The preparer of the packet could face liability for **actual damages up to stipulated limits**.
- Associations may charge fees for preparing and delivering disclosure packets, with caps depending on whether the association is **professionally or self-managed**.

## Association Governance and Enforcement

- The association's board has the power to **make, adopt, and enforce development regulations**.
- Amendments to the declaration often require a **majority vote from members**.
- Associations have the authority to **place liens against lots** for unpaid fees or assessments.

## Legal Remedies and Enforcement

- Non-compliance with regulations can lead to **legal action**.
- The prevailing party in such actions is entitled to recover **reasonable attorney fees, costs, and interest on judgments**.
