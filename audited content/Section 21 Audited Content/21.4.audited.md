# Private Controls and Associations in Subdivided Neighborhoods

<!-- When you're navigating the realm of subdivided neighborhoods, understanding how **private controls** and **associations** work is pivotal. Let's delve into those concepts. -->

In subdivided neighborhoods, understanding how **private controls** and **associations** work is necessary.

## Deed Restrictions (Restrictive Covenants or CCRs)

- **Deed restrictions**, also known as **restrictive covenants** or **CCRs** (covenants, conditions, and restrictions), are rules attached to individual properties.
- These restrictions must be followed by any future owners, not just the current deed holder.
- They can be *more stringent* than local zoning laws but never less restrictive.
- This means limitations imposed by deed restrictions may go beyond what city or county regulations require.

## Subdivision Associations and Homeowner's Associations (HOAs)

- Subdivision associations are typically **non-profit corporate entities**.
- Unit owners are members of these associations, commonly called **homeowner's associations (HOAs)**.
- Members pay **monthly dues** which are used to:
  - Maintain **common areas**
  - Cover various expenses for subdivision upkeep and functionality
- This system creates a structure where everyone contributes to the collective good.

## Powers and Enforcement of Associations

- Associations have power to enforce **private controls** or **deed restrictions**.
- They ensure all unit owners comply with the rules that run with the land.
- This governance model maintains standards across the neighborhood, preserving both its aesthetic appeal and property values.
