# Understanding Reconciliation in Property Appraisal

<!-- When you're conducting an **appraisal**, it's paramount to understand the concept of **reconciliation**. -->

When conducting an **appraisal**, appraisers must understand the concept of **reconciliation**.

## The Three Approaches to Estimating Property Value

<!-- You will use three approaches to estimate a property's value, but remember, you are *not* simply going to average these results. This is because not all data and methods are equally valid and reliable for every property. -->

Appraisers use three approaches to estimate a property's value, but do *not* simply average these results. Not all data and methods are equally valid and reliable for every property.

## The Role of Appraiser Judgment

<!-- This is where your judgment as an **appraiser** comes into play. You should strive to apply all three approaches to the property you're evaluating. However, keep in mind: -->

Appraiser judgment plays a role here. Appraisers should apply all three approaches to the property being evaluated. However:

- Some approaches are more relevant and reliable than others, depending on the type of property.
<!-- - Your skill in discerning which approach holds the most weight for a particular property is crucial. -->
- Skill in discerning which approach holds the most weight for a particular property affects the appraisal.

## Essence of Reconciliation

<!-- The essence of **reconciliation** is to weigh these approaches based on their appropriateness to the subject property, *not* to treat them as equals by default. This nuanced evaluation is what sets apart proficient appraisers from novices. -->

**Reconciliation** involves weighing these approaches based on their appropriateness to the subject property, *not* treating them as equals by default. This evaluation distinguishes proficient appraisers from novices.
