# Understanding Depreciation in Real Estate

<!-- When you're assessing the value of a residential or income-producing property, understanding **depreciation** is crucial. -->

When assessing the value of a residential or income-producing property, understanding **depreciation** is necessary.

## What is Depreciation?

Depreciation is the decline in a property's value over time. This happens for several reasons, including:

- **Natural wear and tear** (aging, weather impacts)
- **Economic changes** (neighborhood changes or market demand changes)

In the context of **property appraisal**, depreciation means the property is worth less money.

## Depreciation from a Tax Perspective

For tax purposes, depreciation works differently:

<!-- - You can claim depreciation as a tax deduction even if your property value goes up. -->
- Property owners can claim depreciation as a tax deduction even if property value goes up.
<!-- - This lets you deduct money for wear and tear on the property. -->
- This allows deducting money for wear and tear on the property.
<!-- - This can lower your taxes even when your property value increases. -->
- This can lower taxes even when property value increases.
