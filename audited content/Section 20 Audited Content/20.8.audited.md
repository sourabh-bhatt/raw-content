# Understanding Appraisal Reports in Real Estate

When an appraiser conducts an **appraisal**, the appraiser produces a document known as an **appraisal report**. This report is the appraiser's professional judgment regarding the **value of a property**. The report is supported by facts and analysis the appraiser has gathered. The complexity of an appraisal report can vary widely:

- Some reports might be straightforward and use a standard template.
- Others could be detailed narratives, rich in analysis and tailored to the specific characteristics of the property and the needs of the client.

## The Uniform Residential Appraisal Report (URAR)

For residential properties, appraisers often use a particular report, especially when working with certain government-related transactions. This is the **Uniform Residential Appraisal Report**, often abbreviated as **URAR**. It is also referred to by its form numbers:

- **Freddie Mac Form 70**
- **Fannie Mae Form 1004**

This standardized form is a requirement for appraisals associated with a wide range of government agency transactions. When completing the URAR, appraisers follow specific guidelines to ensure that all necessary information and analysis are consistently presented. This consistency makes it easier for clients and regulatory bodies to understand and utilize the findings.

## Function of Structured Reporting

Careful and structured reporting provides a **reliable, objective, and well-supported opinion** on the value of a property. This applies in a variety of contexts, including:

- Home purchases
- Refinancing
- Assessments for tax purposes
- Legal disputes
