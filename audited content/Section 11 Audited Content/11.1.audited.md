# Probate and Title Transfer of Real Estate

When someone passes away owning real estate titled in their name, the property's title typically transfers to the person or people designated in their **will**; if there's no will, it goes to their **heirs** as dictated by laws of **intestacy**. However, this transfer of rights is under the **probate court's** jurisdiction, primarily because the deceased may have debts or there could be disputes over the property. To legally pass the title to the new owners, opening a **probate case** with the local Virginia court is required.

## The Probate Estate

The **probate estate** includes all real and personal property owned at death. The handling of real estate within this estate is governed by Virginia law when the property is located in Virginia. Typically, the process begins with the court appointing a **personal representative** (also called an **executor** or **administrator**) to oversee the estate, ensuring all debts and claims are settled. This process covers several steps:

- Validating the will
- Inventorying the estate
- Settling debts and taxes
- Closing the estate
- Distributing the assets to the rightful heirs or **devisees**, who then receive clear title to the property

## Selling Property During Probate

If there is a need to sell the property before the probate is fully settled, this is regulated by Virginia law that must be followed closely. Points include:

- Any sale by heirs or devisees while probate is ongoing is subject to existing probate issues such as **estate taxes** or claims from **creditors**.
- In Virginia, sales by heirs may require the **personal representative's approval**.

## Sales Initiated by Personal Representative

- A **personal representative** generally must obtain a **court order** to sell any real estate.
- If the **will** grants the **personal representative** the power to sell, they may proceed without court consent but must still meet legal requirements, such as:
  - Obtaining **spousal consent**
  - Handling **creditors' claims**
- If the will does not grant this power, a court order must be obtained to approve the sale.
