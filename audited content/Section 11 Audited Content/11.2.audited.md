# Understanding Title Defects in Real Estate Transactions

**Title defects** indicate that the title to the property is not clear or marketable. This situation arises, for instance, if a person attempts to sell property they claim to own, but it's actually owned by someone else. Different types of title defects affect real estate transactions.

## Common Types of Title Defects

Common defects include:

- **Improper Recording of Ownership:** This happens when the property's ownership records are not correctly filed with the appropriate governmental bodies.
- **Missing Signatures:** If a necessary party, like a spouse, didn't sign off on the transaction, the title could be considered defective.
- **Prior Liens and Encumbrances:** For a title to be marketable, it needs to be free of any liens (legal claims) or encumbrances that could affect its transfer. Leftover liens from previous owners can cause problems.
- **Mechanics Liens:** This type of lien occurs if the seller hasn't paid a contractor for improvements made to the property. The unpaid contractor has the right to place a lien on the property.
- **Tax Liens:** Failing to pay property taxes can lead to a tax lien. This happens when the government sells a certificate for the amount of the unpaid taxes, placing a lien on the property.

## Resolving Title Defects

Title defect resolution varies in complexity:

- Some issues, like an old mortgage that's been paid off but never officially cleared from the record, can be resolved by recording a **release document** once proof of payment and lender agreement are obtained.
- Other issues are more challenging, such as when a deed within the title's historical chain lacks all necessary parties' approval. In these instances, initiating a **"quiet title"** legal action might be necessary to clear the defect.

## Contractual Remedies for Title Defects

Typically, real estate contracts allow for a specific period during which the seller must rectify any title defects. If the seller can't clear the title within this timeframe:

- The buyer is usually no longer obligated to proceed with the purchase.
- Any deposits made can be reclaimed.
