Common Law Definitions:
- **General Agent**: An agent authorized to conduct a series of transactions involving continuity of service. Has broad, ongoing authority to act for the principal in all matters connected with a particular business or employment.
- **Special Agent**: An agent authorized to conduct a single transaction or specific act. Has limited authority for a particular purpose or specific transaction.

Property Manager as General Agent: Property managers serving as general agents have continuing authority to perform a wide variety of tasks on behalf of property owners, including routine maintenance, tenant relations, financial management, lease negotiations, and hiring contractors. This is an ongoing relationship with broad authority.

Salesperson as Special Agent: A licensed salesperson working for a broker acts as the broker's special agent (or subagent). The salesperson has limited authority to assist with specific transactions under broker supervision. The broker maintains overall responsibility.

The content accurately distinguishes between property managers as general agents (broad ongoing authority for continuous property oversight) and licensed salespersons as special agents of their employing brokers (limited authority for specific transactions under supervision).

# Understanding the Role of a General Agent in Real Estate

## The General Agent in Property Management

<!-- Imagine you're stepping into the role of a **property manager** with the responsibility to oversee a real estate project continuously. In this role, you become what's known as a **general agent** of the **property owner**. **Property managers** with responsibility to oversee real estate projects continuously serve as **general agents** of the **property owner**. General agents hold authority to execute various tasks on the owner's behalf for effective property management. This can include:

- Routine maintenance
- Tenant relations
- Financial management
- Other duties necessary for property oversight

## The Special Agent in Real Estate Sales

<!-- Similarly, if you're a **licensed salesperson** working for a **broker**, you serve as a special agent for your employing broker. **Licensed salespersons** working for **brokers** serve as special agents for their employing brokers. This relationship provides limited authority to assist with real estate transactions under broker supervision. These activities bridge the gap between property buyers and sellers, ensuring smooth and efficient transactions.
