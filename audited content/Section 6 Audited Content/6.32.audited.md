# Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) in Mortgages and Deeds of Trust

**Private Mortgage Insurance** (**PMI**) operates within mortgages and deeds of trust frameworks.

## What is PMI?

Mortgages exceeding *80% of property value* present higher risk to lenders. Lenders require borrowers to purchase **PMI** as protection against mortgage default. This insurance protects lenders when borrowers cannot repay mortgages.

## Costs Associated with PMI

Mortgages requiring PMI involve two charge types:

- **Initial insurance premium**: A one-time payment due at closing.
- **Annual insurance charge**: A smaller, ongoing fee paid annually for as long as the insurance remains active.

## When Can PMI Be Cancelled?

**PMI** can be cancelled under specific conditions:

- **Borrower-Initiated Cancellation:**
  - Borrowers who maintain payments and reduce mortgage balances to *80% of initial purchase price* or *appraised value at purchase* (whichever is lower) can **request** PMI cancellation.
  - Borrowers must initiate this cancellation process.

- **Automatic Cancellation:**
  - Federal law requires lenders to **automatically cancel PMI** once mortgage balances drop to *78% of property value*.
  - **High risk** loans require automatic cancellation when balances reach *77%*.
  - Automatic cancellation requires no borrower action.

## PMI Applications

PMI knowledge supports efficient mortgage management and cost control. This information helps prevent unnecessary spending and supports mortgage payment decisions.
