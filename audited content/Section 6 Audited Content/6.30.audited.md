# Strict Foreclosure Process

**Note: Strict foreclosure is not used in Virginia. Virginia uses deed of trust foreclosures. This information is provided for general real estate knowledge only.**

**Strict foreclosures** apply in limited states, representing a specialized foreclosure method.

## Initiation of Strict Foreclosure

- The process begins when **proper notice** is served to the **borrower**. This step requires correct execution.
- After notice is served, certain paperwork must be filed with a **court** to advance the process.

## Court Involvement and Deadlines

- Once the court is involved, it sets a **deadline** by which the borrower must repay the **overdue debt**.
- This deadline represents the borrower's **final chance** to keep their property.

## Consequences of Missing the Deadline

- If the borrower fails to meet the deadline, the court awards **full title** of the property directly to the **lender**.
- The lender then becomes the outright owner of the property.

## Impact on Borrower's Rights

- One of the harshest aspects of strict foreclosure is the loss of the borrower's **equity of redemption** and any **statutory redemption rights**.
- These rights, which might have allowed the borrower to reclaim their property, are completely wiped out once the court passes its judgment.

This process demonstrates deadline requirements in foreclosure proceedings and the consequences of failing to satisfy debt obligations within specified timeframes.
