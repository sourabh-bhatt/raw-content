# Foreclosure and Its Implications in Real Estate

When a borrower fails to keep up with their loan payments, the lender may start a **foreclosure** process. In foreclosure, the **lender** has the right to auction off the property used as **collateral** for the loan to cover the **outstanding debt**.

## Virginia Foreclosure Process

**Virginia is a title theory state.** This means Virginia generally allows for a quicker foreclosure process compared to lien theory states.

Virginia allows both **judicial** and **non-judicial foreclosure**, but non-judicial is more common. In Virginia, the **deed of trust** includes a **power of sale clause**. This clause lets the lender sell the property without going to court.

## Foreclosure Process and State Theories

The speed of the foreclosure process varies depending on the state's legal framework:

- **Title Theory States:** Generally allow for a quicker foreclosure process.
- **Lien Theory States:** Typically have a slower foreclosure process.

State theory classification determines foreclosure timelines.

## Auction Sale Outcomes

- If the property sells for **more than the amount owed**, the **excess funds** are returned to the borrower who defaulted on the loan. This return of funds represents part of foreclosure proceedings.

- If the auction sale does **not cover all claims** against the property, any **subordinate liens** (also called **junior liens**, which are ranked below the **primary mortgage** in repayment priority) are wiped out.

## Borrower Property Repurchase Effects

If delinquent borrowers **buy back properties at auctions**, they remain responsible for junior liens. This affects:

- It affects bidding strategies at foreclosure auctions.
- It influences how outstanding debts on foreclosed properties are managed.

These factors affect borrower and investor decisions in foreclosure auctions and associated financial responsibilities.
