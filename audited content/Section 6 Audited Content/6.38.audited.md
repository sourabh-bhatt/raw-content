# Assumption of Mortgages and Deeds of Trust in Real Estate Transactions

The **assumption of mortgages and deeds of trust (DOT)** operates within real estate transactions and affects buyers who take over existing loans.

## What is Mortgage Assumption?

**Assuming the mortgage** occurs when buyers take over existing mortgages or DOT on purchased properties. Mortgage assumptions can benefit buyers when existing mortgages offer **interest rates** below current market rates.

## Responsibilities When Assuming a Mortgage

Assuming mortgages involves taking property ownership and accepting **personal commitment** to repay existing loans in full. This includes:

- Mortgage assumptions place buyer names alongside or in place of original borrower names.
- When loan payments fail and **foreclosure sales** don't cover owed amounts, buyers face direct responsibility for settling remaining debt.
- This remaining debt may be enforced through a **deficiency judgment**, where a court orders you to make the payments.

## Joint and Several Liability

Lenders often hold both buyers and sellers as **jointly and severally liable** for loans, meaning:

- Lenders can pursue either party or both to recover unpaid debt.
- Mortgage assumptions create **co-guarantor** relationships with original borrowers.

## Mortgage Assumption Applications

Mortgage assumptions affect buyers through:

- Full **financial responsibility** assumption for existing loans.
- Potential **financial implications** including unpaid debt liability.
- Risk management requirements during real estate transactions.

These factors affect mortgage and deed of trust assumption decisions.
