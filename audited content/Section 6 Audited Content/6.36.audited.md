# Deeds of Trust and Trustee Sales in Title Theory States

**Deeds of trust** within **title theory states** like Virginia establish procedures when borrowers fail to meet obligations under deed of trust agreements.

## Roles and Responsibilities

- The **borrower** (also called the **trustor**) must adhere to the terms laid out in the note.
- If the borrower **defaults**, the **lender** (also known as the **beneficiary**) must provide undeniable proof to a third party, the **trustee**, that the borrower has not met their obligations.

## Trustee Sale Process

1. **Notice of Default**
   - Upon confirmation of default, the lender instructs the trustee to initiate a **Trustee Sale**.
   - A **notice of default** is officially recorded, making it a matter of public record.

2. **Waiting Period**
   - There is a mandatory waiting period of **at least 14 days** to allow for due process.
   - During this time, details about the impending sale are:
     - Published in a public newspaper.
     - Displayed in public areas.
   - This ensures transparency and fairness, providing potential buyers with accessible information.

3. **Public Auction**
   - After the waiting period, the property is auctioned at a public event, usually in the county where the property is located.
   - This auction is the final opportunity for the sale of the property under these circumstances.

## Key Differences: Title Theory vs. Lien Theory States

- In **title theory states** like Virginia, once the Trustee Sale process is complete, the borrower generally **cannot redeem** the property.
- This contrasts with **lien theory states**, where mortgages are more common and borrowers may have redemption rights after foreclosure.

## Trustee Sale Completion

- The highest bidder at the auction receives a **Trustee's Deed**, which signifies the transfer of property ownership under the auction terms.

This process affects real estate transactions involving deeds of trust in title theory states.
