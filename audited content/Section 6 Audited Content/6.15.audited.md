# Mortgages vs. Deeds of Trust: Understanding State Preferences in Real Estate Financing

States demonstrate different preferences between **mortgages** and **deeds of trust** in real estate financing. These distinctions affect financing processes and professional guidance.

## Lien Theory States: Mortgages

- The majority of U.S. states prefer **mortgages**.
- These states operate under **lien theory**.
- Under lien theory:
  - The **legal title** of the property remains with the homeowner.
  - The property is subject to a **lien**, a legal claim against the property to secure repayment of the loan.
  - If the loan isn't repaid, the lender can **foreclose** on the property to recoup their investment.

## Title Theory States: Deeds of Trust

- A minority of states use **deeds of trust** and are known as **title theory** states.
- In these states:
  - The property title is placed in a **trust** until the loan is fully paid off.
  - A **trustee**, a neutral third party, holds the title for the benefit of the lender.
  - If the borrower defaults, the trustee has the authority to sell the property on behalf of the lender to settle the debt.

### Example: Virginia

- Virginia is an example of an **intermediate theory** state that uses deeds of trust.
- In Virginia, the borrower keeps legal title to the property until default occurs.
- The trustee holds the legal title in trust but the borrower retains equitable title.
- The deed of trust mechanism accelerates the process for lenders to recoup their investments if a borrower defaults.
- This process is typically faster than the foreclosure process in lien theory states.

## State Theory Applications

These differences affect real estate transaction navigation and advisory services across states. Lien theory or title theory state classifications impact financing options and professional advice.
