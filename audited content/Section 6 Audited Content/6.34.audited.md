# The Acceleration Clause in Mortgages and Deeds of Trust

The **Acceleration Clause** operates within **mortgages** and **deeds of trust (DOT)** between **borrowers** and **lenders** in real estate markets.

## What is the Acceleration Clause?

The Acceleration Clause is a loan document provision that protects lender interests through the following mechanism:

- The borrower must follow the agreed terms of the **mortgage** or **DOT**.
- If the borrower breaks these terms - like missing payments or breaking **loan covenants** - the Acceleration Clause activates.
- This clause allows the lender to demand the **entire loan balance immediately**.
- The entire amount becomes due immediately instead of continuing to the original maturity date. This process is called **"accelerating the loan."**

## Enforceability of the Clause

- The Acceleration Clause must be **explicitly included** in the loan agreement to be enforceable.
- It cannot be implied or assumed.
- Loan documents require careful review to confirm this clause's presence.

## Activation and Notice Requirements

When lenders activate the Acceleration Clause:

- Lenders cannot surprise borrowers without warning.
- The lender must give the borrower **proper notice**.
- This notice is typically legally required and informs borrowers of default status.
- Borrowers receive specific timeframes to **"cure the default"** by fixing problems such as catching up on missed payments or addressing covenant breaches.
- This period allows borrowers to address problems.

## Acceleration Clause Applications

The Acceleration Clause affects real estate lending through:

- Demonstrating loan agreement compliance requirements.
- Showing consequences of borrower default on loan terms.
- Illustrating effects for both borrowers and lenders in real estate transactions.

These concepts affect the real estate lending process.
