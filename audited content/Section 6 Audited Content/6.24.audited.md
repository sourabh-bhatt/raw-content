# Understanding the Deed of Trust (DOT) in Property Financing

A **Deed of Trust (DOT)** involves a neutral third party called a **trustee** who holds the property title. The trustee holds the **title** to the financed property to protect the lender and ensure loan repayment. This arrangement differs from other loan types.

## Key Parties Involved

- **Borrower (Trustor):** The person financing the property.
- **Lender:** The party providing the loan.
- **Trustee:** A neutral third party who holds the legal title temporarily.

## How the Deed of Trust Works

- The property title stays in the **trustee's name** until the borrower pays off the loan in full.
- Virginia is a **title theory** state. This means the trustee holds legal title while the loan exists.
- When the loan is fully repaid, the trustee transfers the title back to the borrower. This confirms clear ownership.

## Trustee's Authority in Case of Default

- If the borrower fails to repay the loan, the trustee has the authority to **sell the property on behalf of the lender**.
- This power demonstrates the trustee's role and borrower loan obligations.

## Differences Between Deed of Trust and Traditional Mortgage

- In a traditional mortgage, the **legal title** usually remains with the property owner.
- In a Deed of Trust, the legal title is held temporarily by the trustee.
- The Deed of Trust allows for **non-judicial foreclosure through a power of sale**. This gives lenders a more streamlined way to recover the loan amount if repayments fail.

These differences explain Deed of Trust usage in Virginia. This structure provides a streamlined and secure framework for property financing.
