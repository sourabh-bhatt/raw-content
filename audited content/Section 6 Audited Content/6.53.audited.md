# Understanding Acceleration in Mortgages and Deeds of Trust

**Acceleration** in **mortgages** and **deeds of trust (DOT)** allows lenders to demand immediate payment of the entire loan balance under specific conditions.

## What is Acceleration?

Acceleration occurs when a borrower violates any **covenants** or agreements within the mortgage or deed of trust, giving the lender the right to demand the entire loan balance immediately. The expected life of the loan ends abruptly, requiring immediate payment of the full amount.

## Acceleration Clause Requirement

- For a lender to take this action, there must be an explicit **acceleration clause** included within the loan document.
- This is not an automatic power the lender possesses; it needs to be a defined condition of the borrowing agreement.

## Notice and Cure Period

Before any acceleration can take place, the lender must give the debtor **proper notice**. This notice:

- Is often a legal requirement, not just a courtesy.
- Outlines the specifics of the **default**.
- Provides a specified period in which the borrower can **rectify** or "**cure**" the default.

This curing period offers an opportunity to avoid the full loan balance becoming due immediately.

## Where Acceleration Clauses Appear

Acceleration clauses can appear in various contracts related to real estate financing, including:

- Mortgages
- Deeds of Trust agreements
- Leases
- Land Installment Contracts (also known as **installment sales contracts**)

These clauses directly impact how and when the balance of a loan might suddenly become due.
