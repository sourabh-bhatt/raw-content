# Acceleration Clause in Mortgages and Deeds of Trust

The **acceleration clause** operates within **mortgages** and **deeds of trust** frameworks.

## What is an Acceleration Clause?

An acceleration clause allows the **lender** to demand the full balance of the loan immediately instead of waiting for the loan term to end. This clause is typically invoked when the **borrower**:

- Fails to keep up with their payments
- Causes damage to the property
- Neglects necessary repairs, putting the property's value at risk

## Purpose of the Acceleration Clause

This clause acts as a **protective measure** to ensure the lender's investment is not jeopardized by the borrower's actions or inactions.

### Example

Example: You are the lender, and your borrower has missed several payments and allowed the property to fall into disrepair. By invoking the acceleration clause, you can demand the entire remaining loan balance immediately, rather than waiting for the original loan term to expire.
