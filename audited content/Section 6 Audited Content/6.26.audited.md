# Subordination Clause in Junior Mortgages

The **subordination clause** operates as a component within **junior mortgages**.

## What is a Subordination Clause?

When existing **first mortgages** are repaid or renegotiated, the **subordination clause** ensures junior mortgages remain in secondary positions rather than advancing to first mortgage status.

## Why Accept a Subordinate Position?

Lenders who take on junior mortgages typically do so with the understanding that they're in a riskier position, which entitles them to charge higher **interest rates**. The logic here is straightforward:

- Being further down the pecking order means there's a greater chance they won't fully recover their funds if the borrower defaults on the loan.

## Subordination Clause vs. Subordination Agreement

**Subordination clauses** and **subordination agreements** serve different purposes:

- **Subordination Clause:** Automatically keeps a junior mortgage in a lower priority position relative to a first mortgage.
- **Subordination Agreement:** A specific agreement where a higher priority mortgage (like the first mortgage) agrees to step down in priority in favor of a new or future lien.

### Use of Subordination Agreement

This arrangement can be particularly useful in certain financing scenarios, ensuring that new loans or liens can be accommodated within the existing structure of debt on a property.

These distinctions affect real estate financing structures and decisions.
