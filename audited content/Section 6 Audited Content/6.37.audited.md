# Foreclosure Process Under Deeds of Trust in Virginia

The **foreclosure process** operates within **Deeds of Trust** in **Virginia**. Virginia is a **title theory state** using deeds of trust, offering quicker and more cost-effective foreclosure processes compared to **judicial foreclosures** in other states.

## How the Foreclosure Process Works

When a borrower defaults on a deed of trust, the lender can start foreclosure. The **lender (beneficiary)** must prove the borrower **(trustor)** defaulted on the loan terms. The lender then directs the **trustee** to sell the property. This process leads to what's called a **Trustee Sale**.

### Foreclosure Process Steps

1. **Recording a Notice of Default**
   - The lender records a formal notice of default in public records.
   - This serves as an official indication that the borrower has not met their obligations.

2. **Mandatory Waiting Period**
   - At least 14 days in Virginia.
   - Allows the borrower time to resolve the default before further action.

3. **Publicizing the Sale**
   - Details of the upcoming sale are advertised in a public newspaper.
   - Notices are also placed in public spots for a set period to ensure adequate exposure.

4. **Public Auction**
   - The property is sold at a public auction, usually held in the county where the property is located.
   - The property is sold to the highest bidder, completing the foreclosure process under the Deed of Trust.

These steps establish procedural and timing requirements for foreclosure processes in Virginia under **Deeds of Trust**.
