# Right of Redemption in Real Estate

The **Right of Redemption** operates within **deeds of trust** in Virginia real estate law.

## What is the Right of Redemption?

Virginia's Right of Redemption is called the **equity of redemption**. This right allows original property owners to pay off outstanding debt and reclaim property **before** foreclosure sales occur. Virginia follows the **deed of trust theory**, where this right exists only until the foreclosure sale.

- In Virginia, there is **no** redemption period after the foreclosure sale.
- Once the foreclosure sale happens, redemption rights end.

## How Does the Redemption Process Work in Virginia?

- Before the foreclosure sale, owners can redeem property by paying the full debt amount.
- At the foreclosure sale, the highest bidder immediately becomes the new owner.
- The winning bidder receives a **trustee's deed** that transfers full ownership rights.
- After the foreclosure sale, the original owner has no right to redeem the property.

This pre-sale redemption opportunity constitutes the **equity of redemption**.

## Right of Redemption Applications

This right affects real estate processes in several ways:

- It affects the process of buying and selling properties under certain conditions.
- It gives homeowners a chance to retain ownership of their homes before the foreclosure sale.
- Virginia's system provides finality at the foreclosure sale with no post-sale redemption rights.

These redemption rights affect property transaction strategies and outcomes.
