# Understanding Reserve Accounts in Real Estate Finance

The **Reserve Account** operates within **mortgages** and **deeds of trust** as a financial mechanism that lenders use to ensure timely payment of property-related expenses.

## What is a Reserve Account?

Borrowers securing mortgages often face lender requirements to establish a **Reserve Account**. This account holds funds to pay for items such as:

- **Real estate taxes**
- **Hazard insurance**
- Any deferred maintenance the property might need

Reserve Accounts are also called **escrow accounts** or **impound accounts**. These names reflect the account's purpose: maintaining funds until needed for required expenses.

## How Does a Reserve Account Work?

- **Initial Deposit:** Property closings may require lump sum payments into this account to ensure adequate funds for upcoming expenses.

- **Monthly Contributions:** After initial deposits, borrowers contribute calculated monthly portions of estimated taxes and insurance costs alongside mortgage payments.

This process ensures that when the time comes to pay property taxes or insurance premiums, the funds are readily available in the Reserve Account, preventing any risk of late payments.

Reserve Account knowledge supports property ownership financial management and prevents unexpected payment issues.
