# Foreclosure Processes in Mortgage Law

When dealing with **mortgages** and **deeds of trust**, if a borrower fails to make payments, there are two key types of **foreclosure** processes depending on the state's legal framework. These are known as **judicial foreclosure** and **non-judicial foreclosure**.

## Types of Foreclosure

The two main foreclosure processes are:

1. **Judicial Foreclosure**
   - The process is handled through the **court system**.
   - The lender files a lawsuit against the **defaulting borrower**.
   - If the court rules in the lender's favor, the property is sold under court supervision.
   - The proceeds from the sale go toward paying off the debt.

2. **Non-Judicial Foreclosure (Power of Sale)**
   - This is the most common method in Virginia.
   - Virginia primarily uses **deeds of trust** rather than mortgages.
   - The deed of trust includes a **power of sale clause**.
   - The **trustee** can sell the property without going to court.
   - The trustee must follow specific notice requirements before the sale.
   - The property is sold at a **trustee sale** or **foreclosure auction**.

## Virginia's Foreclosure Process

In Virginia:
- Most residential loans use deeds of trust with power of sale provisions
- The trustee must give proper notice to the borrower
- The sale must be advertised in a local newspaper
- The foreclosure sale is typically held at the courthouse or the property

## Foreclosure Process Benefits

Understanding these processes provides benefits:

- Guides clients through potential pitfalls related to mortgage defaults.
- Different **state laws** impact how foreclosures are handled, affecting the buying or selling process.
- Enables advising clients on their rights and obligations when facing financial difficulties.

Knowledge of judicial and non-judicial foreclosure specifics allows real estate professionals to better support clients during challenging financial situations.
