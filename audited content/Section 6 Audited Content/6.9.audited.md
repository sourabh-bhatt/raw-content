# Understanding the Exculpatory Clause in Mortgages and Deeds of Trust

The **Exculpatory Clause** functions within **mortgages** and **deeds of trust** as part of the lending and borrowing process.

## What is an Exculpatory Clause?

An exculpatory clause is a provision in mortgage or deed of trust agreements that protects **borrowers from personal liability for any deficiency**.

## Deficiency Judgment Explained

A **deficiency judgment** is what a court issues when the sale of collateral doesn't cover the full amount of debt owed to the lender. 

Example: If a house sale falls short, the borrower could be personally liable for the difference.

However, with an **exculpatory clause** included in the agreement, the clause protects the borrower by stating:

> "If the sale of the collateral doesn't cover the debt, the borrower won't be held personally responsible for the shortfall."

## Exculpatory Clause Applications

The exculpatory clause affects **financial responsibilities and risks** that borrowers assume. In volatile markets where property values fluctuate, the clause provides borrower protection.

Note that exculpatory clauses are uncommon in Virginia residential mortgages. Most residential loans hold borrowers personally liable.

This clause affects **risk management** during the lending process.
