# Understanding Protected Classes in Fair Housing

<!-- When navigating the real estate landscape, it is crucial to be aware of the **fair housing laws** that safeguard against discrimination. -->
**Fair housing laws** safeguard against discrimination. These laws ensure that everyone has equal access to housing, regardless of certain characteristics. Below are the specifics of these protections.

## Protected Classes

### Race

It is prohibited to discriminate against anyone based on their **race**. This includes, but is not limited to, individuals identified as Caucasian, African American, or Hispanic. A person's race should never influence professional decisions or actions in real estate.

### Color

Discrimination based on the **color** of someone's skin is strictly forbidden. The shade or tone of a person's skin cannot be a factor in any housing-related decisions.

### Religion

Clients' **religious beliefs** are protected. Whether someone practices Islam, Christianity, Buddhism, or any other religion, it cannot influence professional interactions or the opportunities provided.

### National Origin

Discrimination based on **national origin** is prohibited. This refers to the country or region where a person comes from, such as China, Germany, or Brazil. Everyone deserves equal treatment in the housing market regardless of heritage.

### Sex

Fair housing laws protect individuals based on **sex**. Under federal law, this refers to male or female status. Virginia may provide additional protections for **gender identity** and **sexual orientation**. Treat all individuals equitably, irrespective of these characteristics.

### Familial Status

This protection applies to families with children under the age of 18. It is not permissible to market or sell properties as "child-free" areas unless a specific exemption applies (e.g., retirement communities). This protection also covers pregnant individuals or those securing legal custody of a minor.

- Setting conditions based on familial status, such as requiring buyers to not have children or not plan to have any in the future, violates the **Fair Housing Act**.

### Disability

Individuals with **disabilities** are protected under the Fair Housing Act. This includes both physical and mental impairments that substantially limit major life activities. Important points include:

- Disabilities may be visible or non-visible (e.g., HIV/AIDS, cancer, recovering alcoholism).
- Illegal drug use is *not* protected under this definition.
