# Fair Housing Discrimination Complaint Investigation Process

<!-- When you come across a complaint related to **Fair Housing discrimination**, it's important to understand how the **investigation process** gets underway. Knowing these steps can help you navigate potential future situations as a professional in the field. -->

Complaints related to **Fair Housing discrimination** initiate an **investigation process**. Knowledge of these steps helps professionals navigate potential future situations.

## Initiation and Timeline of the Investigation

- After a complaint is received, **HUD (U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development)** responsible for these matters must start the investigation within **30 days**.
- The goal is to complete the investigation within **100 days**.
- If the investigation cannot be completed within this timeframe, both the complainant and the accused party will be informed **in writing** explaining the delay.

## Investigation Procedures

During the investigation, **HUD** has the authority to:

- Conduct **interviews** with anyone who might have relevant information, including the complainant or their acquaintances.
- Request access to any **documents** or records that could provide insight into the discrimination claim.
- Administer interviews **under oath** to ensure seriousness and accuracy.
- Issue **subpoenas** to compel individuals to testify or provide necessary documents.

## Final Investigative Report

At the conclusion of the investigation, **HUD compiles** a **final investigative report** which includes:

- Names of **witnesses** and the dates they were contacted.
- A summary of interactions with both the complainant and the accused party.
- Descriptions of pertinent records examined.
- Summaries of witness statements.
- Answers to any formal questions posed during the investigation.

## Access to Investigation Materials

- Upon request, once the investigation is officially concluded, **HUD must provide access** to the investigation materials and the final report to both the complainant and the accused party.
- This ensures **transparency** and allows all parties to review the findings.
