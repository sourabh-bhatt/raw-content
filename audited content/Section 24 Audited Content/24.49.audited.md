# Familial Status and Fair Housing Protections in Real Estate Contracts

Example: A seller agreeing to sell their property to potential buyers. The seller makes this agreement on one specific condition: the buyers **must not currently have children** and **must not plan to have any in the future**. 

## Conflict with Fair Housing Laws

This requirement directly conflicts with the protections regarding **familial status** outlined in both the **Virginia** and **Federal Fair Housing Acts**. Because of this conflict, such a condition is **unenforceable** and considered **null and void**.

## Validity of the Overall Contract

- If the rest of the contract is perfectly valid and adheres to all other laws and regulations, the overall contract remains **enforceable** despite the problematic condition.
- If it is discovered later that the buyers do have children, they have the right to **overlook this void condition** and proceed with enforcing the main aspects of the contract.

## Protection Against Discrimination

This principle protects individuals and families from **discrimination based on their current familial status or plans to change their familial status in the future**, ensuring **equal housing opportunities** for everyone.
