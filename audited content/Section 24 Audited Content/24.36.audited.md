# Handling Fair Housing Complaints and Conciliation Agreements

When handling a **fair housing complaint**, a specific period exists. This period starts from the moment such a complaint is filed and continues up until a charge is filed or the complaint is dismissed by HUD. During this time, participation in **conciliation** regarding the complaint is strongly encouraged when possible.

## Conciliation Agreements

<!-- Let's break down what happens with conciliation agreements, as understanding this is crucial for you: -->
Conciliation agreements involve several key elements:

1. **Creation of a Conciliation Agreement**
   - This agreement results from the conciliation efforts and is a pact between the person who filed the complaint and the respondent.
   - The agreement requires **HUD approval** to be valid, meaning agreement between the parties alone is not sufficient.

2. **Binding Arbitration**
   - Sometimes, a conciliation agreement includes a clause for **binding arbitration**.
   - If the dispute proceeds to arbitration, any decision made-including ordering payment of damages-must be followed.

3. **Public Disclosure**
   - Normally, the details of a conciliation agreement are made public.
   - However, if both the complainant and respondent agree, and HUD sees no need for disclosure, the agreement may be kept private.

4. **Breach of Agreement**
   - If there is reasonable belief that the respondent has violated the conciliation agreement, HUD can take serious action.
   - This may include recommending to the **U.S. Department of Justice** that legal action be taken against the violator.
