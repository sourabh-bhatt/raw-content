# Virginia Fair Housing Law Exemptions

<!-- When navigating the complexities of **Virginia Fair Housing laws**, it's crucial to understand certain **exemptions** that might apply. This knowledge can significantly impact how you conduct real estate transactions. -->

**Virginia Fair Housing laws** include certain **exemptions** that might apply. This knowledge can impact how real estate transactions are conducted.

## Exemptions for Single-Family Houses

- If a **single-family house** is sold or rented by its owner (**For Sale By Owner - FSBO**), federal Fair Housing laws might not apply if the owner does not own more than **three such houses** at any given time.
- Specifically, if the owner **does not live in the house** or was **not the most recent resident before selling**, they can only use this exemption **once within a 24-month period**.

- **Note**: Virginia Fair Housing Act may still apply even when federal exemptions exist.

## Owner-Occupied Multi-Family Dwellings

- For dwellings designed for **no more than four families living independently**, the laws do not apply if the **owner occupies one of the units as their residence**.
- This exemption allows for a more personal management approach to tenant selection.
<!-- - **Important Note**: Virginia Fair Housing Act may still apply even when federal exemptions exist. -->
- **Note**: Virginia Fair Housing Act may still apply even when federal exemptions exist.

## Religious and Nonprofit Exemptions

- **Religious organizations** or **nonprofit institutions** may sell, rent, or manage occupancy based on **religious affiliations** without violating Fair Housing laws, provided it is for a **non-commercial purpose**.

## Private Membership Clubs

- **Private membership clubs** that are **not open to the public** may limit lodging to members only.
- These limitations must align with the club's **primary, non-commercial objectives**.

## Sex-Based Occupancy Rules

- **Educational, healthcare, religious, or correctional facilities** operated by private or state-supported institutions may impose **sex-based occupancy rules** for single-family residences or units they own or manage.
- This also applies to **restroom facilities** within these units or buildings.

## Criminal History and Threat Assessments

- Fair Housing laws **do not protect individuals convicted** of illegal manufacture or distribution of controlled substances.
- Owners have the right to **deny or limit housing** to individuals who present a **clear threat to others or property**.

- During the rental application process, owners may:
  - **Inquire about an applicant's criminal history**.
  - **Request consent for a criminal record check**.
  - **Charge the applicant for any costs** incurred during this process.
- A history of convictions related to harm to persons or property can justify rental denial if the individual is deemed a threat.

## Occupancy Limitations

- Occupancy limits set by **local, state, or federal guidelines** are respected under Virginia Fair Housing laws.
- Property owners or managers are encouraged to establish **occupancy and safety standards** considering:
  - The **number and size of sleeping areas**.
  - The **overall size of the dwelling**.

## Income Source Exemptions

- **Note**: Virginia Fair Housing Act includes source of income as a protected class. The following applies to federal law exemptions only:
- Under federal law, an owner may deny or limit occupancy based on the **source of a potential tenant's income** under certain conditions:
  - The owner must not own more than **four rental units** at the time.
  - If the owner, individually or through a business entity, has more than a **10% interest in over four rental units**, this exemption does **not** apply.
- **Virginia law may provide additional protections** beyond federal requirements.
