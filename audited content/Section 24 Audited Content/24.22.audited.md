# Fair Housing Laws and Violations

**Fair Housing statutes** structure the ethical and legal landscape for real estate practice. The **Fair Housing Act**, along with its amendments, ensures everyone has equitable access to housing without discrimination. Various violations must be avoided to uphold these principles.

## Common Fair Housing Violations to Avoid

- **Steering**: This involves guiding prospective buyers or renters towards or away from specific neighborhoods based on their membership in a *protected class*. <!-- It's a subtle way of segregating communities and one that you must steer clear of. --> This practice segregates communities and must be avoided.

- **Blockbusting**: This occurs when someone exploits homeowners' fears of neighborhood demographic changes to persuade them to sell their properties hastily, often at a loss. For example, warning homeowners that a certain group is moving into the neighborhood to induce panic selling.

- **Redlining**: The practice of denying or limiting financial services to certain neighborhoods based on racial or ethnic composition.

## Legal Obligations Under Fair Housing Laws

<!-- You have a legal obligation to: -->

Real estate professionals have legal obligations to:

- Not refuse to sell, rent, finance, or insure a property based on someone's inclusion in a *protected class*.
- Not refuse to deal or negotiate with any person based on discriminatory reasons.
- Treat all potential clients equally and impartially.

## Additional Prohibited Practices

- Quoting terms or conditions of sale or rental that discriminate.
- Misrepresenting the availability of a dwelling.
- Denying someone membership to the **Multiple Listing Service (MLS)**.
- Making, printing, or publishing any advertisement that suggests a preference, limitation, or discrimination against a protected class.
