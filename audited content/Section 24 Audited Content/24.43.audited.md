# Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA) and Credit Rights

<!-- When exploring the realm of credit, the **Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA)** serves as a critical guide. This federal law safeguards you from discrimination when applying for credit based on **race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age** (as long as you're legally an adult), or if you rely on **public assistance**. -->
The **Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA)** provides federal protection from discrimination when applying for credit based on **race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age** (for legally adult applicants), or reliance on **public assistance**.

<!-- It's crucial that lenders notify you in writing about the reasons for any denial or termination of credit within **30 days** after you apply. This law encompasses various creditors, including **banks, retail stores, credit unions**, and also applies to businesses seeking credit. -->
Lenders must notify applicants in writing about the reasons for any denial or termination of credit within **30 days** after application. This law encompasses various creditors, including **banks, retail stores, credit unions**, and also applies to businesses seeking credit.

## Income Assessment Guidelines

Lenders are prohibited from discounting certain types of income, such as:

- **Public assistance income**
- Income from **part-time employment**

## Examples of ECOA Violations and Allowances

- A lender **cannot discount a woman's salary** because of potential maternity leave.
- They cannot provide **less favorable terms** strictly based on non-credit related factors.
- However, a lender **is allowed to consider age** under specific conditions, such as understanding a potential drop in income due to retirement.
