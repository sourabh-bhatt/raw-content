# Addressing Housing Discrimination Under the Virginia Fair Housing Law

<!-- Imagine you're in a scenario where you've learned that a real estate broker, salesperson, brokerage firm, or their representatives have potentially taken part in housing discrimination practices outlawed by the *Virginia Fair Housing Law*. Example: When a real estate broker, salesperson, brokerage firm, or their representatives have potentially taken part in housing discrimination practices outlawed by the *Virginia Fair Housing Law*, steps can be taken to address this issue. The process typically unfolds as follows.

## Initial Complaint and Investigation

- If a complaint comes to the Board's attention, or if the Board uncovers discriminatory practices through its own investigation, and there is reasonable evidence to believe discrimination has occurred, the Board's first step is to attempt resolution through discussion and mediation. 
<!-- - This mediation is a critical first attempt to resolve the issue amicably. -->
- This mediation represents the first attempt to resolve the issue amicably.

## Administrative Hearing

- If mediation fails, the Board may proceed to hold an *administrative hearing*.
- The purpose of this hearing is to determine whether the real estate license(s) involved should be revoked, suspended, or not renewed.
- The accused party will receive a written report detailing the investigation's scope, findings, and conclusions at least 10 days before the hearing, allowing adequate preparation.

## Court Verdict and Board Action

- If a real estate professional is found guilty in court of violating any part of the *Virginia Fair Housing Law*, the Board is notified of the verdict.
- Upon notification, the Board is obligated to consider suspending or revoking the license of the guilty party.
