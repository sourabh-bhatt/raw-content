# Stigmatized Properties in Real Estate

<!-- When you're navigating the world of real estate, you're bound to encounter a variety of properties with unique histories and characteristics. -->

Real estate includes a variety of properties with unique histories and characteristics. Among these, **stigmatized properties** stand out due to incidents or conditions (such as *murder, suicide,* or *purported haunting*) that, while not affecting the property's physical structure, might influence its value because of **psychological impacts**.

## Legal Obligations and Disclosure

- There is **no overarching federal law** or common law requirement to investigate, inspect, verify, or disclose if a property is considered stigmatized.
- However, **Virginia law is specific** about stigmatized property disclosure:
  - **Virginia Code Â§ 55.1-703** states that sellers and agents are **not required** to disclose that a property was the site of a homicide, suicide, or felony.
  - Agents are also **not required** to disclose that an occupant had HIV/AIDS.
  - However, if **directly asked** about these matters, agents must answer **truthfully**.
  - Agents cannot volunteer false information about stigmatizing events.
<!-- - It is crucial to understand these **specific Virginia regulations** for your practice. -->
- These **specific Virginia regulations** apply to real estate practice.

## Federal Law Considerations

- Although there is no universal federal statute addressing stigmatized properties, important laws like the **Fair Housing Act** influence disclosure practices.
- For example, the Fair Housing Act **prohibits disclosure of an individual's HIV/AIDS status**, demonstrating federal guidance on sensitive information in real estate transactions.
