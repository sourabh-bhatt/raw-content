# Enforcement of Fair Housing Statutes

<!-- Understanding how fair housing statutes are enforced is crucial for compliance and protection against discrimination. -->

Fair housing statute enforcement provides compliance and protection against discrimination. Two primary governmental bodies are responsible for this enforcement: the **Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD)** and the **Department of Justice (DOJ)**.

## Role of the Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD)

- HUD has the authority to take legal action against violations of fair housing laws through its **administrative forum**.
- Cases of discrimination or violations can be brought before an **administrative law judge** for a hearing.
- This process ensures complaints are addressed in a structured and legal manner.

## Role of the Department of Justice (DOJ)

- The DOJ handles enforcement at the **federal court** level.
- It can prosecute complaints of fair housing violations, providing a legal pathway to address and rectify issues nationally.

## Proactive Enforcement Powers

- Both HUD and the DOJ can initiate enforcement actions **without a formal complaint** being lodged.
- This proactive authority highlights the government's commitment to combating fair housing violations.

## Burden of Proof in Enforcement Actions

- In any public action brought by HUD or the DOJ, the **burden of proof** lies with the government.
- The government must provide substantial evidence to prove that a violation of fair housing statutes has occurred.
- This principle ensures a fair and just process for all parties involved.
