# Familial Status Protections Under the Fair Housing Act

<!-- Imagine you're engaging in a real estate transaction where a seller agrees to sell a property to buyers under one stringent condition: the buyers must **not have any children at the moment nor plan to have any in the future**. -->

Example: In a real estate transaction where a seller agrees to sell a property to buyers under one stringent condition: the buyers must **not have any children at the moment nor plan to have any in the future**. This scenario brings up a critical concept under the **Fair Housing Act**, specifically the protection against discrimination based on **familial status**.

## Violation of Familial Status Protections

- Imposing such a condition **directly violates** the familial status protections established by the Fair Housing Act.
- This makes the particular stipulation **null and void**.
- Despite this, the **rest of the contract remains enforceable**.

## Rights of Buyers with Children

- If it is discovered that the buyers do have children, they are fully within their rights to:
  - **Disregard the unlawful condition**
  - Proceed with enforcing the **primary terms of the contract**
