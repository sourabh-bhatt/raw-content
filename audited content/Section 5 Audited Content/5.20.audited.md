# 5.20 Property Descriptions in Deeds

**Deeds** require the element of **property description**. When handling a deed, the property must be legally described using one of three primary methods:

-   **Metes and Bounds**
-   **Public Land Survey System**
-   **Lot, Block, and Subdivision**

## Metes and Bounds Method

This method describes land by its boundaries. It uses distances, directions, and landmarks. It often starts at a specific point and follows the boundaries of the land back to the point of beginning. This method is particularly common in Virginia and other eastern states. It works well for properties that have irregular shapes.

## Public Land Survey System

Also known as the **rectangular survey system**, this method breaks down land into **townships** and **sections**, making it easier to identify parcels of land in a more structured manner. This system is not used in Virginia. It is typically used in the western United States and is ideal for describing large, undeveloped stretches of land.

## Lot, Block, and Subdivision Method

This technique is familiar in residential areas and involves defining property within a development where the land has been subdivided into blocks and lots. The legal description may reference a recorded **plat map**, which provides a visual representation of the subdivided land.

These methods describe property in deeds accurately. Each method has specific contexts and uses, adapting to the geographical features and human developments of the area. Accurately identifying and understanding these property descriptions ensures legal clarity and precision in transactions.
