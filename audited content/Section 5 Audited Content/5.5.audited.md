# 5.5 Sheriff's Deed in Real Estate Transactions

When a property is sold to satisfy a judgment, a **Sheriff's Deed** is issued. This deed resembles the deed in foreclosure. A court issues this document to a buyer when a property has been auctioned due to legal action.

## What is a Sheriff's Deed?

-   When a property is sold at a **public auction** to satisfy a judgment, the winning buyer receives a **Sheriff's Deed** (sometimes called a **referee's deed**).
-   This deed **eliminates any junior liens** against the property, giving the buyer a cleaner title.

## Impact on Junior Liens and Title

-   If **someone other than the delinquent borrower** (the person who failed to pay their debts) wins the property at auction, the **junior liens are wiped out**.
-   The buyer can obtain a **marketable title** for future transactions.

## Exception: When the Delinquent Borrower Buys Back the Property

-   If the **delinquent borrower ends up buying back their property** at the auction, the benefit of wiping out junior liens **does not apply**.
-   In this case, the **junior liens remain attached** to the property, affecting its title and potentially its marketability and value.

These nuances of a **Sheriff's Deed** and its effect on property title and liens affect real estate transactions. All parties need information about the status and potential risks related to the property.
