# 5.3 Special Warranty Deed in Real Estate Transactions

The **Special Warranty Deed** transfers ownership of property with a specific promise from the seller, known as the **grantor**, to the **buyer**.

## What is a Special Warranty Deed?

A Special Warranty Deed assures that during the time the grantor owned the property, they have not compromised the **title**. The deed covers only those issues that arose while the grantor owned it. Problems that occurred before their ownership are **not covered**.

These deeds often contain phrases like _"by, through, or under the grantor, but not otherwise"._ This legal language means the grantor guarantees the title only against problems that occurred under their ownership.

## Covenant Against the Grantor's Acts

The **covenant against the grantor's acts** represents a key element in a Special Warranty Deed. This provision applies when the person selling the property is in a position of trust or authority over it, such as:

-   An executor of an estate
-   A trustee
-   A guardian

In these situations, the person states, _"I swear I didn't do anything to harm the property's title while it was under my care."_ This covenant allows executors and trustees to promise they have not personally done anything wrong concerning the property.

This promise does **not** extend to:

-   Issues that existed before their involvement
-   Problems caused by the actions of others

## Special Warranty Deed for Buyers

The scope of the Special Warranty Deed helps manage risks and set expectations in real estate dealings. It defines what the seller is guaranteeing and determines the protections regarding the property's title.
