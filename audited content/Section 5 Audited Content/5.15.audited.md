# 5.15 Legal Methods for Describing Real Estate Property

Property transactions require accurate and legal descriptions of real estate. This involves more than the address; it requires a description detailed enough to be recognized and confirmed by a professional surveyor. Three primary methods describe a property legally.

## 1. Metes and Bounds System

-   One of the oldest methods of property description.
-   Describes boundaries based on physical features of the land, distances, and angles.
-   Involves drawing an invisible line around a property, noting every turn and boundary marker such as trees, stones, or other landmarks.
-   Precise but can be complex, especially in areas with many natural features.
-   Commonly used in Virginia for irregular parcels and rural properties.

## 2. Government Survey Method (Public Land Survey System - PLSS)

-   Divides land into rectangular plots using a grid of meridians (north-south lines) and base lines (east-west lines).
-   Plots are further divided into sections and townships.
-   Used in most states except the original 13 colonies and a few others.
-   Useful for large, undeveloped lands.
-   This system resembles using a giant checkerboard to map out land parcels.
-   Not used in Virginia since Virginia was surveyed before this system was created.

## 3. Lot, Block, and Subdivision Method

-   Frequently used in residential areas.
-   A developer divides a large area of land into smaller lots within a subdivision.
-   Properties are identified by their lot and block number within a specific subdivision.
-   These details are recorded on a **plat map** filed in the local land registry.
-   Straightforward and especially handy in neighborhoods and urban developments.
-   Most common method used in Virginia for residential properties.

## Reference to Other Publicly Recorded Documents

-   Sometimes land descriptions refer to another publicly recorded document, such as a previous deed or mortgage.
-   This is valid only if the referenced document includes a proper **legal description** of the property.
-   This method references a previous document to explain the current property description.

These methods ensure that properties are described accurately and legally, avoiding confusion and disputes over property lines and ownership. Understanding these methods enables clear, precise, and indisputable transactions.
