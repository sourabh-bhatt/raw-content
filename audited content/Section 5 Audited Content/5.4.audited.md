# 5.4 Deeds in Real Estate

A **deed** transfers the **title** of land from one person to another. The seller is the **grantor** who gives the deed. The buyer is the **grantee** who receives the deed. At closing, the deed replaces the original sales contract.

## Types of Deeds

Here are the four basic types of deeds:

-   **Warranty Deed**
-   **Special Warranty Deed**
-   **Bargain and Sale Deed**
-   **Quitclaim Deed**

Each type has different **covenants** or promises made by the seller. These covenants provide different levels of protection for the buyer.

## Legal Aspects of a Deed

A deed shows the **legal right** to own property. This document should clearly state any limitations, such as **easements** retained by the seller, **restrictions**, or **encumbrances** that might impact the title. For a deed to be legally enforceable and valid, it must:

-   Accurately describe the property
-   Be signed by the involved parties
-   Be properly delivered and accepted

## Ownership and Title Defects

However, having a deed does not guarantee clear ownership. **Title defects** can affect ownership rights even when someone has a deed. Buyers need to research the property's title carefully.
