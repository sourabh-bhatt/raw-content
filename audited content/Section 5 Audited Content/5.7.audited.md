# 5.7 Delivery and Acceptance of Deeds in Real Estate

**"Delivery and Acceptance"** represents a key concept for **deeds**. For a deed to transfer **property ownership** from one party to another, two main actions must occur:

-   The deed's **delivery** by the current owner, known as the **grantor**
-   Its voluntary **acceptance** by the new owner, or **grantee**

## Delivery and Acceptance

This process marks the moment when ownership officially changes hands. This involves more than physically handing over a document. The **intent** behind this exchange matters most. When both parties intend for the deed to convey ownership, the actual ownership transfers at the moment of that intended exchange.

-   If the deed is later lost or destroyed, the ownership status remains unaffected.
-   The physical deed serves as a **testament of ownership** rather than the determinant of ownership itself.

## Timing and Role of Third Parties

Typically, the delivery of a deed occurs at or after the **real estate closing**, the final step in a property sale when all paperwork is signed and the property officially changes hands. However, deeds can also be delivered to a third party, known as an **escrow agent**.

-   In such cases, the deed does not go directly to the grantee.
-   The escrow agent holds the deed and only passes it to the grantee once a specific condition or event has been fulfilled.

## Delivery and Acceptance Summary

The nuances of delivery and acceptance affect real estate transactions. This process demonstrates the role of the deed as a document and highlights the **intentions** and **actions** of those involved in its transfer.
