# 5.29 Special Warranty Deed in Property Transactions

Situations like **tax deeds**, where property is conveyed due to non-payment of taxes, or instances where a **trustee**, such as in a **sheriff's sale**, distributes property to satisfy a judgment, likely involve a **Special Warranty Deed**.

## What is a Special Warranty Deed?

What makes the Special Warranty Deed unique is the extent of the warranty provided by the grantor, often a trustee in these cases.

-   The person conveying the property, known as the **grantor**, guarantees the title only against issues or claims that might have arisen from *their own actions* during their period of ownership.
-   This guarantee **does not cover** any problems caused by previous owners.

Essentially, the warranty provided by a Special Warranty Deed does **not extend beyond the grantor's tenure** with the property.

## Special Warranty Deed Applications

The nuances of a Special Warranty Deed help navigate and explain the risks and guarantees involved in these types of property transactions. This knowledge helps in:

-   Setting expectations for buyers and sellers.
-   Preparing for potential title issues that were not within the grantor's control.

Recognizing the limits of the warranty enables real estate professionals to advise clients on the risks associated with Special Warranty Deeds.
