# 5.11 Warranty Deeds and Covenants of Title

**Warranty deeds** involve the **seller**, or **grantor**, making explicit promises about the **property's title**. This includes affirmations that there are no **encumbrances**, such as **liens** or outstanding **mortgages**. A warranty deed provides the **buyer** or **grantee** with the highest level of protection. This deed type is commonly preferred for transferring **real estate ownership** due to this assurance.

## The Role of Covenants in Warranty Deeds

The security offered by warranty deeds hinges on **covenants**, which are essentially guarantees made by the seller. These guarantees can be either:

-   **Expressed** (stated outright in the deed)
-   **Implied** (assumed based on the deed's wording)

Covenants are not unique to deeds; they also appear in various real estate documents such as:

-   Leases
-   Mortgages
-   Contracts for deed

Their main purpose is to provide peace of mind by ensuring certain conditions are met.

## Recognizing Covenants of Title

Warranty deeds require determining whether the **covenants of title** are explicitly mentioned or implied. Some deeds clearly state covenants directly within the document. Others imply these assurances using specific language such as:

-   "**convey and warrant**"
-   "**warrant generally**"
-   "**warrant specially**"

Recognizing these terms indicates the level of security promised regarding the property's title. This knowledge enables understanding of property transaction nuances.
