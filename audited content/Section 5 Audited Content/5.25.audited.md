# 5.25 Covenant Against Encumbrances in Property Deeds

**Deeds** include the **covenant against encumbrances**. This is a promise made by the **seller (or grantor)** of the property. Through this covenant, the grantor assures that the property does not bear any hidden **encumbrances** or liabilities, other than those explicitly disclosed in the deed itself.

## Encumbrances

**Encumbrances** can be anything from:

-   **Liens**
-   Outstanding loans
-   Legal claims against the property

These can affect the property's use or transferability. To ensure transparency, any and all encumbrances are listed immediately after the description of the property within the deed. This helps real estate professionals understand the property and prepare for any potential hurdles in the property transaction process.

## Visible vs. Hidden Encumbrances

-   The **covenant against encumbrances** is *not* considered broken if an encumbrance is openly and visibly apparent, such as:
    -   A power line running across the property
    -   A visible drainage ditch

    In these cases, the seller is not held liable for failing to list such encumbrances in the deed.

-   Conversely, if there is an encumbrance that is *not* openly visible and it was not mentioned in the deed, the **grantor could be held responsible** for that oversight.

## Due Diligence

This distinction requires thorough **inspections** and **due diligence**. Ensuring that all encumbrances, whether visible or hidden, are accounted for and properly disclosed protects clients' interests and helps ensure smooth property transactions.
