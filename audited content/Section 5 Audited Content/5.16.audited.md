# 5.16 The Habendum Clause in Deeds

The **Habendum Clause**, commonly referred to as the *"to have and to hold clause,"* comes right after the **premises (granting) clause** in a deed.

## Purpose of the Habendum Clause

The main purpose of the Habendum Clause is to clarify the type of **estate** or **interest** being transferred through the deed. This could be:

-   A **fee simple estate**, which is the most complete form of ownership.
-   A **life estate**, which lasts for the lifetime of a specific individual.

## Relationship Between Habendum and Premises Clauses

A key point:

-   If there is ever a conflict between the **habendum clause** and the **premises clause** regarding the extent of ownership being transferred, the **premises clause** (also called the **granting clause**) takes precedence.

This concept affects how deeds communicate the specific rights being conveyed from the **grantor** to the **grantee**. Understanding this ensures that the rights and interests intended to be transferred are accurately reflected in the deed's language for real estate transactions.
