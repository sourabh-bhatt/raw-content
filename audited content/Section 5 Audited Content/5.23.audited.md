# 5.23 Deeds: Roles and Legal Requirements

**Deeds** involve the roles of the **grantor** (also known as the seller) and the **grantee** (known as the buyer). Both parties must be correctly identified and possess the **legal capacity** to engage in the transaction. Both individuals must have the legal authority and mental capability to understand the implications of the transaction.

## Signing the Deed

-   It is **mandatory for the grantor to sign the deed** to signify their agreement and the transfer of property.
-   The **grantee is not required to sign the deed**.
-   However, the deed must clearly state the **grantee's identity** to be valid and effective.

## Formalizing the Deed: State Variations

The process of formalizing the deed varies from state to state:

-   Some states require the deed's signing to be **witnessed by two people**. Other states require **acknowledgment**.
-   Other states require the deed to be **notarized**. This means a **notary public** confirms the identity of the person signing.

### Notarization

-   Notarization helps ensure that the record of the deed is **accurate and legally binding**.

These nuances help ensure that the **transfer of property** adheres to legal standards, making the transaction smooth and legally sound.
