# Foreclosure and Its Impact on Leases

<!-- When a property is subjected to **foreclosure**, which is an **involuntary transfer**, it is essential to understand the legal implications regarding any existing leases. -->
When a property is subjected to **foreclosure**, which is an **involuntary transfer**, the legal implications regarding any existing leases must be understood. In Virginia, foreclosure does not automatically terminate leases that were recorded before the deed of trust.

## Legal Principles

- If a property goes into foreclosure, leases recorded before the deed of trust generally survive foreclosure, while unrecorded leases may be terminated.
- Virginia follows the "first in time, first in right" principle, meaning tenants with recorded leases that predate the deed of trust typically retain their lease rights.

- The impact of foreclosure on leases affects client advisory practices.

- This knowledge helps in managing properties during and after foreclosure proceedings.

Example: If you are representing a landlord whose property is foreclosed, you must inform them that unrecorded leases or leases created after the deed of trust may no longer be valid, and those tenants might be required to vacate the premises.
