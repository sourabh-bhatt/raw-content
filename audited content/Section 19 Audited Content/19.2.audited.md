# Understanding Prorations in Property Closings

<!-- When closing a property sale, it's crucial to understand how to adjust the costs that are shared by the buyer and seller. Beyond the fees and expenses you'll encounter at settlement, you'll often need to address the distribution of **prepaid costs**. These might include: -->

When closing a property sale, buyers and sellers must adjust shared costs. Beyond fees and expenses at settlement, parties need to address the distribution of **prepaid costs**. These might include:

- **Property taxes**
- **Association dues**
- **Utilities**
- Other similar expenses

This process of balancing these payments is typically known as **"prorations"**.

## What Are Prorations?

<!-- Prorations ensure that the seller pays for their portion of the costs up until the property changes hands, and the buyer assumes responsibility from that point forward. For example, if the seller has already paid property taxes for the full year and you buy the property mid-year, you'll need to reimburse the seller for the months you will own the property. -->

Prorations ensure that sellers pay for their portion of costs up until property changes hands, and buyers assume responsibility from that point forward. 

Example: If sellers have already paid property taxes for the full year and buyers purchase the property mid-year, buyers need to reimburse sellers for the months they will own the property.

## Prorations in the Closing Disclosure

These financial adjustments or prorations are meticulously detailed in the **Closing Disclosure's Summaries of Transactions** section. This section provides a clear, itemized list of who owes what to whom, ensuring both parties handle their fair share of the costs based on the exact period each has ownership over the property.
