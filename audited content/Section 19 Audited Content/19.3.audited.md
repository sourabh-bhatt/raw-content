# Prorations in Real Estate Transactions

**Valuation calculations** occur during the **closing** or **settlement** of real estate transactions. These calculations focus on the subdivision known as **prorations**.

## Understanding Prorations

**Prorations** revolve around the equitable division of certain costs between the property buyer and seller upon closing. These can include a variety of expenses such as:

- Dues for the **homeowner's association**
- **Utility bills**
- **Property taxes**
- **Rental income**, if applicable

<!-- Moreover, the buyer's monthly **interest on the mortgage** is prorated to ensure they're only responsible for interest charges from the settlement day forward, not before. -->

The buyer's monthly **interest on the mortgage** is prorated to ensure responsibility for interest charges from the settlement day forward, not before.

## Proration Example: County Taxes

<!-- To make this more tangible, let's run through a proration example: -->

Example: A seller has prepaid the annual county taxes amounting to $720. If the closing is scheduled for October 10, how much should the seller be reimbursed for the county taxes at the time of closing?

### Step-by-Step Calculation

1. **Determine the daily tax rate:**
   - Divide the annual county tax by 360 days:
   
   
   $720 Ã· 360 days = $2 per day
   

2. **Calculate the number of days the buyer will own the property post-closing:**
   - 20 days in October (from October 11 to October 30)
   - 30 days in November
   - 30 days in December
   
   **Total days = 20 + 30 + 30 = 80 days**

3. **Calculate the reimbursement amount:**
   
   
   $2 per day Ã— 80 days = $160
   

### Interpretation

Therefore, at closing, the seller should be reimbursed a sum of **$160** for county taxes, effectively transferring the responsibility for these taxes to the buyer for the period they will own the property.
