# Closing on a Property: In-Person vs. Escrow Closings

<!-- When it comes to closing on a property, the process can unfold in different ways depending on whether it involves the physical presence of both the buyer and seller or is conducted through an escrow. Understanding how each closing format works is critical for navigating your transactions smoothly. -->

Property closings can unfold in different ways depending on whether they involve the physical presence of both the buyer and seller or are conducted through an escrow. Each closing format works differently in property transactions.

## Closing Day Scenarios

- Both **buyer and seller** may be present along with their respective agents.
- Alternatively, the transaction might be handled by a **neutral third party** in an **escrow closing**.

No matter the method, the outcome should be the same: the **title** is handed over to the buyer or to a neutral third party in return for the agreed-upon **purchase price**. The attendance of the buyer and seller directly at the closing can vary depending on the specific arrangements made.

## In-Person Closings

In an **in-person closing**:

- Any lingering issues can be resolved immediately.
- All documents are signed on the spot.
- The **seller receives payment**.
- The **buyer takes possession** of both the **title** and **keys** to the new property.

This is a straightforward transaction where everything is settled during the closing meeting.

## Escrow Closings

In an **escrow closing**:

- Neither buyer nor seller needs to be physically present.
- A **neutral third party**, called an **escrow agent**, manages the process according to instructions from both parties.
- The escrow agent is authorized to hold property or complete specific tasks for closing.

### Responsibilities of the Parties and Escrow Agent

- Both parties must submit all necessary **documents** and **funds** to the escrow agent.
- The escrow agent confirms that:
  - The **seller** has met all obligations (e.g., providing proof of a **marketable title**).
  - The **buyer** has made the required funds available.
- Once confirmed, the escrow agent:
  - Transfers the **title** to the buyer.
  - Releases the **funds** to the seller.

If the escrow agent finds that either party has not met their obligations, the original terms of the escrow agreement usually require both parties to revert to their initial positions, as if the sale never occurred.
