# Escrow Closing in Real Estate Transactions

In real estate transactions, an **escrow closing** involves an arrangement where the **seller** (also called the **grantor**) hands over the **property deed** to a neutral third party, referred to as the **escrow agent**. The escrow agent transfers the deed to the **buyer** (or **grantee**) once all previously agreed-upon conditions have been met. If these conditions are not fulfilled, the deed is returned to the seller.

## Role and Responsibilities of the Escrow Agent

- The escrow agent must act **impartially**, performing their duties without favoring either party.
- They follow the instructions provided to them carefully.
- This impartial obligation is part of their **fiduciary duties**, ensuring the agent acts in the best interest of both the seller and buyer.

## Effect of Seller's Death on Escrow

<!-- An important aspect of escrow involves what happens if the seller dies after depositing the deed into escrow but before the deed is transferred to the buyer: -->

If the seller dies after depositing the deed into escrow but before the deed is transferred to the buyer:

- The escrow agent is still required to deliver the deed to the buyer as originally agreed.
- This requirement is supported by the **relation-back doctrine**, which states that the transfer of title is considered effective from the moment the deed was placed into escrow, not when it is physically handed over.

This doctrine ensures that the intentions of all parties are honored despite unforeseen circumstances such as the seller's death.
