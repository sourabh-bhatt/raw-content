# Federal Disclosure Responsibilities in Real Estate Transactions

Real estate transactions require understanding responsibilities for **federal disclosures**. There is no single federal form for property disclaimers or disclosures. Even without uniform federal requirements, certain federal laws require specific duties when selling or leasing properties. This is especially true for properties built before 1978 due to concerns about **lead-based paint**.

## Lead-Based Paint Hazard Reduction Act (Lead Act)

<!-- You must know about the **Lead-Based Paint Hazard Reduction Act** (also called the **Lead Act**). This law requires two things when selling or leasing properties built before 1978: -->

The **Lead-Based Paint Hazard Reduction Act** (also called the **Lead Act**) requires two things when selling or leasing properties built before 1978:

1. **Knowledge Disclosure**
   - Sellers must disclose any knowledge about lead-based paint on the property.
   - This ensures that potential buyers or tenants know about potential lead hazards before making a decision.

2. **Providing a Pamphlet and Allowing Inspection Time**
   - Sellers must provide prospective buyers or tenants with a specific pamphlet that educates them about lead paint hazards.
   - Sellers must also give buyers or tenants at least **ten (10) days** to conduct their inspection of the property for lead-based paint.

Real estate professionals must follow these rules to work ethically and legally. These measures:

- Protect professionals from legal problems.
- Safeguard the health and well-being of buyers and tenants.
- Ensure buyers and tenants know about the risks of lead-based paint.

Following these federal disclosure requirements helps create safer real estate transactions and promotes transparency in the market.
