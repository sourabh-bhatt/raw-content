The sale of **residential properties** (ranging from 1-4 units) falls under the **Virginia Residential Property Disclosure Act (RPDA)**. This act sets specific **disclosure obligations** for sellers and agents who represent these sellers. Sellers must disclose information that could affect a buyer's decision. The **Residential Property Disclosure Statement** is available on the **Virginia Real Estate Board website**.

## Scope of the Act

The act covers many types of property transactions, including:

- Sales
- Options
- Installment sales
- Leases that might lead to eventual ownership

However, certain transactions are **exempt** from the act, such as:

- Court-ordered transactions (e.g., foreclosures, bankruptcy settlements)
- Transactions among co-owners or relatives
- Transactions resulting from divorce settlements

## Seller Requirements Under the Act

Sellers are required to provide a **Disclosure Form** to potential buyers. Key points include:

- The form discloses essential information. It also emphasizes that buyers must perform their own **due diligence**.
- The disclosure statement covers known material defects and conditions that could affect the property's value or desirability.
- Sellers must disclose what they actually know about the property's condition.
