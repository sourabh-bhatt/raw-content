# Understanding Flood Risk and Insurance in Virginia Real Estate

Real estate professionals working with properties on the market must understand **flood risk information**.

The **Flood Risk Information Form**, available on the Real Estate Board website, can be provided to potential buyers if a property is located in a **Special Flood Hazard Area (SFHA)**. This form provides current and prospective property owners with details about flood risk. This form itself does *not* dictate the necessity for a flood insurance policy. Instead, the **lender** financing the property purchase typically decides whether to require flood insurance.

## Flood Insurance Requirements

Flood insurance requirements are governed by federal regulations, including the **Flood Disaster Protection Act of 1973** and the **National Flood Insurance Reform Act of 1994**. These laws obligate mortgage lenders to require flood insurance if the property is located within an SFHA.

- **Special Flood Hazard Area (SFHA):** An area identified as high risk for flooding, defined as land that would experience flooding from a baseline flood event (a flood with a 1% chance of occurring in any given year).

- **Lender's Role:** Mortgage lenders must mandate flood insurance for properties in SFHAs.

## Flood Risk Beyond SFHAs

Properties outside SFHAs are not immune to flooding. Key points include:

- Flood damage is typically *not* covered under standard homeowner insurance policies.
<!-- - Even if flood insurance is not required by a lender, purchasing it is a wise precaution. -->
- Even if flood insurance is not required by a lender, purchasing it provides protection.
- Properties outside SFHAs may qualify for a more affordable **Preferred Risk Policy**.

## Staying Updated on Flood Zones

Flood zone designations can change over time. Important resources to stay current include:

- **FEMA Flood Maps:** Updated every five years.
- **Virginia Department of Conservation and Recreation's Flood Risk Information System:** Provides localized flood risk data.

These resources help verify the latest flood zone status for any specific property.

## Repetitive Risk Loss Disclosure

Homeowners in Virginia have a responsibility to disclose certain flood risk information:

- A **repetitive risk loss** occurs when a residential property has had two or more claims exceeding $1,000 through the **National Flood Insurance Program** within any rolling 10-year period since 1978.
- Owners of such properties must disclose this information to potential buyers using a disclosure form provided by the Real Estate Board.
