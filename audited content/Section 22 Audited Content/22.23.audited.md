# Prohibited Fees and Referral Practices under RESPA

<!-- When you're navigating through the process of real estate transactions, it's crucial to understand the regulations surrounding **prohibited fees**. Real estate transactions require understanding regulations surrounding **prohibited fees**. Under the **Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA)**, professionals cannot pay or receive any fee, kickback, or anything of value in exchange for referring settlement service business to a specific person or organization. This law ensures fairness and transparency in real estate transactions.

## Examples of Prohibited Practices

<!-- - Imagine if you, as a real estate professional, were offered $250 by a mortgage lender just for referring a client to them. According to RESPA, this would be illegal.

- Example: If a real estate professional were offered $250 by a mortgage lender for referring a client to them, this would be illegal according to RESPA.
- Increasing a third-party's fee, such as for an appraisal, and then keeping the difference is also prohibited.

These practices are not allowed because they can lead to biased decisions and compromise the integrity of the real estate transaction process.

## Fees and Services Requirements

- It is against the law to accept a fee or part of a fee for services when **no actual settlement services have been performed**.
- Every fee charged must be tied to a **genuine service** that has been provided.

However, RESPA permits compensation for professionals such as:

- **Title companies**
- **Mortgage brokers**
- **Appraisers**
- **Attorneys**
- **Settlement/closing agents**

These professionals may be compensated for the reasonable value of their work, provided they are actually performing services in connection with the mortgage loan or the settlement.

## Addressing Violations

- If someone involved in the settlement process receives a fee without having contributed any work, they may be violating RESPA's rules against referral fees.
