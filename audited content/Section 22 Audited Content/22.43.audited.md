# Understanding Non-Conforming Uses in Zoning Laws

<!-- When learning about regulatory statutes, it's essential to understand the concept of **non-conforming uses**. Regulatory statutes include the concept of **non-conforming uses**. A non-conforming use represents a property use that doesn't match the current **zoning laws** of an area. **Legal non-conforming use** and **illegal non-conforming use** have different implications.

## Legal Non-Conforming Use

A legal non-conforming use occurs when a property's use was in line with zoning ordinances at one time but, due to a change in those zoning laws, it now falls outside of those rules. This scenario is often referred to as **"grandfathering."** Under these circumstances, the property is allowed to continue its current use despite the change in zoning laws. This exception acknowledges that the property was compliant before the law changed.

<!-- - If the property faces significant damage or destruction and you wish to rebuild, you might need a special approval known as a **variance**. -->

- If the property faces damage or destruction and rebuilding is desired, special approval known as a **variance** might be needed.
- A variance grants permission to deviate from the current zoning requirements.

## Illegal Non-Conforming Use

An illegal non-conforming use applies to properties that started being used in a way that contradicts zoning laws after those laws were already in place. Unlike legal non-conforming uses, these have no protection and cannot continue.

- Such properties are required to adhere to current zoning statutes.
- Adjustments must be made to comply with zoning laws.
