# Compliance with Laws Governing Unsolicited Fax and Text Message Communications

Real estate professionals must understand and comply with specific laws that govern unsolicited communications such as **faxes** and **text messages**. This understanding helps avoid legal pitfalls and ensures marketing efforts are both respectful and effective.

## Key Laws to Know

The primary regulations to be aware of are the **Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA)** and the **Junk Fax Prevention Act**. These laws are designed to protect consumers from unwelcome advertisements.

## Fax Advertisements

- Professionals **cannot send unsolicited fax advertisements** to any fax machine without prior permission.

- Sending faxes is allowed with **explicit consent** or a **pre-existing business relationship** with the recipient.
<!-- - Required information on fax advertisements includes your **company's name**, **phone number**, **date**, and **time sent**. -->
- Required information on fax advertisements includes the **company's name**, **phone number**, **date**, and **time sent**.
- Unsolicited faxes can only be sent if the recipient's fax number was obtained **directly from them** or through a **public source** (e.g., website or advertisement).
- **Purchasing client lists** for fax numbers is strictly prohibited.

## Text Message Advertisements

- The TCPA **prohibits sending automated text messages** to mobile phones or devices without **prior written consent**, except for emergency purposes.
- A **one-time message** is allowed following a direct request from a consumer, but it must contain **only the requested information**â€”no additional marketing content.

## Opt-Out Requirements

- Both fax and text message advertisements must include a **clear opt-out mechanism**.

### For Faxes:
- The opt-out notice must be **visible on the first page**.
- It should provide a **toll-free number or fax number** where recipients can request to stop receiving communications.
- Ignoring opt-out requests is **illegal**.

### For Text Messages:
- Include an **opt-out mechanism within the message**.
- Consumers must be able to **revoke consent by any reasonable means**, such as calling, texting, or in person.

## Consequences of Non-Compliance

- Recipients of unauthorized communications can file complaints with the **FCC** or sue for damages in **federal court**.
- They may seek compensation for **actual monetary losses** or **statutory damages** up to **$1,500 per violation**.
- Damages can be **tripled (up to $4,500 per violation) for willful or knowing violations**.

## Best Practices

- Always obtain **explicit consent** before sending marketing communications.
- Respect and promptly act on **opt-out requests**.
- Ensure all communications comply with the **TCPA** and **Junk Fax Prevention Act**.
