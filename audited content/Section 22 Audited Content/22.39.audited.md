# Virginia Condominium Act: Understanding Condominium Development, Sales, and Management in Virginia

<!-- When working with condominiums, you must understand the **Virginia Condominium Act**. This law guides how condominiums are created and transferred in Virginia. -->

Working with condominiums requires understanding the **Virginia Condominium Act**. This law guides how condominiums are created and transferred in Virginia.

## Scope of the Virginia Condominium Act

The Act regulates how **condominiums** and **horizontal property regimes** come to life in the state. A key takeaway is viewing a condominium not just as a structure, but as **real property** that grants owners an **undivided interest in common areas**.

## Development of Condominiums

<!-- If you're involved in developing a condominium, there are specific steps to follow: -->

Developing a condominium involves specific steps:

- The person or entity in charge of development, known as the **declarant**, must:
  - Register the project with the **Virginia Real Estate Board**.
  - Ensure that the **condominium instruments** are recorded at the local level.

### Condominium Instruments to Record

<!-- These crucial documents include: -->

These documents include:

- **Declaration**: Details everything from the development's name to construction specifics and easements.
- **Plats and plans**: Maps and architectural drawings of the property.
- **By-laws**: Rules governing operations, including meeting schedules and community conduct.

## Conversion of Existing Structures

When converting existing buildings into condominiums, the same principles apply with an added requirement:

- **Tenant Notification**: Existing tenants must be informed about:
  - Potential assessments.
  - Measures to prevent displacement.
  - Their right to purchase their units before the public.

## Selling Condominium Units

### Ownership and Initial Sales

- Buyers obtain **fee simple ownership** of their unit plus a share in the common areas.
- For initial sales by the declarant, a **public offering statement** is mandatory. This statement provides a comprehensive overview of the project, including:
  - Construction status.
  - Amenities offered.

### Marketing Regulations

Marketing these units must comply with rules to avoid legal issues:

- No false promises.
- No suggesting unregistered improvements.
- Adherence to **fair housing standards**.

## Resale of Condominium Units

The Act provides protections for buyers during resales:

- Sellers must provide a **resale certificate** disclosing:
  - Financial health of the condominium association.
  - Legal standing and other relevant details.
- Buyers have a **right of rescission**, allowing them to back out under certain conditions.

## Role of the Condominium Association

- Initially managed by the **declarant**, control gradually shifts to **unit owners** as sales progress.
- This transition affects:
  - **Voting rights**.
  - Decision-making on bylaw changes.
  - Handling of unpaid assessments, which may lead to **liens**.

## Termination of Condominium Ownership

- A **supermajority** of unit owners can vote to terminate the condominium and change the property's ownership model.
