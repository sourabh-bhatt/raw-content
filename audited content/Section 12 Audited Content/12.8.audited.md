# Understanding Real Estate Owned (REO) Properties

<!-- When you're diving into the realm of real estate investment, one term you might encounter is **Real Estate Owned (REO)**. Let's break down how a property ends up being classified as REO, providing you insight that can be invaluable in your journey. -->
**Real Estate Owned (REO)** represents a specific classification in real estate investment. The following explains how a property becomes classified as REO.

## How a Property Becomes REO

- **Short Sale or Foreclosure Auction:** These processes are initiated by lenders aiming to recover as much as possible of their invested funds.
- **When Sale Attempts Fail:** If these attempts don't lead to a sale, often because the outstanding debt on a property exceeds its current market value, the property becomes less attractive to buyers at auction.
- **Lender Takes Possession:** In cases where a foreclosure auction doesn't result in a sale, the lenderâ€”typically a bank or financial institutionâ€”takes possession of the property. This transition marks the creation of an REO property.

## What Happens After the Property Becomes REO

- The lender, now the new owner, attempts to sell the property directly on the market.
- Their goal remains to recover their investment.
- However, they are dealing with a property that has already proven challenging to sell.
